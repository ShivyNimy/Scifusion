Mechanism of Hormone Action

Hormones act on target cells through receptors. Water-soluble hormones use surface receptors; lipid-soluble hormones enter cells.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Protein hormones bind surface receptors and trigger second messengers such as cAMP.
  • Steroid hormones enter cells and bind intracellular receptors, altering gene expression.
  • Hormone levels are regulated by feedback loops.
  • The hypothalamus links the nervous and endocrine systems.
  • Hormonal imbalance causes diseases such as diabetes and hypothyroidism.

Worked examples

Example 1 Negative feedback

High thyroxine levels inhibit TSH release from the pituitary, keeping thyroid activity in check.

Example 2 Steroid hormone action

Cortisol enters cells and binds receptors that act as transcription factors, changing gene expression slowly but durably.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Steroid hormones act by:
    A. Binding intracellular receptorsB. Binding surface receptors onlyC. Entering the blood directlyD. Acting as enzymes
    Show answer

    Answer: Binding intracellular receptors

    They enter cells and affect gene expression.

  2. MCQ A negative feedback loop:
    A. Reduces the stimulus that started itB. Increases hormone secretion indefinitelyC. Ignores hormone levelsD. Stops the gland permanently
    Show answer

    Answer: Reduces the stimulus that started it

    The response counteracts the initial stimulus.

  3. Short answer Why does hormone action take longer than nerve action?
    Show answer

    Hormones travel in blood, bind receptors and often alter gene expression, which takes time.
