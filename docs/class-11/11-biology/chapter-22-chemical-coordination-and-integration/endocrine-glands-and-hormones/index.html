Endocrine Glands and Hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands into the blood, regulating growth, metabolism and reproduction.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The pituitary gland is the master gland, controlling other endocrine glands.
  • The thyroid gland produces thyroxine, regulating metabolism.
  • The pancreas secretes insulin and glucagon to control blood glucose.
  • The adrenal glands produce adrenaline and corticosteroids.
  • The gonads produce sex hormones.

Worked examples

Example 1 Goitre

Iodine deficiency reduces thyroxine production; the thyroid enlarges as it tries to compensate.

Example 2 Insulin and glucose

After a meal, insulin helps cells absorb glucose; in diabetes this fails, so blood glucose remains high.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The master endocrine gland is the:
    A. PituitaryB. ThyroidC. AdrenalD. Pancreas
    Show answer

    Answer: Pituitary

    The pituitary controls other endocrine glands.

  2. MCQ Insulin is secreted by the:
    A. PancreasB. ThyroidC. AdrenalD. Pituitary
    Show answer

    Answer: Pancreas

    Beta cells of the pancreas secrete insulin.

  3. Short answer Why is iodine necessary in the diet?
    Show answer

    It is needed to synthesise thyroid hormones; deficiency causes goitre and hypothyroidism.
