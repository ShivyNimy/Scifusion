Angiosperms and Plant Life Cycles

Angiosperms are flowering plants with enclosed seeds. Their life cycle alternates between sporophyte and gametophyte generations.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Angiosperms are divided into dicots and monocots.
  • Flowers may be unisexual or bisexual; pollination precedes fertilisation.
  • Double fertilisation produces a zygote and a triploid endosperm in angiosperms.
  • Alternation of generations: diploid sporophyte and haploid gametophyte.
  • Fruits protect seeds and aid dispersal.

Worked examples

Example 1 Double fertilisation

One male gamete fuses with the egg to form the zygote, while another fuses with two polar nuclei to form the triploid endosperm — unique to angiosperms.

Example 2 Monocot versus dicot

Monocots have parallel veins and one cotyledon; dicots have net veins and two cotyledons.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Endosperm in angiosperms is:
    A. TriploidB. DiploidC. HaploidD. Absent
    Show answer

    Answer: Triploid

    Double fertilisation produces triploid endosperm.

  2. MCQ Which group dominates modern vegetation?
    A. AngiospermsB. GymnospermsC. PteridophytesD. Bryophytes
    Show answer

    Answer: Angiosperms

    Flowering plants are the most diverse and widespread.

  3. Short answer What is alternation of generations?
    Show answer

    The life cycle alternates between a haploid gametophyte and a diploid sporophyte generation.
