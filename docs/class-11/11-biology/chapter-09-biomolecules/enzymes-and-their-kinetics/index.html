Enzymes and Their Kinetics

Enzymes lower activation energy and are specific. Their activity depends on pH, temperature and concentration.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Enzymes are biological catalysts, mostly proteins.
  • They lower activation energy by stabilising transition states.
  • Optimum temperature and pH maximise enzyme activity.
  • Competitive inhibitors bind the active site; non-competitive inhibitors bind elsewhere.
  • Michaelis-Menten kinetics describes initial rates; Km indicates substrate affinity.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why fever makes you feel weak

High temperature denatures enzymes and disturbs metabolism, which is why fever is dangerous.

Example 2 Enzyme in the stomach

Pepsin works best at acidic pH, while intestinal enzymes prefer alkaline conditions — the environment matches the organ.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Enzymes increase the rate of reaction by:
    A. Lowering activation energyB. Raising temperatureC. Changing productsD. Adding energy
    Show answer

    Answer: Lowering activation energy

    Enzymes lower the activation energy barrier.

  2. MCQ A competitive inhibitor binds to the:
    A. Active siteB. Allosteric siteC. ProductD. Substrate
    Show answer

    Answer: Active site

    It competes with the substrate for the active site.

  3. Short answer Why is enzyme activity reduced at very high temperature?
    Show answer

    The enzyme's tertiary structure denatures, so the active site loses its shape and function.
