Anaerobic Respiration and Amphibolic Pathway

Fermentation allows energy release without oxygen, but yields much less ATP. The respiratory pathway is amphibolic.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • In yeast, pyruvate is converted to ethanol and CO2.
  • In muscles, pyruvate becomes lactic acid during vigorous exercise.
  • Fermentation yields only 2 ATP per glucose.
  • The respiratory pathway provides intermediates for other biosynthetic reactions (amphibolic).
  • Respiration is controlled by the energy needs of the cell.

Worked examples

Example 1 Muscle cramp

Lactic acid from anaerobic respiration accumulates during intense exercise, causing cramps.

Example 2 Bread rising

Yeast ferments sugars and releases CO2 bubbles, which make dough rise.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Anaerobic respiration in muscles produces:
    A. Lactic acidB. EthanolC. CO2 onlyD. Pyruvate
    Show answer

    Answer: Lactic acid

    Lactic acid forms when oxygen is limited.

  2. MCQ Fermentation yields how many ATP per glucose?
    A. 2B. 36C. 30D. 38
    Show answer

    Answer: 2

    Fermentation yields only 2 ATP.

  3. Short answer Why is the respiratory pathway called amphibolic?
    Show answer

    It both breaks down (catabolic) and supplies intermediates for synthesis (anabolic) of molecules.
