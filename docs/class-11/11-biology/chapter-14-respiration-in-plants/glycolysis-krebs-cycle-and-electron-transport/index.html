Glycolysis, Krebs Cycle and Electron Transport

Aerobic respiration releases energy from glucose in three stages: glycolysis, Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and yields 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose.
  • Pyruvate enters mitochondria and is converted to acetyl CoA.
  • Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix and yields CO2, ATP, NADH and FADH2.
  • The electron transport chain and ATP synthase produce most ATP.
  • The overall aerobic yield is about 30–38 ATP per glucose depending on shuttle efficiency.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why aerobic respiration yields more ATP

Complete oxidation transfers more electrons to the electron transport chain, producing far more ATP than fermentation.

Example 2 Respiratory quotient

RQ = CO2 released ÷ O2 consumed; it is 1 for carbohydrates and less than 1 for fats.

  2. MCQ Most ATP is produced by:
    A. Oxidative phosphorylationB. GlycolysisC. Krebs cycleD. Fermentation

    Answer: Oxidative phosphorylation

    The electron transport chain produces most ATP.

  3. Short answer Write the overall equation for aerobic respiration.
