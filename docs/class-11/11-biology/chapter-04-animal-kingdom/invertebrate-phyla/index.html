Invertebrate Phyla

Animals are grouped by body symmetry, body cavity and development. Invertebrates include sponges through echinoderms.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Porifera (sponges): cellular level, water canal system.
  • Cnidaria: radial symmetry with stinging cells (Hydra, jellyfish).
  • Platyhelminthes: flatworms; Aschelminthes: roundworms.
  • Annelida: segmented worms (earthworm); Arthropoda: jointed legs and exoskeleton (insects).
  • Mollusca: soft bodies with shells; Echinodermata: spiny skinned with water-vascular system.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why arthropods are so successful

Jointed appendages, a protective exoskeleton and metamorphosis allow insects to exploit almost every habitat.

Example 2 Earthworm and soil

Segmented bodies and setae let earthworms burrow, mixing and aerating the soil.

    Answer: Arthropoda

    Arthropods have jointed appendages and exoskeletons.

  2. MCQ Sponges belong to the phylum:
    A. PoriferaB. CnidariaC. MolluscaD. Annelida
    Show answer

    Answer: Porifera

    Porifera are sponges.

  3. Short answer Name three features of arthropods.
    Show answer

    Jointed legs, exoskeleton and segmented body (also compound eyes).
