Synapses, Brain and Reflexes

Signals cross synapses chemically. The brain and spinal cord coordinate complex and reflex actions.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Synaptic transmission uses neurotransmitters released by calcium-triggered exocytosis.
  • Excitatory synapses depolarise the postsynaptic cell; inhibitory ones hyperpolarise it.
  • The cerebral cortex handles higher functions; the cerebellum coordinates movement.
  • The medulla controls breathing and heartbeat.
  • Reflex arcs provide rapid responses to stimuli.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reflex arc

A hammer tap on the knee stretches a tendon, triggering a sensory neuron that synapses in the spinal cord and excites a motor neuron — the knee jerks.

Example 2 Neurotransmitter balance

Dopamine and serotonin influence mood and movement; imbalances are linked to Parkinson's disease and depression.

  2. MCQ Which brain region controls balance?

    Answer: Cerebellum

    The cerebellum coordinates posture and balance.

  3. Short answer What is a reflex arc?

    The neural pathway that mediates a reflex action, from receptor to effector.
