Neurons and Nerve Impulses

Neurons transmit electrical impulses. The resting potential and action potential depend on ion movement across the membrane.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A neuron has dendrites, a cell body and an axon.
  • The resting membrane potential is about −70 mV.
  • Depolarisation opens sodium channels, generating an action potential.
  • Repolarisation restores the potential by potassium movement.
  • Impulses jump between nodes of Ranvier in myelinated axons, speeding conduction.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why myelinated axons conduct faster

Myelin insulates the axon except at nodes, so the impulse jumps from node to node — saltatory conduction.

Example 2 Local anaesthetics

They block sodium channels, preventing action potentials and therefore pain signals.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The resting potential of a neuron is about:
    A. −70 mVB. +70 mVC. 0 mVD. −140 mV
    Show answer

    Answer: −70 mV

    The inside is negative relative to the outside.

  2. MCQ Depolarisation is caused by movement of:
    A. Sodium ions inwardB. Potassium ions outwardC. Calcium ions inwardD. Chloride ions inward
    Show answer

    Answer: Sodium ions inward

    Sodium influx raises the membrane potential.

  3. Short answer Why are impulses faster in myelinated neurons?
    Show answer

    Conduction is saltatory, jumping between nodes of Ranvier rather than travelling continuously.
