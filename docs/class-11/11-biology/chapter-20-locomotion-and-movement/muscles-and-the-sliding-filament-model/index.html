Muscles and the Sliding Filament Model

Muscles contract to produce movement. Skeletal muscle consists of bundles of fibres containing actin and myosin filaments.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A muscle fibre contains myofibrils with sarcomeres.
  • In contraction, actin filaments slide over myosin, shortening the sarcomere.
  • Calcium ions trigger binding of actin and myosin.
  • ATP powers myosin heads and is needed for relaxation too.
  • Red muscle fibres are slow and fatigue-resistant; white fibres are fast and tire quickly.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why muscles need calcium

Calcium binds troponin and exposes myosin-binding sites on actin, allowing cross-bridge cycling.

Example 2 Rigor mortis

After death, ATP is depleted so myosin cross-bridges cannot detach, making muscles stiff.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Muscle contraction occurs by:
    A. Sliding of actin over myosinB. Shortening of actin filamentsC. Myosin shrinkingD. Sarcomere growing
    Show answer

    Answer: Sliding of actin over myosin

    The sliding filament model explains contraction.

  2. MCQ Which ion triggers contraction?
    A. CalciumB. SodiumC. PotassiumD. Chloride
    Show answer

    Answer: Calcium

    Calcium release triggers cross-bridge cycling.

  3. Short answer Why is ATP needed for muscle relaxation?
    Show answer

    ATP is needed to pump calcium back and detach myosin heads so the muscle can relax.
