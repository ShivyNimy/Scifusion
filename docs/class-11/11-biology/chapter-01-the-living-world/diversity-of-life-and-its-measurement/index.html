Diversity of Life and Its Measurement

Biodiversity is unevenly distributed; some regions are exceptionally rich and are called hotspots. Classification keeps the variety manageable.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • About 1.7–1.8 million species have been described, but many more exist.
  • India is a megadiverse country with several biodiversity hotspots.
  • Hotspots include the Western Ghats, Himalaya, Indo-Burma and Sundaland (parts in India).
  • Taxonomic keys use pairs of contrasting statements (couplets).
  • Museums and zoological parks preserve specimens and living collections.

Worked examples

Example 1 Western Ghats hotspot

The Western Ghats are home to thousands of endemic plants and animals, which is why they are protected as a biodiversity hotspot.

Example 2 Using a key

A dichotomous key asks whether a plant has, for example, net-like or parallel veins, leading step by step to the correct group.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which is a biodiversity hotspot in India?
    A. Western GhatsB. Thar desertC. Gangetic plainD. Deccan plateau
    Show answer

    Answer: Western Ghats

    The Western Ghats are recognised globally for high endemism.

  2. MCQ A herbarium stores:
    A. Preserved plant specimensB. Live animalsC. Seeds onlyD. Soil samples
    Show answer

    Answer: Preserved plant specimens

    Herbaria keep dried, pressed and labelled plant specimens.

  3. Short answer What is a taxonomic key?
    Show answer

    A device of paired contrasting statements used to identify organisms step by step.
