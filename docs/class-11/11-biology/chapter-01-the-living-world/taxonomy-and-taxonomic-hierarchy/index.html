Taxonomy and Taxonomic Hierarchy

Taxonomy identifies, names and classifies organisms. The hierarchical system groups species into increasingly broad categories.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Taxonomic categories: species, genus, family, order, class, phylum/division, kingdom.
  • Binomial nomenclature: genus + specific epithet, written in italics (Homo sapiens).
  • Taxonomical aids include herbaria, botanical gardens, museums, zoological parks and keys.
  • A species is the fundamental unit of taxonomy.
  • Systematics studies the diversity and evolutionary relationships of organisms.

Worked examples

Example 1 Naming a mango

Mangifera indica: Mangifera is the genus, indica the species. The name is universal and avoids confusion across languages.

Example 2 Herbarium sheet

A pressed, dried and labelled plant specimen preserves the plant's identity for reference and future study.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The correct way to write a scientific name is:
    A. Genus capitalised, species lowercase, italicisedB. Both capitalisedC. Both lowercaseD. Species capitalised
    Show answer

    Answer: Genus capitalised, species lowercase, italicised

    Binomial names follow strict capitalisation and italicisation rules.

  2. MCQ The basic unit of classification is the:
    A. SpeciesB. GenusC. FamilyD. Order
    Show answer

    Answer: Species

    Species is the basic taxonomic unit.

  3. Short answer Arrange in increasing order: order, species, family, genus, class.
    Show answer

    Species -> genus -> family -> order -> class.
