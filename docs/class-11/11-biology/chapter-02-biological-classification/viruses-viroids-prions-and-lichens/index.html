Viruses, Viroids, Prions and Lichens

Viruses are acellular infectious agents. Viroids and prions are simpler, and lichens are obligate partnerships.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Viruses contain nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protein capsid, and are obligate parasites.
  • Viroids are naked RNA molecules that infect plants.
  • Prions are infectious proteins that cause neurological diseases.
  • Lichens are symbiotic associations of algae and fungi.
  • Viruses show characteristics of both living and non-living things.

Worked examples

Example 1 Tobacco mosaic virus

TMV is an RNA virus causing mosaic patterns on tobacco leaves; its discovery founded virology.

Example 2 Lichens as pollution indicators

Lichens are sensitive to air pollution, so their presence indicates clean air and their absence signals poor air quality.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A virus contains:
    A. Nucleic acid and a protein capsidB. Only proteinsC. Only DNAD. Cell wall and nucleus
    Show answer

    Answer: Nucleic acid and a protein capsid

    The capsid encloses the genetic material.

  2. MCQ A lichen is an association of:
    A. Alga and fungusB. Virus and bacteriaC. Two fungiD. Two algae
    Show answer

    Answer: Alga and fungus

    The alga photosynthesises and the fungus provides shelter.

  3. Short answer Why are viruses considered both living and non-living?
    Show answer

    They have genetic material and can reproduce inside a host, but outside they are inert.
