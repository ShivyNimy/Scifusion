Respiratory Organs and Gas Exchange

Gas exchange surfaces are thin, moist and richly supplied with blood. In humans, alveoli exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Insects use tracheae; fish use gills; humans use lungs with alveoli.
  • Alveoli have a large surface area and thin walls for diffusion.
  • Breathing involves inspiration by the diaphragm and intercostal muscles.
  • Oxygen diffuses into blood and binds haemoglobin; CO2 is transported mainly as bicarbonate.
  • Vital capacity is the maximum volume exhaled after a maximum inhalation.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why lungs are spongy

Millions of alveoli give a total surface area of about 70 m2, enough for efficient gas exchange.

Example 2 CO2 transport

About 70% of CO2 is carried as bicarbonate ions, which also help maintain blood pH.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Gas exchange in humans occurs in the:
    A. AlveoliB. TracheaC. BronchiD. Larynx
    Show answer

    Answer: Alveoli

    Alveoli are the functional units of exchange.

  2. MCQ Most CO2 is transported in blood as:
    A. BicarbonateB. Carbamino haemoglobinC. Dissolved CO2D. Carbonate
    Show answer

    Answer: Bicarbonate

    About 70% travels as bicarbonate.

  3. Short answer Why must respiratory surfaces be thin and moist?
    Show answer

    Thin walls and moisture allow fast diffusion of gases and protect the delicate surface.
