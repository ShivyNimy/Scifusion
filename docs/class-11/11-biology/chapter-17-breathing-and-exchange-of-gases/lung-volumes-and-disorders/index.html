Lung Volumes and Disorders

Respiratory volumes describe lung function. Disorders reduce gas exchange and require care.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Tidal volume is the air moved in one normal breath.
  • Functional residual capacity is the air left after normal expiration.
  • Asthma narrows airways and makes breathing difficult.
  • Emphysema destroys alveolar walls and reduces surface area.
  • Smoking damages cilia and alveoli and increases risk of lung disease.

Worked examples

Example 1 Asthma attack

Smooth muscle constricts the bronchioles and mucus increases, so airways narrow and wheezing occurs.

Example 2 Effect of altitude

Lower oxygen pressure stimulates faster breathing and red blood cell production.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which disorder destroys alveolar walls?
    A. EmphysemaB. AsthmaC. BronchitisD. Pneumonia
    Show answer

    Answer: Emphysema

    Emphysema reduces the surface for gas exchange.

  2. MCQ The volume of air breathed in normal rest is called:
    A. Tidal volumeB. Vital capacityC. Residual volumeD. Total capacity
    Show answer

    Answer: Tidal volume

    Tidal volume is the resting breath volume.

  3. Short answer Why is smoking harmful to the respiratory system?
    Show answer

    It damages cilia and alveoli, reduces surface area and increases the risk of cancer and chronic disease.
