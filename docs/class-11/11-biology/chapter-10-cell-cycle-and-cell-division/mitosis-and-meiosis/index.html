Mitosis and Meiosis

Cells divide for growth and reproduction. Mitosis gives identical cells; meiosis produces gametes with genetic variation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The cell cycle has interphase (G1, S, G2) and mitosis.
  • In the S phase DNA replicates, doubling chromosome content.
  • Mitosis stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase.
  • Meiosis has two divisions and produces four haploid cells.
  • Crossing over in prophase I creates genetic variation.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why crossing over matters

Genetic recombination in meiosis produces new combinations of alleles, increasing variation in offspring.

Example 2 Cancer and the cell cycle

Cancer results from loss of cell-cycle control, causing uncontrolled division.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ DNA replicates during:
    A. S phaseB. G1 phaseC. G2 phaseD. Telophase
    Show answer

    Answer: S phase

    Synthesis of DNA occurs in S phase.

  2. MCQ Meiosis produces:
    A. Four haploid cellsB. Two identical cellsC. One cellD. Eight cells
    Show answer

    Answer: Four haploid cells

    Meiosis halves the chromosome number.

  3. Short answer Differentiate mitosis and meiosis.
    Show answer

    Mitosis gives two identical diploid cells for growth; meiosis gives four haploid gametes with genetic variation.
