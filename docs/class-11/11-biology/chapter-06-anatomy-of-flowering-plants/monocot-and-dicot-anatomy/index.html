Monocot and Dicot Anatomy

Monocot and dicot stems and roots differ in vascular bundle arrangement, which helps identify plant groups.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Dicot stems have vascular bundles in a ring; monocot stems have scattered bundles.
  • Dicot roots have a central xylem core; monocot roots have a pith.
  • Secondary growth occurs in dicots through the cambium.
  • Monocots lack secondary growth because they have no cambium ring.
  • Wood is secondary xylem formed by cambial activity.

Worked examples

Example 1 Tree rings and age

Each year's secondary xylem forms one ring, so counting rings estimates the age of a tree.

Example 2 Bamboo has scattered bundles

As a monocot, bamboo has scattered vascular bundles and shows no secondary thickening.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Vascular bundles in dicot stems are arranged:
    A. In a ringB. ScatteredC. RadiallyD. Randomly
    Show answer

    Answer: In a ring

    A ring of bundles is typical of dicots.

  2. MCQ Secondary growth is due to the:
    A. CambiumB. EpidermisC. PithD. Cortex
    Show answer

    Answer: Cambium

    The cambium produces secondary xylem and phloem.

  3. Short answer Why do monocots not show secondary growth?
    Show answer

    They lack a vascular cambium, so they cannot form secondary tissues.
