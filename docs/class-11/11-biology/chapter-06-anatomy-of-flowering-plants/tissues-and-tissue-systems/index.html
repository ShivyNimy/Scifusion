Tissues and Tissue Systems

Plant anatomy studies internal structure. Tissues form three systems: epidermal, ground and vascular.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Epidermal tissue covers surfaces; guard cells regulate stomata.
  • Ground tissue includes parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma.
  • Vascular tissue is xylem (water) and phloem (food).
  • Xylem elements: tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres and parenchyma.
  • Phloem elements: sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem fibres and parenchyma.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why sclerenchyma is dead at maturity

Thick lignified walls provide strength but leave no room for a living protoplast, so the cells are dead when mature.

Example 2 Guard cells

Swelling and shrinking of guard cells opens and closes stomata, controlling gas exchange and water loss.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which tissue provides mechanical strength to plants?
    A. SclerenchymaB. ParenchymaC. PhloemD. Epidermis
    Show answer

    Answer: Sclerenchyma

    Lignified sclerenchyma fibres give strength.

  2. MCQ Water-conducting elements of xylem are:
    A. Tracheids and vesselsB. Sieve tubesC. Companion cellsD. Guard cells
    Show answer

    Answer: Tracheids and vessels

    Tracheids and vessels transport water.

  3. Short answer Differentiate xylem and phloem on the basis of function.
    Show answer

    Xylem transports water and minerals; phloem transports food.
