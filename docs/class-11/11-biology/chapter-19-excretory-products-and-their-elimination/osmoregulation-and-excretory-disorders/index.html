Osmoregulation and Excretory Disorders

The body regulates water and solute balance through the kidneys and hormones. Dialysis replaces kidney function in failure.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) increases water reabsorption.
  • Aldosterone regulates sodium reabsorption.
  • Renal failure requires dialysis or a transplant.
  • Kidney stones are crystallised mineral deposits in the urinary tract.
  • Ammonia, urea and uric acid are nitrogenous wastes; their form depends on water availability.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why we urinate less when dehydrated

ADH release increases water reabsorption, producing small amounts of concentrated urine.

Example 2 Dialysis

A dialysis machine removes nitrogenous wastes from blood when kidneys have failed.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ ADH increases:
    A. Water reabsorptionB. Sodium excretionC. Urine glucoseD. Protein loss
    Show answer

    Answer: Water reabsorption

    ADH makes collecting ducts more permeable to water.

  2. MCQ A person whose kidneys fail may need:
    A. DialysisB. PhysiotherapyC. VaccinationD. Radiotherapy
    Show answer

    Answer: Dialysis

    Dialysis filters waste from blood.

  3. Short answer Why do desert animals excrete uric acid?
    Show answer

    Uric acid is almost insoluble and requires little water to excrete, conserving water.
