The Kidney and Urine Formation

Kidneys filter blood and form urine. The nephron is the functional unit, and the process has three stages.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The nephron has a glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, tubules and collecting duct.
  • Ultrafiltration occurs in the glomerulus under pressure.
  • Reabsorption returns water, glucose and ions from the tubules to blood.
  • Tubular secretion moves wastes from blood into the filtrate.
  • The counter-current mechanism in the loop of Henle conserves water.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why urine is concentrated

The loop of Henle builds a salt gradient that allows water reabsorption, concentrating the urine and conserving water.

Example 2 Glucose generally absent from urine

Glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule; its presence in urine may indicate diabetes.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The functional unit of the kidney is the:
    A. NephronB. NeuronC. AlveolusD. Glomerulus
    Show answer

    Answer: Nephron

    Each kidney contains about a million nephrons.

  2. MCQ Ultrafiltration occurs in the:
    A. GlomerulusB. Loop of HenleC. Collecting ductD. Ureter
    Show answer

    Answer: Glomerulus

    Pressure forces filtrate into Bowman's capsule.

  3. Short answer Name the three processes of urine formation.
    Show answer

    Ultrafiltration, selective reabsorption and tubular secretion.
