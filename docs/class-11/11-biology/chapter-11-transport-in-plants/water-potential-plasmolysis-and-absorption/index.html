Water Potential, Plasmolysis and Absorption

Water moves by osmosis from high to low water potential. Plant cells use water potential and pressure to regulate turgor.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Water potential ψw = ψs + ψp (solute and pressure potentials).
  • Osmosis moves water toward lower water potential.
  • Turgid cells have positive pressure potential; plasmolysed cells lose turgor.
  • Root hairs increase surface area for water absorption.
  • Mineral ions are often actively absorbed using energy.

Worked examples

Example 1 Fresh lettuce in salt water

Salt water has lower water potential than the cells, so water leaves and the lettuce wilts — plasmolysis.

Example 2 Root pressure

Active ion transport into the root draws water in and pushes it up, explaining guttation in some plants.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Water moves by osmosis from:
    A. Higher water potential to lowerB. Lower to higherC. Low solute to highD. Cold to hot
    Show answer

    Answer: Higher water potential to lower

    Water moves down the water potential gradient.

  2. MCQ Plasmolysis occurs when a cell is placed in:
    A. Hypertonic solutionB. Hypotonic solutionC. Pure waterD. Isotonic solution
    Show answer

    Answer: Hypertonic solution

    Water leaves the cell in a hypertonic solution.

  3. Short answer Define water potential and give its units.
    Show answer

    The potential energy of water per unit volume relative to pure water; measured in bars or pascals.
