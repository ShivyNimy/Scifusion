Transpiration, Ascent of Sap and Translocation

Water moves up tall trees mainly by transpiration pull; food moves through the phloem by pressure flow.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Transpiration is water loss from leaves through stomata.
  • Cohesion-tension theory explains the ascent of sap: transpiration pull draws a continuous water column.
  • Guttation is the loss of water droplets from leaf margins.
  • Translocation moves sugars in the phloem from source to sink.
  • The pressure-flow hypothesis explains phloem transport.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why trees wilt in drought

Transpiration exceeds water uptake, so cells lose turgor and leaves droop.

Example 2 Sugar transport in spring

Stored sugars move from roots (source) to developing buds (sink) through the phloem.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Ascent of sap is mainly explained by:
    A. Cohesion-tension theoryB. Root pressureC. Active transportD. Diffusion
    Show answer

    Answer: Cohesion-tension theory

    Transpiration pull drives water up the xylem.

  2. MCQ Food is transported in:
    A. PhloemB. XylemC. StomataD. Lenticels
    Show answer

    Answer: Phloem

    Phloem carries sugars and other solutes.

  3. Short answer Why does transpiration pull work in tall trees?
    Show answer

    Water molecules cohere, forming a continuous column that is pulled up by evaporation at the leaves.
