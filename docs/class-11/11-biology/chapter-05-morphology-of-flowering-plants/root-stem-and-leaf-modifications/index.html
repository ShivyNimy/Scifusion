Root, Stem and Leaf Modifications

Plant organs are adapted for storage, support and survival. Modifications reveal function.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Tap roots store food (carrot, turnip); prop roots support banyan; pneumatophores aid breathing in mangroves.
  • Stems may be underground (potato, ginger) or modified for climbing (tendrils).
  • Leaves may become spines (cactus) or tendrils (pea) and may store water (aloe).
  • Phyllotaxy is the arrangement of leaves on the stem.
  • Modifications are adaptations to environment and function.

Worked examples

Example 1 Mangrove pneumatophores

Roots grow upwards above the mud to take in air in waterlogged soil, an adaptation to saline, oxygen-poor habitats.

Example 2 Cactus spines

Leaves reduced to spines reduce water loss, while the green stem performs photosynthesis.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Potato is a modified:
    A. StemB. RootC. LeafD. Flower
    Show answer

    Answer: Stem

    The potato tuber is an underground stem with eyes (buds).

  2. MCQ Tendrils in peas are modified:
    A. LeavesB. RootsC. StemsD. Flowers
    Show answer

    Answer: Leaves

    Pea tendrils are modified leaf parts that help climbing.

  3. Short answer Why are cactus leaves reduced to spines?
    Show answer

    To reduce transpiration and protect the plant while the green stem carries out photosynthesis.
