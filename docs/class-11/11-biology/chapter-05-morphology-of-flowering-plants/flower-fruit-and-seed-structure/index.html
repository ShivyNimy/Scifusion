Flower, Fruit and Seed Structure

The flower contains the reproductive organs. Fruits and seeds protect and disperse the embryo.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A typical flower has calyx (sepals), corolla (petals), androecium (stamens) and gynoecium (carpels).
  • The ovary contains ovules; after fertilisation it becomes the fruit.
  • Aestivation is the arrangement of sepals or petals in a bud.
  • Placentation is the arrangement of ovules within the ovary.
  • Seeds contain an embryo, cotyledons and a seed coat.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reading a flower in the field

Count the sepals, petals, stamens and carpels. The numbers and fusion help identify the family and species.

Example 2 From ovary to fruit

The ovary wall becomes the pericarp; the ovule becomes the seed. The fruit protects and helps disperse the seed.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The gynoecium consists of:
    A. CarpelsB. StamensC. PetalsD. Sepals
    Show answer

    Answer: Carpels

    Carpels are the female reproductive organs.

  2. MCQ The fruit develops from the:
    A. OvaryB. StamenC. SepalD. Petal
    Show answer

    Answer: Ovary

    The ovary enlarges into the fruit after fertilisation.

  3. Short answer Name the four whorls of a flower.
    Show answer

    Calyx, corolla, androecium and gynoecium.
