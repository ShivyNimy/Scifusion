Calvin Cycle and Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar. Many factors limit the rate.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma of the chloroplast.
  • CO2 is fixed by RuBisCO onto RuBP.
  • The cycle uses ATP and NADPH to reduce carbon compounds to sugar.
  • C3 plants fix CO2 once; C4 plants fix it twice and are more efficient in hot climates.
  • Limiting factors include light, CO2 concentration and temperature.

Worked examples

Example 1 C4 advantage

C4 plants concentrate CO2 in bundle sheath cells, reducing photorespiration and boosting productivity in heat.

Example 2 Greenhouse CO2 enrichment

Increasing CO2 can increase photosynthesis, up to the point where another factor becomes limiting.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The Calvin cycle occurs in the:
    A. StromaB. Thylakoid membraneC. CytoplasmD. Mitochondria
    Show answer

    Answer: Stroma

    Carbon fixation happens in the stroma.

  2. MCQ The enzyme that fixes CO2 in C3 plants is:
    A. RuBisCOB. PepsinC. CatalaseD. Nitrogenase
    Show answer

    Answer: RuBisCO

    RuBisCO catalyses the first step of carbon fixation.

  3. Short answer Why are C4 plants more efficient in hot climates?
    Show answer

    They concentrate CO2 at the site of fixation, minimising photorespiration.
