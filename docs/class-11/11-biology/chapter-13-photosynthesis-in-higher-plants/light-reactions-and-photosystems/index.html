Light Reactions and Photosystems

Photosynthesis uses light energy to produce ATP and NADPH, which drive sugar synthesis.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Chlorophyll a and b absorb light, mainly in blue and red regions.
  • Photosystem II splits water, releasing oxygen and electrons.
  • The electron transport chain pumps protons, producing a gradient used to make ATP (photophosphorylation).
  • Photosystem I produces NADPH.
  • Light reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes.

Worked examples

Example 1 Effect of green light

Chlorophyll reflects green light, so it is least effective for photosynthesis, which is why a leaf appears green.

Example 2 Where the oxygen comes from

Oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from water, not carbon dioxide — shown by isotope experiments.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from:
    A. WaterB. Carbon dioxideC. GlucoseD. Chlorophyll
    Show answer

    Answer: Water

    Photolysis of water in PSII releases oxygen.

  2. MCQ Light reactions occur in the:
    A. Thylakoid membranesB. StromaC. CytoplasmD. Mitochondria
    Show answer

    Answer: Thylakoid membranes

    The thylakoids contain the photosystems.

  3. Short answer Why is chlorophyll green?
    Show answer

    It absorbs red and blue light strongly and reflects green light.
