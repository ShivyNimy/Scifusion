Absorption and Digestive Disorders

Nutrients are absorbed in different regions of the gut. Disorders can be caused by infection, diet or organ dysfunction.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Glucose and amino acids are absorbed in the small intestine by active transport.
  • Fatty acids and glycerol are absorbed as chylomicrons into lymph.
  • Vitamin B12 absorption needs intrinsic factor from the stomach.
  • Jaundice is associated with liver dysfunction and bilirubin accumulation.
  • Vomiting and diarrhoea cause dehydration and loss of electrolytes.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why rapid fluid replacement is needed in diarrhoea

Diarrhoea causes severe water and electrolyte loss, so ORS restores fluids and salts.

Example 2 Fat-soluble vitamins

Vitamins A, D, E and K need bile for absorption because they are fat-soluble.

  1. MCQ Which vitamin needs intrinsic factor for absorption?
    A. Vitamin B12B. Vitamin CC. Vitamin B1D. Vitamin K
    Show answer

    Answer: Vitamin B12

    Intrinsic factor from the stomach is needed for B12 uptake.

  2. MCQ Most water is absorbed in the:
    A. Large intestineB. StomachC. OesophagusD. Mouth
    Show answer

    Answer: Large intestine

    The large intestine absorbs most remaining water.

  3. Short answer Why is jaundice linked to liver problems?
    Show answer

    The liver processes bilirubin; when it cannot, bilirubin accumulates and yellows the skin and eyes.
