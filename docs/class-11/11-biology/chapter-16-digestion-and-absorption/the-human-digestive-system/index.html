The Human Digestive System

Digestion breaks food into absorbable molecules. The alimentary canal and associated glands secrete enzymes that act at specific pH values.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine form the alimentary canal.
  • Saliva contains amylase, which begins starch digestion.
  • The stomach secretes HCl and pepsin, digesting proteins in an acidic medium.
  • The small intestine receives bile and pancreatic juice and completes digestion; villi absorb nutrients.
  • The large intestine absorbs water and electrolytes and forms faeces.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why the stomach does not digest itself

A thick mucus layer and rapid cell replacement protect the stomach lining from acid and pepsin.

Example 2 Bile and fat digestion

Bile salts emulsify large fat droplets into smaller ones, increasing the surface area for lipase.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Protein digestion begins in the:
    A. StomachB. MouthC. Small intestineD. Large intestine
    Show answer

    Answer: Stomach

    Pepsin in the stomach starts protein digestion.

  2. MCQ Bile is produced by the:
    A. LiverB. PancreasC. StomachD. Gall bladder
    Show answer

    Answer: Liver

    The liver produces bile, stored in the gall bladder.

  3. Short answer Why is the small intestine the main site of absorption?
    Show answer

    Its long length and millions of villi and microvilli give an enormous surface area.
