Growth Stages and Seed Germination

Seed germination, growth, flowering and senescence are regulated by internal and external factors.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Imbibition is the first step of seed germination, absorbing water.
  • Germination needs water, oxygen and a suitable temperature.
  • Some seeds need light or stratification to germinate.
  • Growth curves show an initial slow phase, rapid growth and a plateau.
  • Senescence and abscission are controlled processes that recycle nutrients.

Worked examples

Example 1 Dormancy

A dormant seed waits for favourable conditions; the seed coat may block water or oxygen, or inhibitors may block germination.

Example 2 Growth curve

An annual crop grows slowly at first, rapidly in the middle season, then levels off as nutrients go into grain formation.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The first step of seed germination is:
    A. ImbibitionB. PhotosynthesisC. FloweringD. Senescence
    Show answer

    Answer: Imbibition

    Seeds first absorb water and swell.

  2. MCQ Seed dormancy helps the seed to:
    A. Survive unfavourable conditionsB. Grow fasterC. PhotosynthesiseD. Avoid water
    Show answer

    Answer: Survive unfavourable conditions

    Dormancy delays germination until conditions are suitable.

  3. Short answer Why do seeds need oxygen to germinate?
    Show answer

    Germination requires energy from aerobic respiration, which needs oxygen.
