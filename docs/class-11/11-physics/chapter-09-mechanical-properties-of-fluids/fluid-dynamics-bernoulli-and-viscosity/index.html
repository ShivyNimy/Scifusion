Fluid Dynamics: Bernoulli and Viscosity

Moving fluids obey continuity and Bernoulli's equation. Real fluids have viscosity that resists flow.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Equation of continuity: A1v1 = A2v2 for incompressible flow.
  • Bernoulli's theorem: P + ½ρv2 + ρgh = constant along a streamline.
  • Faster flow means lower pressure, which explains lift and the working of sprayers.
  • Viscous force F = ηA dv/dx; η is the coefficient of viscosity.
  • Terminal velocity is reached when viscous drag plus buoyancy balances weight.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why aeroplanes fly

Air moves faster over the curved upper wing, lowering pressure there, so the higher pressure below produces lift.

Example 2 Terminal velocity of a raindrop

Drag increases with speed until it balances weight; the drop then falls at a constant terminal speed instead of accelerating.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Bernoulli's theorem is based on:
    A. Conservation of energyB. Conservation of massC. Conservation of momentumD. Newton's third law
    Show answer

    Answer: Conservation of energy

    It follows from energy conservation in an ideal fluid.

  2. MCQ At a narrower section of a pipe, the fluid speed:
    A. IncreasesB. DecreasesC. Stays the sameD. Becomes zero
    Show answer

    Answer: Increases

    By continuity, smaller area means higher speed.

  3. Short answer State the equation of continuity and explain it.
    Show answer

    A1v1 = A2v2; the mass flow rate of an incompressible fluid is constant, so speed rises where the pipe narrows.
