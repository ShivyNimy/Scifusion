Pressure, Pascal's Law and Buoyancy

Fluids exert pressure in all directions. Pascal's law and Archimedes' principle underlie hydraulics and floating.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Pressure at depth h: P = P0 + ρgh.
  • Pascal's law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.
  • Buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced (Archimedes' principle).
  • A body floats if its average density is less than the fluid's.
  • Hydraulic machines use Pascal's law to multiply force.

Worked examples

Example 1 Hydraulic lift

A small force on a small piston creates pressure transmitted to a larger piston, producing a larger force: F2 = F1 A2/A1.

Example 2 Iceberg floating

Ice density is about 0.92 of seawater, so roughly 92% of an iceberg lies below the surface.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Pressure at depth in a liquid depends on:
    A. Depth and densityB. Shape of containerC. Total volumeD. Surface area
    Show answer

    Answer: Depth and density

    P = ρgh, so depth and density matter.

  2. MCQ Archimedes' principle gives the buoyant force as:
    A. Weight of displaced fluidB. Weight of the bodyC. Volume of the bodyD. Density of the body
    Show answer

    Answer: Weight of displaced fluid

    The upthrust equals the weight of the displaced fluid.

  3. Short answer Why does a ship made of iron float?
    Show answer

    Its hollow shape displaces water whose weight equals the ship's weight, so it floats.
