Newton's Laws and Common Forces

Newton's three laws explain motion in terms of forces. Friction, tension, normal force and spring force are the forces that appear most often.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • First law: no net force means no change in velocity (inertia).
  • Second law: Fnet = ma, or F = dp/dt.
  • Third law: action and reaction are equal, opposite and act on different bodies.
  • Friction f ≤ μN; static friction adjusts up to a maximum.
  • Tension is the same throughout a light, frictionless string.

Worked examples

Example 1 Elevator physics

A person of mass m in an elevator accelerating upward experiences N = m(g + a); downward acceleration gives N = m(g − a).

Example 2 Block on an incline

On a frictionless incline at angle θ, the acceleration is g sin θ regardless of mass.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The normal force on a body on a horizontal floor is:
    A. mgB. maC. m(g + a) alwaysD. m(g − a) always
    Show answer

    Answer: mg

    When not accelerating vertically, N balances weight.

  2. MCQ A body on a circular path at constant speed has:
    A. Changing velocityB. Constant velocityC. Zero accelerationD. No force
    Show answer

    Answer: Changing velocity

    Direction of velocity changes, so velocity is not constant.

  3. Short answer Why do we require seat belts even when a car moves at constant speed?
    Show answer

    If the car stops suddenly, inertia carries the passenger forward; the belt provides the stopping force.
