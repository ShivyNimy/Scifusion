Work and Energy Theorem

Work is the transfer of energy by a force. The work-energy theorem links net work with the change in kinetic energy.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • W = F·s = Fs cos θ; work is a scalar.
  • Work is positive when force and displacement are in the same direction, negative when opposite.
  • Work-energy theorem: Wnet = ΔKE.
  • Kinetic energy KE = ½mv2; potential energy for a spring is ½kx2.
  • Gravity is a conservative force; friction is non-conservative.

Worked examples

Example 1 Work by friction

A 5 kg box sliding 2 m against 10 N friction loses 20 J of mechanical energy, which becomes heat.

Example 2 Spring compression

Compressing a spring of k = 200 N/m by 0.1 m stores ½ × 200 × 0.01 = 1 J of energy.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Work done is zero when:
    A. Force is perpendicular to displacementB. Force is largeC. Displacement is largeD. Speed is constant
    Show answer

    Answer: Force is perpendicular to displacement

    cos 90° = 0, so W = 0.

  2. MCQ The unit of energy is:
    A. JouleB. NewtonC. WattD. Pascal
    Show answer

    Answer: Joule

    Energy is measured in joules.

  3. Short answer A force of 20 N moves a box 4 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.
    Show answer

    W = Fs cos 0° = 20 × 4 = 80 J.
