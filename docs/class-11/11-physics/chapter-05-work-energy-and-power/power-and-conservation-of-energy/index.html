Power and Conservation of Energy

Power is the rate of doing work. Energy is conserved but may be converted into less useful forms.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Power P = W/t = Fv.
  • 1 horsepower ≈ 746 W; 1 kWh = 3.6 × 106 J.
  • Conservation of mechanical energy holds when only conservative forces act.
  • Efficiency = useful output energy ÷ total input energy × 100%.
  • Collisions conserve momentum; elastic collisions also conserve kinetic energy.

Worked examples

Example 1 Climbing stairs

A 60 kg person climbing 5 m in 10 s does 60 × 9.8 × 5 = 2940 J, giving a power of 294 W.

Example 2 Elastic collision

In an elastic collision between equal masses, one at rest, the moving ball stops and the other moves off with the original velocity.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Power is:
    A. Rate of doing workB. Force times timeC. Work times timeD. Change in momentum
    Show answer

    Answer: Rate of doing work

    Power = work ÷ time.

  2. MCQ In a perfectly inelastic collision:
    A. Kinetic energy is not conservedB. Momentum is not conservedC. Mass changesD. Energy increases
    Show answer

    Answer: Kinetic energy is not conserved

    The bodies stick together and some KE becomes heat or deformation.

  3. Short answer A pump lifts 500 kg of water 10 m in 20 s. Find its power (g = 10 m/s2).
    Show answer

    Work = mgh = 500 × 10 × 10 = 50,000 J; P = 50,000/20 = 2500 W.
