Heat Transfer and Newton's Law of Cooling

Heat moves by conduction, convection and radiation. Cooling rates depend on temperature difference and surface.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Conduction rate H = kA(T1 − T2)/L.
  • Convection transfers heat through fluid motion; radiation needs no medium.
  • Stefan's law: power radiated ∝ T4; Wien's law: λmax ∝ 1/T.
  • Newton's law of cooling: rate of cooling ∝ temperature difference for small differences.
  • Black surfaces absorb and emit better than shiny ones.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why a thermos flask works

Double walls with a vacuum prevent conduction and convection, and silvered surfaces reduce radiation, so drinks stay hot or cold for hours.

Example 2 Cooling of coffee

The coffee cools fast when much hotter than the room and slows as it approaches room temperature, in line with Newton's law of cooling.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Heat transfer that needs no medium is:
    A. RadiationB. ConductionC. ConvectionD. Evaporation
    Show answer

    Answer: Radiation

    Radiation travels through a vacuum, like sunlight.

  2. MCQ A good absorber is also a good:
    A. EmitterB. InsulatorC. ConductorD. Reflector
    Show answer

    Answer: Emitter

    Kirchhoff's law links absorptivity and emissivity.

  3. Short answer Why do we feel hot near a fire even in a cold room?
    Show answer

    The fire radiates infrared energy that reaches us without needing a medium.
