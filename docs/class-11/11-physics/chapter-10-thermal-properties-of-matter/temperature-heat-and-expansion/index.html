Temperature, Heat and Expansion

Temperature measures hotness; heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference. Materials expand with temperature.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin scales are related; K = °C + 273.15.
  • Linear expansion ΔL = LαΔT; area expansion uses 2α; volume expansion uses 3α.
  • Specific heat capacity c: Q = mcΔT.
  • Latent heat L: Q = mL for a change of state at constant temperature.
  • Water has a very high specific heat, which stabilises climate.

Worked examples

Example 1 Heating water

To heat 2 kg of water by 30 °C, with c = 4200 J/kg·K: Q = 2 × 4200 × 30 = 252,000 J.

Example 2 Melting ice

Melting 1 kg of ice at 0 °C takes Q = mL = 1 × 3.34 × 105 J = 334 kJ.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The SI unit of specific heat capacity is:
    A. J/kg·KB. J/KC. W/m·KD. J/mol
    Show answer

    Answer: J/kg·K

    It is the energy per kilogram per kelvin.

  2. MCQ During melting, the temperature:
    A. Remains constantB. RisesC. FallsD. Doubles
    Show answer

    Answer: Remains constant

    Latent heat changes state without changing temperature.

  3. Short answer How much heat is needed to raise 0.5 kg of water by 10 °C (c = 4200 J/kg·K)?
    Show answer

    Q = mcΔT = 0.5 × 4200 × 10 = 21,000 J.
