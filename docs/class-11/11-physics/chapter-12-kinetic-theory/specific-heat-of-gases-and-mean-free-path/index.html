Specific Heat of Gases and Mean Free Path

Gases have two specific heats, Cp and Cv. Molecules travel only short distances between collisions.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Cp − Cv = R for an ideal gas; Cp > Cv.
  • Monatomic gas: Cv = (3/2)R, γ = 5/3; diatomic: Cv = (5/2)R, γ = 7/5.
  • Mean free path λ = 1/(√2 πnd2).
  • Degrees of freedom determine the energy distribution — the equipartition theorem.
  • Real gases deviate from ideal behaviour at high pressure and low temperature.

Worked examples

Example 1 Value of γ

For a monatomic gas γ = 1.67, for a diatomic gas γ = 1.4. These values affect the speed of sound.

Example 2 Mean free path

At standard conditions λ for air molecules is about 10−7 m — tiny compared with the size of the container.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ For an ideal gas, Cp − Cv equals:
    A. RB. 2RC. R/2D. 0
    Show answer

    Answer: R

    This is Mayer's relation.

  2. MCQ γ for a monatomic gas is:
    A. 5/3B. 7/5C. 4/3D. 1
    Show answer

    Answer: 5/3

    Cp/Cv = (5/2)/(3/2) = 5/3.

  3. Short answer Why does temperature stay constant during an isothermal process?
    Show answer

    Heat added equals work done by the gas, so internal energy and hence temperature do not change.
