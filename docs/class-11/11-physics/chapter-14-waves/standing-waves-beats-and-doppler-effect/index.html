Standing Waves, Beats and Doppler Effect

Superposition produces standing waves, beats and the Doppler shift. These explain musical notes and moving-source effects.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Standing waves form nodes (no displacement) and antinodes (maximum displacement).
  • On a string fixed at both ends, λ = 2L/n for the nth harmonic.
  • Beats: beat frequency = |f1 − f2|.
  • Doppler effect: observed frequency rises when source and observer approach.
  • An overtone is any resonant frequency above the fundamental.

Worked examples

Example 1 Guitar string

A 0.65 m string fixed at both ends has fundamental wavelength 1.3 m; with v = 300 m/s the frequency is f = 300/1.3 ≈ 231 Hz.

Example 2 Ambulance siren

The siren sounds higher in pitch as the ambulance approaches and lower as it passes, because of the Doppler shift.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A node in a standing wave has:
    A. Zero displacementB. Maximum displacementC. Maximum speedD. Maximum amplitude
    Show answer

    Answer: Zero displacement

    Nodes are points of no motion.

  2. MCQ Beat frequency equals:
    A. Difference of frequenciesB. Sum of frequenciesC. Product of frequenciesD. Half the difference
    Show answer

    Answer: Difference of frequencies

    Beats arise from the difference between two close frequencies.

  3. Short answer Two tuning forks of 256 Hz and 260 Hz are sounded together. Find the beat frequency.
    Show answer

    Beat frequency = |260 − 256| = 4 Hz.
