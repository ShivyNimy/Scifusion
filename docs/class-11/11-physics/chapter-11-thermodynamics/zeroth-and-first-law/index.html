Zeroth and First Law

Thermodynamics describes energy changes in systems. The first law is the conservation of energy applied to heat and work.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Zeroth law: two bodies in thermal equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other — basis of temperature.
  • First law: ΔU = Q − W, where W is work done by the system.
  • Work done by a gas at constant pressure: W = PΔV.
  • Internal energy is a state function; heat and work are path-dependent.
  • Isothermal, adiabatic, isochoric and isobaric processes are the basic ones.

Worked examples

Example 1 Gas expansion

A gas expanding at constant 105 Pa by 2 × 10−3 m3 does W = PΔV = 200 J of work.

Example 2 Isothermal compression

At constant temperature, compressing a gas requires work, which is given out as heat, so ΔU = 0.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The first law of thermodynamics is:
    A. ΔU = Q − WB. ΔU = Q + W alwaysC. Q = ΔU × WD. W = Q/T
    Show answer

    Answer: ΔU = Q − W

    Q is heat added and W is work done by the system.

  2. MCQ In an adiabatic process:
    A. Q = 0B. W = 0C. ΔU = 0D. T is constant
    Show answer

    Answer: Q = 0

    No heat is exchanged with the surroundings.

  3. Short answer A gas absorbs 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work. Find ΔU.
    Show answer

    ΔU = Q − W = 500 − 200 = 300 J.
