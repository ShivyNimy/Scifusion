Second Law, Heat Engines and Refrigerators

The second law limits the conversion of heat into work. Engines have a maximum possible efficiency.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Kelvin-Planck statement: no engine converts all heat into work.
  • Clausius statement: heat cannot flow from cold to hot without work.
  • Carnot efficiency η = 1 − Tc/Th (temperatures in kelvin).
  • A heat engine takes Qh from the hot reservoir, does work W and rejects Qc to the cold reservoir.
  • A refrigerator is a reversed heat engine with COP = Qc/W.

Worked examples

Example 1 Carnot engine

Between 500 K and 300 K the Carnot efficiency is 1 − 300/500 = 40%. No real engine can exceed this.

Example 2 Why engines are inefficient

Friction, incomplete combustion and heat losses all reduce efficiency below the Carnot limit.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Carnot efficiency depends on:
    A. Source and sink temperaturesB. Fuel typeC. Engine sizeD. Speed only
    Show answer

    Answer: Source and sink temperatures

    η = 1 − Tc/Th.

  2. MCQ The second law forbids:
    A. Converting all heat into work in a cycleB. Doing workC. Heat transferD. Cooling
    Show answer

    Answer: Converting all heat into work in a cycle

    Some heat must be rejected to a colder reservoir.

  3. Short answer An engine works between 600 K and 300 K. Find its maximum efficiency.
    Show answer

    η = 1 − 300/600 = 0.5, so 50%.
