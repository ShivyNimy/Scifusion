Position, Displacement, Velocity and Acceleration

Kinematics describes motion without asking about its cause. Position, displacement, velocity and acceleration are the basic variables.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Displacement is the change in position; distance is the path length travelled.
  • Average velocity = displacement ÷ time; instantaneous velocity is the limit as time tends to zero.
  • Acceleration = rate of change of velocity.
  • For uniform acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at2 and v2 = u2 + 2as.
  • The slope of a position-time graph gives velocity; the area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement.

Worked examples

Example 1 Free fall

A ball dropped from rest falls ½gt2. In 2 s, with g = 9.8 m/s2, it falls 19.6 m and reaches 19.6 m/s.

Example 2 Relative motion

Two trains moving at 60 km/h in opposite directions approach each other at 120 km/h relative to each other.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The area under a velocity-time graph gives:
    A. DisplacementB. AccelerationC. SpeedD. Force
    Show answer

    Answer: Displacement

    Integrating velocity over time gives displacement.

  2. MCQ A body thrown upward reaches its highest point when its velocity is:
    A. ZeroB. MaximumC. Equal to gD. Negative
    Show answer

    Answer: Zero

    At the top the vertical velocity is momentarily zero.

  3. Short answer A car accelerates from rest at 2 m/s2 for 5 s. Find its final velocity and distance.
    Show answer

    v = at = 10 m/s; s = ½at2 = ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 m.
