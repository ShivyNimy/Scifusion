Graphs and Relative Velocity

Graphical analysis turns motion into geometry. Relative velocity lets us describe motion from different frames.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A straight position-time line means uniform velocity; a curve means acceleration.
  • Velocity-time graphs: horizontal line = constant velocity; sloped line = constant acceleration.
  • The relative velocity of A with respect to B is vAB = vA − vB.
  • Deceleration appears as a negative slope on a velocity-time graph.
  • The area between the velocity-time curve and the time axis (with sign) gives displacement.

Worked examples

Example 1 Graph of a ball thrown up

Velocity decreases linearly with time, becomes zero at the top, then becomes negative as the ball falls back.

Example 2 River crossing

A boat heading straight across a flowing river drifts downstream; its resultant velocity is the vector sum of boat and current velocities.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A negative acceleration with positive velocity means the body is:
    A. Slowing downB. Speeding upC. At restD. Turning
    Show answer

    Answer: Slowing down

    Velocity and acceleration in opposite directions reduce speed.

  2. MCQ Relative velocity of A with respect to B is:
    A. vA − vBB. vA + vBC. vB − vAD. vA × vB
    Show answer

    Answer: vA − vB

    It is the velocity of A measured in B's frame.

  3. Short answer A stone is dropped from a 45 m cliff. How long does it take to land (g = 10 m/s2)?
    Show answer

    s = ½gt2 gives 45 = 5t2, so t = 3 s.
