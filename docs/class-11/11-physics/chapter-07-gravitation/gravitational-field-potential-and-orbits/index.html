Gravitational Field, Potential and Orbits

Newton's law of gravitation explains falling bodies, orbits and the motion of planets. Field strength and potential describe gravity at a point.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • F = Gm1m2/r2; gravitational field g = GM/r2.
  • Gravitational potential energy U = −GMm/r; gravitational potential V = −GM/r.
  • Orbital velocity vo = √(GM/r); escape velocity ve = √(2GM/R).
  • Kepler's third law: T2 ∝ a3 for orbits around the same central body.
  • Inside a uniform spherical shell the gravitational field is zero.

Worked examples

Example 1 Satellite speed

For a satellite 400 km above Earth (r ≈ 6.77 × 106 m) with GM = 3.986 × 1014 m3/s2, v = √(GM/r) ≈ 7.67 km/s.

Example 2 Escape velocity

ve = √(2gR) = √(2 × 9.8 × 6.4 × 106) ≈ 11.2 km/s.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The escape velocity from Earth is:
    A. ≈ 11.2 km/sB. ≈ 7.9 km/sC. ≈ 3 × 108 m/sD. ≈ 9.8 m/s
    Show answer

    Answer: ≈ 11.2 km/s

    It is about 11.2 km/s.

  2. MCQ Gravitational potential energy of a satellite in orbit is:
    A. NegativeB. PositiveC. ZeroD. Undefined
    Show answer

    Answer: Negative

    It is negative because the force is attractive with zero at infinity.

  3. Short answer Why is g smaller at the equator than at the poles?
    Show answer

    Earth bulges at the equator (larger radius) and rotation reduces effective gravity there.
