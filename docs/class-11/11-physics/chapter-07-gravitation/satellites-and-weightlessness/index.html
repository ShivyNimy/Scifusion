Satellites and Weightlessness

A satellite is in continual free fall around Earth. Its horizontal speed keeps it from hitting the ground.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Orbital period T = 2π√(r3/GM).
  • Geostationary satellites orbit at about 36,000 km with a period of 24 h.
  • Inside an orbiting satellite everything is in free fall, giving apparent weightlessness.
  • Total energy of a satellite in orbit is E = −GMm/(2r).
  • Rockets need large fuel mass; the rocket equation relates speed gain to exhaust speed and mass ratio.

Worked examples

Example 1 Geostationary orbit

A satellite at about 36,000 km above the equator takes one day to orbit, so it stays over the same point — ideal for communication and weather.

Example 2 Weightlessness in orbit

Astronauts float not because gravity is absent but because they and the spacecraft fall together.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A geostationary satellite has an orbital period of:
    A. 24 hoursB. 12 hoursC. 1 monthD. 1 year
    Show answer

    Answer: 24 hours

    It matches Earth's rotation period.

  2. MCQ Weightlessness in orbit is due to:
    A. Free fallB. Zero gravityC. No massD. No atmosphere
    Show answer

    Answer: Free fall

    Gravity provides the centripetal force; the astronaut and cabin fall together.

  3. Short answer State Kepler's third law.
    Show answer

    The square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.
