Stress, Strain and Elastic Moduli

Solids deform under force. Stress is force per area and strain is fractional deformation; their ratio is a modulus.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Stress = force ÷ area; strain = change in dimension ÷ original dimension.
  • Young's modulus Y = longitudinal stress ÷ longitudinal strain.
  • Bulk modulus B = pressure ÷ fractional volume change.
  • Shear modulus G = shear stress ÷ shear strain.
  • Steel has a much larger Young's modulus than rubber, so it is stiffer.

Worked examples

Example 1 Stretching a wire

A steel wire of length 2 m and area 10−4 m2 under 100 N stretches by ΔL = FL/(AY). With Y = 2 × 1011 N/m2, ΔL = 100 × 2/(10−4 × 2 × 1011) = 10−5 m.

Example 2 Bulk compression

Water has a high bulk modulus, so it resists compression strongly — which is why it is used in hydraulic systems.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Young's modulus is the ratio of:
    A. Longitudinal stress to longitudinal strainB. Shear to volumeC. Force to areaD. Pressure to density
    Show answer

    Answer: Longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain

    It measures stiffness along the length.

  2. MCQ A material with a larger Young's modulus is:
    A. StifferB. SofterC. More ductileD. Less elastic
    Show answer

    Answer: Stiffer

    Higher Y means less strain for the same stress.

  3. Short answer Define strain and give its unit.
    Show answer

    Strain is the ratio of change in dimension to original dimension; it has no unit.
