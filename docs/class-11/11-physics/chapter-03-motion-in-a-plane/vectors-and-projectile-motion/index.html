Vectors and Projectile Motion

Motion in two dimensions uses vectors. A projectile is an object moving under gravity with a constant horizontal velocity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Vectors add by the triangle or parallelogram law; components resolve a vector along axes.
  • Projectile motion: horizontal motion is uniform, vertical motion is uniformly accelerated.
  • Time of flight T = 2u sin θ / g; range R = u2 sin 2θ / g; maximum height H = u2 sin2θ / 2g.
  • Maximum range occurs at 45° for a given speed.
  • The horizontal velocity stays constant if air resistance is ignored.

Worked examples

Example 1 Football kick

A ball kicked at 20 m/s at 30° has range R = 400 × sin 60° / 9.8 ≈ 35.3 m and time of flight T = 2 × 20 × 0.5 / 9.8 ≈ 2.04 s.

Example 2 Why 45° gives maximum range

sin 2θ is maximum when 2θ = 90°, that is θ = 45°.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The horizontal acceleration of a projectile (ignoring air resistance) is:
    A. ZeroB. gC. g sin θD. g cos θ
    Show answer

    Answer: Zero

    Only gravity acts, vertically.

  2. MCQ Maximum range for a given launch speed occurs at:
    A. 45°B. 30°C. 60°D. 90°
    Show answer

    Answer: 45°

    R ∝ sin 2θ, maximum at θ = 45°.

  3. Short answer A ball is thrown at 10 m/s at 45°. Find its range (g = 10 m/s2).
    Show answer

    R = u2 sin 2θ / g = 100 × 1 / 10 = 10 m.
