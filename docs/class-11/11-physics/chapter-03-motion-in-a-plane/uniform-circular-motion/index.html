Uniform Circular Motion

In uniform circular motion the speed is constant but velocity changes because direction changes, producing centripetal acceleration.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Angular velocity ω = 2π/T = v/r.
  • Centripetal acceleration ac = v2/r = ω2r, directed toward the centre.
  • Centripetal force F = mv2/r.
  • Linear speed v = ωr relates the linear and angular descriptions.
  • Centrifugal force is a pseudo-force experienced in the rotating frame.

Worked examples

Example 1 Car on a curve

A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s on a 50 m radius curve needs F = mv2/r = 1000 × 400 / 50 = 8000 N of friction toward the centre.

Example 2 Why cyclists lean inward

Leaning aligns the resultant of gravity and friction through the centre, preventing skidding.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Centripetal acceleration is directed:
    A. Toward the centreB. Away from the centreC. Along velocityD. Opposite velocity
    Show answer

    Answer: Toward the centre

    It always points to the centre of the circle.

  2. MCQ If speed doubles in the same circle, centripetal force becomes:
    A. Four timesB. TwiceC. HalfD. Same
    Show answer

    Answer: Four times

    F ∝ v2.

  3. Short answer Find the angular velocity of a point on the equator (radius 6400 km) due to Earth's rotation.
    Show answer

    T = 86400 s, ω = 2π/T ≈ 7.27 × 10−5 rad/s.
