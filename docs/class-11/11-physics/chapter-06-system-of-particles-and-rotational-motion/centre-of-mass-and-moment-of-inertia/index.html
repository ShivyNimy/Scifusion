Centre of Mass and Moment of Inertia

Extended bodies are treated as systems of particles. The centre of mass moves as if all forces acted there, and rotational inertia depends on mass distribution.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The centre of mass is the mass-weighted average position of a system.
  • For two masses, the centre of mass divides the distance in inverse ratio to masses.
  • Torque τ = r × F; it produces angular acceleration.
  • Moment of inertia I = Σmr2; larger I means more resistance to rotation.
  • Angular momentum L = Iω is conserved when net torque is zero.

Worked examples

Example 1 Balancing on a finger

A ruler balances at its centre of mass. Adding a heavy mass at one end shifts the centre of mass toward that end.

Example 2 Figure skater spin

Pulling arms in reduces I, so ω increases to conserve angular momentum, and the skater spins faster.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Torque depends on:
    A. Force and perpendicular distanceB. Force onlyC. Mass onlyD. Velocity only
    Show answer

    Answer: Force and perpendicular distance

    τ = rF sin θ.

  2. MCQ When a skater pulls her arms in, her angular velocity:
    A. IncreasesB. DecreasesC. Stays sameD. Becomes zero
    Show answer

    Answer: Increases

    Reducing I increases ω to keep L constant.

  3. Short answer Why is it easier to open a door by pushing near the handle than near the hinge?
    Show answer

    The handle is farther from the hinge, so the same force produces a larger torque.
