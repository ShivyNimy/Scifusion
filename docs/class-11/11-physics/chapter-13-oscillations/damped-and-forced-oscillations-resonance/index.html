Damped and Forced Oscillations, Resonance

Real oscillations lose energy (damping). Forced oscillations can build up dramatically at resonance.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Damping force is proportional to velocity; amplitude decays exponentially.
  • Forced oscillations occur when an external periodic force drives the system.
  • Resonance occurs when the driving frequency matches the natural frequency.
  • At resonance the amplitude is maximum; it can damage structures.
  • Examples: musical instruments, tuning radios, soldiers breaking step on bridges.

Worked examples

Example 1 Soldiers on a bridge

If marching frequency matches the bridge's natural frequency, resonant swaying can build up, so soldiers break step on bridges.

Example 2 Tuning a radio

Adjusting the tuning circuit changes its natural frequency until it matches the station, giving resonant maximum response.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Resonance occurs when:
    A. Driving frequency equals natural frequencyB. Amplitude is zeroC. Damping is maximumD. Force is constant
    Show answer

    Answer: Driving frequency equals natural frequency

    At matching frequencies the amplitude peaks.

  2. MCQ Damping ______ the amplitude of oscillation.
    A. ReducesB. IncreasesC. DoublesD. Keeps constant
    Show answer

    Answer: Reduces

    Energy is dissipated, so amplitude decays.

  3. Short answer Why do soldiers break step while crossing a bridge?
    Show answer

    To avoid resonance with the bridge's natural frequency, which could cause dangerous oscillations.
