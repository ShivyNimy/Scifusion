Ionic Equilibrium: Acids, Bases and Buffers

Acids and bases ionise in water. pH, buffers and solubility product describe ionic equilibria.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Kw = [H+][OH−] = 10−14 at 25 °C.
  • pH = −log[H+]; pOH = −log[OH−]; pH + pOH = 14.
  • Strong acids dissociate completely; weak acids partially (Ka small).
  • A buffer resists pH change and works best near pKa.
  • Henderson-Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]).

Worked examples

Example 1 pH of a strong acid

0.01 M HCl gives [H+] = 10−2 M and pH = 2.

Example 2 Buffer in blood

The carbonic acid–bicarbonate buffer keeps blood pH near 7.4, protecting enzymes from pH swings.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The pH of a neutral solution at 25 °C is:
    A. 7B. 0C. 14D. 1
    Show answer

    Answer: 7

    [H+] = [OH−] = 10−7 M at 25 °C.

  2. MCQ A buffer works best when:
    A. pH ≈ pKaB. pH = 0C. pH = 14D. The acid is strong
    Show answer

    Answer: pH ≈ pKa

    Maximum buffering capacity is near the pKa.

  3. Short answer Calculate the pH of 0.001 M HCl.
    Show answer

    pH = −log(10−3) = 3.
