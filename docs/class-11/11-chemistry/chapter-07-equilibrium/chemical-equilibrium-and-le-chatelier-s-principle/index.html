Chemical Equilibrium and Le Chatelier's Principle

Reversible reactions reach equilibrium when forward and backward rates are equal. Le Chatelier's principle predicts the response to change.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • At equilibrium, concentrations are constant but reactions continue in both directions.
  • Kc = [products]n/[reactants]m for a balanced reaction.
  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δn for gases.
  • Le Chatelier: a system shifts to oppose the change imposed.
  • A catalyst speeds up both directions equally and does not change K.

Worked examples

Example 1 Haber process

N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 is exothermic and reduces gas moles, so high pressure and moderate temperature favour ammonia.

Example 2 Effect of removing product

Removing a product shifts the equilibrium to the right, increasing the forward reaction until equilibrium is re-established.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Adding a catalyst to an equilibrium:
    A. Does not change the equilibrium constantB. Increases KC. Decreases KD. Shifts the equilibrium right
    Show answer

    Answer: Does not change the equilibrium constant

    It speeds both forward and reverse reactions equally.

  2. MCQ Increasing pressure favours the side with:
    A. Fewer moles of gasB. More moles of gasC. Equal molesD. No gas
    Show answer

    Answer: Fewer moles of gas

    Le Chatelier's principle favours the smaller volume.

  3. Short answer How does increasing temperature affect an exothermic equilibrium?
    Show answer

    It shifts the equilibrium to the left, decreasing the product concentration.
