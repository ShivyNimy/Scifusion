Electronic Effects and Reaction Types

Organic reactions are classified by how bonds break and form. Inductive, resonance and hyperconjugation effects explain stability and reactivity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Homolytic cleavage gives radicals; heterolytic gives ions.
  • Inductive effect: electron withdrawal or donation along sigma bonds.
  • Resonance delocalises π electrons over several atoms.
  • Electrophiles are electron-poor; nucleophiles are electron-rich.
  • Reactions: substitution, addition, elimination and rearrangement.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why benzene is stable

Delocalised π electrons around the ring (resonance) give benzene extra stability, so it undergoes substitution rather than addition.

Example 2 Markovnikov's rule

In addition of HX to propene, hydrogen adds to the carbon with more hydrogens, giving the more stable carbocation intermediate.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A nucleophile is:
    A. Electron-richB. Electron-poorC. Always positiveD. A radical
    Show answer

    Answer: Electron-rich

    Nucleophiles donate electron pairs to electron-poor centres.

  2. MCQ Benzene undergoes mainly:
    A. SubstitutionB. AdditionC. EliminationD. Rearrangement
    Show answer

    Answer: Substitution

    Aromatic stability favours substitution over addition.

  3. Short answer Why does benzene not decolourise bromine water?
    Show answer

    Its delocalised π system is stable, so it does not undergo addition like alkenes.
