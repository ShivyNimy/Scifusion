First Law and Enthalpy

Thermodynamics studies energy changes. Enthalpy measures heat exchanged at constant pressure.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • First law: ΔU = q + w, where w is work done on the system.
  • Work done on a gas: w = −PΔV.
  • Enthalpy H = U + PV; ΔH = qp at constant pressure.
  • Exothermic reactions have ΔH < 0; endothermic have ΔH > 0.
  • Hess's law: enthalpy change is independent of the route.

Worked examples

Example 1 Hess's law calculation

If the enthalpy of formation of CO2 is −394 kJ/mol and of CO is −111 kJ/mol, the combustion of CO releases 283 kJ/mol.

Example 2 Enthalpy of a reaction

ΔH = ΣΔHf(products) − ΣΔHf(reactants) gives the heat of reaction from formation enthalpies.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ For an exothermic reaction, ΔH is:
    A. NegativeB. PositiveC. ZeroD. Undefined
    Show answer

    Answer: Negative

    Heat is released, so enthalpy decreases.

  2. MCQ Hess's law follows from:
    A. Conservation of energyB. Conservation of massC. Ideal gas lawD. Le Chatelier's principle
    Show answer

    Answer: Conservation of energy

    Enthalpy is a state function, so the route does not matter.

  3. Short answer State the first law of thermodynamics.
    Show answer

    Energy can be exchanged as heat or work, but the total energy of the universe is conserved: ΔU = q + w.
