Entropy, Gibbs Energy and Spontaneity

The second law introduces entropy. Gibbs energy combines enthalpy and entropy to predict spontaneity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Entropy S is a measure of disorder; ΔSuniverse > 0 for a spontaneous process.
  • Gibbs energy G = H − TS.
  • ΔG < 0 means spontaneous; ΔG = 0 means equilibrium; ΔG > 0 means non-spontaneous.
  • ΔG° = −RT ln K relates free energy to the equilibrium constant.
  • Exothermic reactions with increasing entropy are always spontaneous.

Worked examples

Example 1 Melting ice

Above 0 °C, ΔG for melting is negative because the entropy gain outweighs the enthalpy cost; below 0 °C the reverse is true.

Example 2 Thermodynamic versus kinetic control

A reaction may be thermodynamically favourable (ΔG < 0) but very slow; a catalyst affects only the rate.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A spontaneous reaction has:
    A. ΔG < 0B. ΔG > 0C. ΔG = 0D. ΔH > 0 always
    Show answer

    Answer: ΔG < 0

    Negative Gibbs energy means spontaneity.

  2. MCQ Entropy is a measure of:
    A. DisorderB. Heat contentC. PressureD. Volume
    Show answer

    Answer: Disorder

    Higher disorder means higher entropy.

  3. Short answer Why is the combustion of fuels spontaneous even though it releases heat?
    Show answer

    It is strongly exothermic and increases entropy of the surroundings, giving ΔG < 0.
