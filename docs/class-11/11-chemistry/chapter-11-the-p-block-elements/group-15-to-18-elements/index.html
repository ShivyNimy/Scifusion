Group 15 to 18 Elements

Nitrogen, oxygen, halogens and noble gases show trends in bonding, reactivity and physical state.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Group 15: N, P, As, Sb, Bi — nitrogen is a gas and phosphorus forms P4 molecules.
  • Group 16: O, S, Se, Te, Po — oxygen is a gas and sulphur forms S8 rings.
  • Halogens (17) are coloured, reactive non-metals; reactivity decreases down the group.
  • Noble gases are monatomic with very low reactivity; xenon forms some compounds.
  • Allotropes are important: oxygen/ozone, phosphorus white/red, sulphur rhombic/monoclinic.

Worked examples

Example 1 Ozone versus oxygen

Ozone (O3) is a pale blue, reactive gas that absorbs ultraviolet light; oxygen (O2) is essential for respiration.

Example 2 Halogen displacement

Chlorine displaces bromide from bromide solutions because chlorine is more reactive, showing the trend down group 17.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Which halogen is the most reactive?
    A. FluorineB. ChlorineC. BromineD. Iodine
    Show answer

    Answer: Fluorine

    Reactivity decreases down group 17.

  2. MCQ Phosphorus is stored in:
    A. WaterB. KeroseneC. AcidD. Alcohol
    Show answer

    Answer: Water

    White phosphorus reacts with air and is kept under water.

  3. Short answer Why are noble gases chemically inert?
    Show answer

    They have filled outer shells with very high ionisation energies and no tendency to gain electrons.
