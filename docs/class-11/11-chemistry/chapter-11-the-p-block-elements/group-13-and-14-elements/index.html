Class 11 Chemistry · Chapter 11 · Topic 11.1

Group 13 and 14 Elements

Boron and carbon groups show varied bonding and oxidation states, from covalent to metallic characters.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Group 13: B, Al, Ga, In, Tl; aluminium is the most important industrial metal.
  • Group 14: C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb; carbon shows catenation and silicon is the backbone of semiconductors.
  • Aluminium oxide is amphoteric; it reacts with acids and bases.
  • Carbon exists as diamond, graphite and fullerenes with very different properties.
  • Inert pair effect reduces the stability of the highest oxidation state down the group.

Worked examples

Example 1 Diamond versus graphite

Diamond is hard because each carbon is tetrahedrally bonded; graphite is slippery because layers are weakly held and conduct electricity along the layers.

Example 2 Silicon semiconductors

Silicon's four valence electrons form a stable crystal lattice whose conductivity can be controlled by doping.

  3. Short answer Why does graphite conduct electricity?

    Each carbon has a delocalised electron that is free to move along the layers.
