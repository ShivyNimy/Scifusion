Quantum Numbers and Orbitals

Quantum mechanics describes electrons in orbitals defined by four quantum numbers. Orbitals have shapes and energies.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Principal quantum number n gives the shell and energy level.
  • Azimuthal quantum number l gives the subshell (s, p, d, f).
  • Magnetic quantum number ml gives orbital orientation; spin ms is ±½.
  • Number of orbitals in a subshell = 2l + 1; each orbital holds 2 electrons.
  • Aufbau principle: electrons fill lower-energy orbitals first.

Worked examples

Example 1 Quantum numbers of a 2p electron

n = 2, l = 1, ml can be −1, 0 or +1, and ms is ±½. The 2p subshell has 3 orbitals and holds 6 electrons.

Example 2 Filling chromium

Chromium is [Ar]3d54s1 rather than 3d44s2 because half-filled d orbitals are extra stable.
