Electronic Configuration and Periodic Trends

Electron configurations explain the periodic table and trends such as atomic radius, ionisation energy and electronegativity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Electrons fill 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d … following the (n + l) rule.
  • Atomic radius decreases across a period and increases down a group.
  • Ionisation energy increases across a period and decreases down a group.
  • Electronegativity increases across a period and decreases down a group.
  • Hund's rule: degenerate orbitals fill singly first with parallel spins.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why sodium is more reactive than magnesium

Sodium loses its single 3s electron easily, while magnesium needs to lose two. Lower ionisation energy makes sodium more reactive.

Example 2 Why noble gases are inert

Their filled outer shells give high ionisation energies and no tendency to gain or lose electrons.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Across a period, atomic radius generally:
    A. DecreasesB. IncreasesC. Remains constantD. Doubles
    Show answer

    Answer: Decreases

    Increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer.

  2. MCQ Which has the largest atomic radius?
    A. PotassiumB. SodiumC. LithiumD. Hydrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: Potassium

    Radius increases down the group.

  3. Short answer Why does ionisation energy decrease down a group?
    Show answer

    The outer electron is farther from the nucleus and shielded by more inner shells, so it is easier to remove.
