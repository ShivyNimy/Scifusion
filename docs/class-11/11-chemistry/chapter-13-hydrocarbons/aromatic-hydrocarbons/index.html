Aromatic Hydrocarbons

Benzene and its derivatives are aromatic: planar rings with delocalised π electrons conferring extra stability.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Aromatic compounds follow Hückel's rule: 4n + 2 delocalised π electrons.
  • Benzene has six delocalised π electrons over a planar ring.
  • Electrophilic aromatic substitution is the main reaction type.
  • Substituents direct incoming groups ortho/para or meta.
  • Fossil fuels and incomplete combustion release aromatic pollutants such as benzene.

Worked examples

Example 1 Nitration of benzene

Benzene reacts with mixed acid (HNO3 + H2SO4) to give nitrobenzene, a classic electrophilic aromatic substitution.

Example 2 Methylbenzene and nitration

A methyl group directs incoming groups to ortho and para positions because it donates electron density by hyperconjugation.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Benzene has:
    A. 6 delocalised π electronsB. 4 π electronsC. 2 π electronsD. No π electrons
    Show answer

    Answer: 6 delocalised π electrons

    Six π electrons satisfy Hückel's rule for n = 1.

  2. MCQ A methyl group on benzene is:
    A. Ortho-para directingB. Meta directingC. DeactivatingD. Neutral
    Show answer

    Answer: Ortho-para directing

    Electron-donating groups direct ortho and para.

  3. Short answer Why is benzene unusually stable?
    Show answer

    Its delocalised π electrons are spread over the ring, lowering the energy of the molecule.
