Alkanes, Alkenes and Alkynes

Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen. Saturation determines their reactions and properties.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Alkanes are saturated and undergo substitution by free radicals.
  • Alkenes have C=C double bonds and undergo addition reactions.
  • Alkynes have C≡C triple bonds and also undergo addition; they are acidic at terminal carbons.
  • Markovnikov's rule predicts the major product in addition to alkenes.
  • Boiling points increase with chain length and branching lowers them.

Worked examples

Example 1 Bromine water test

Bromine water decolourises with an alkene or alkyne (addition) but not with an alkane, so it distinguishes unsaturated from saturated hydrocarbons.

Example 2 Combustion and soot

Alkanes burn with a clean flame when oxygen is plentiful; alkynes with more carbon content tend to produce smoky flames.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Alkanes react with halogens by:
    A. SubstitutionB. AdditionC. EliminationD. Oxidation only
    Show answer

    Answer: Substitution

    Free-radical substitution replaces a hydrogen.

  2. MCQ Which hydrocarbon decolourises bromine water?
    A. EtheneB. EthaneC. PropaneD. Methane
    Show answer

    Answer: Ethene

    Unsaturated hydrocarbons add bromine and decolourise the solution.

  3. Short answer Why do alkanes make good fuels?
    Show answer

    They are highly reduced and release a lot of heat on complete combustion.
