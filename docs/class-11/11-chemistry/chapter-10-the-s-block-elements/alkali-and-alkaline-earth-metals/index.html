Alkali and Alkaline Earth Metals

Group 1 and 2 metals are highly reactive, with trends in atomic size, ionisation energy and solubility.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Alkali metals (Li–Cs) react vigorously with water to give hydroxides and hydrogen.
  • Alkaline earth metals (Be–Ba) are less reactive but still reactive.
  • Reactivity increases down both groups as ionisation energy decreases.
  • Hydroxide solubility increases down group 2 for most compounds; sulphates decrease.
  • Flame colours help identify these metals.

Worked examples

Example 1 Sodium in water

2Na + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + H2 with vigorous fizzing and a yellow flame; potassium is even more violent.

Example 2 Solubility trend

Beryllium hydroxide is nearly insoluble while barium hydroxide is quite soluble, due to lattice and hydration enthalpy changes.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which group 1 metal reacts most vigorously with water?
    A. CaesiumB. LithiumC. SodiumD. Potassium
    Show answer

    Answer: Caesium

    Reactivity increases down the group.

  2. MCQ Magnesium burns in air with a:
    A. Brilliant white flameB. Green flameC. Blue flameD. No flame
    Show answer

    Answer: Brilliant white flame

    Magnesium gives a bright white light.

  3. Short answer Why are alkali metals stored in kerosene?
    Show answer

    They react rapidly with air and moisture, so kerosene keeps them dry and unreactive.
