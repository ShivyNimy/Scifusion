Class 11 Chemistry · Chapter 3 · Topic 3.2

Periodic Properties in Detail

Ionisation enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy and valence describe reactivity and bonding.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • First ionisation enthalpy is the energy to remove the most loosely bound electron.
  • Electron gain enthalpy is the energy change when an electron is added.
  • Group 17 elements have very negative electron gain enthalpies, making them reactive non-metals.
  • Valence is the number of electrons an atom gains, loses or shares to complete its octet.
  • Atomic and ionic radii vary with effective nuclear charge and shielding.

Worked examples

Example 1 Fluorine versus chlorine electron gain

Fluorine's small size causes electron-electron repulsion, so chlorine has a more negative electron gain enthalpy than fluorine.

Example 2 Radius of cation versus atom

Na+ is smaller than Na because losing an electron reduces repulsion and the same nuclear charge pulls the remaining electrons closer.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A cation is ______ than its parent atom.
    A. SmallerB. LargerC. Equal in sizeD. Twice as large
    Show answer

    Answer: Smaller

    Loss of electrons and reduced repulsion contract the ion.

  2. MCQ Which element has the highest first ionisation enthalpy?
    A. HeliumB. SodiumC. PotassiumD. Caesium
    Show answer

    Answer: Helium

    Helium's tightly held 1s electrons need the most energy to remove.

  3. Short answer Why is the second ionisation energy of sodium much greater than the first?
    Show answer

    Removing a second electron from a stable noble-gas configuration requires far more energy.
