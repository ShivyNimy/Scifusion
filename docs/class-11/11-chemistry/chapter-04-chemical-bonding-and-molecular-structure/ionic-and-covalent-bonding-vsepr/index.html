Ionic and Covalent Bonding, VSEPR

Atoms bond to achieve stable electron configurations. VSEPR theory predicts molecular shapes from electron-pair repulsion.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Ionic bonds form by complete transfer of electrons between metals and non-metals.
  • Covalent bonds form by sharing electrons, giving single, double or triple bonds.
  • VSEPR: electron pairs repel and arrange to minimise repulsion.
  • Lone pairs repel more strongly than bond pairs, compressing bond angles.
  • Common shapes: linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal pyramidal and bent.

Worked examples

Example 1 Water's bent shape

Oxygen has two lone pairs in water, which push the O–H bonds closer, making the bond angle about 104.5° instead of the tetrahedral 109.5°.

Example 2 Methane versus ammonia

Methane (CH4) is tetrahedral at 109.5°; ammonia (NH3) has one lone pair, so it is trigonal pyramidal with an angle near 107°.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The shape of a methane molecule is:
    A. TetrahedralB. Square planarC. LinearD. Trigonal planar
    Show answer

    Answer: Tetrahedral

    Four bonding pairs arrange tetrahedrally.

  2. MCQ Lone pair–lone pair repulsion is:
    A. Stronger than bond pair–bond pairB. WeakerC. EqualD. Zero
    Show answer

    Answer: Stronger than bond pair–bond pair

    Lone pairs occupy more space near the central atom.

  3. Short answer Predict the shape of BF3.
    Show answer

    Boron has three bonding pairs and no lone pairs, so they spread 120° apart.
