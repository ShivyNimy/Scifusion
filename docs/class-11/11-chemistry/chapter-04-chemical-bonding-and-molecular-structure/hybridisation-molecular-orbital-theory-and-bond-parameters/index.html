Hybridisation, Molecular Orbital Theory and Bond Parameters

Hybridisation explains geometry while molecular orbital theory explains magnetism and bond order. Bond length, order and energy describe bond strength.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • sp: linear (180°); sp2: trigonal planar (120°); sp3: tetrahedral (109.5°).
  • Bond order = (bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2.
  • Higher bond order means shorter, stronger bonds.
  • O2 is paramagnetic because of unpaired electrons in π* orbitals.
  • Dipole moment measures bond polarity and molecular polarity.

Worked examples

Example 1 Bond order of O2

MO theory gives bond order 2 and two unpaired electrons, correctly predicting that oxygen is paramagnetic.

Example 2 Why CO2 is nonpolar

Each C=O bond is polar, but the linear shape makes the two dipoles cancel, so the molecule has zero net dipole moment.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Hybridisation in ethene is:
    A. sp2B. sp3C. spD. sp3d
    Show answer

    Answer: sp2

    The C=C double bond uses sp2 hybrid orbitals with a π bond.

  2. MCQ Bond order of N2 is:
    A. 3B. 2C. 1D. 4
    Show answer

    Answer: 3

    The triple bond gives bond order 3.

  3. Short answer Why is the bond angle in water less than in ammonia?
    Show answer

    Water has two lone pairs versus one in ammonia, so lone-pair repulsion compresses the angle more.
