Water, Hydrogen Peroxide and Hard Water

Water has unusual properties due to hydrogen bonding. Hydrogen peroxide is a useful oxidant, and hard water contains calcium and magnesium ions.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Water is a polar molecule with strong hydrogen bonds, giving high boiling point and surface tension.
  • Hardness is temporary (bicarbonates) or permanent (sulphates and chlorides of Ca and Mg).
  • Water hardness is determined by EDTA titration.
  • H2O2 is a strong oxidant and decomposes to water and oxygen.
  • Water pollution and purification involve filtration, chlorination and aeration.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why ice floats

Hydrogen-bonded water molecules form an open lattice in ice, making it less dense than liquid water.

Example 2 Bleaching with H2O2

Hydrogen peroxide bleaches by oxidation and breaks down into water and oxygen, making it an environment-friendly bleach.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Temporary hardness of water is due to:
    A. Calcium and magnesium bicarbonatesB. ChloridesC. SulphatesD. Sodium salts
    Show answer

    Answer: Calcium and magnesium bicarbonates

    Bicarbonates can be removed by boiling.

  2. MCQ Hydrogen peroxide decomposes into:
    A. Water and oxygenB. Hydrogen and oxygenC. Water onlyD. Ozone
    Show answer

    Answer: Water and oxygen

    2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2.

  3. Short answer Why does water have a high boiling point?
    Show answer

    Extensive hydrogen bonding requires a lot of energy to break before boiling.
