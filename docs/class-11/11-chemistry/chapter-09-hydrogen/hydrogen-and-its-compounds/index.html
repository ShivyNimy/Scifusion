Hydrogen and Its Compounds

Hydrogen has unique chemistry with three isotopes. It forms hydrides and is important in energy and industry.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Isotopes: protium, deuterium and tritium.
  • Hydrides: ionic (NaH), covalent (HCl) and interstitial (with transition metals).
  • Hydrogen resembles alkali metals and halogens in some reactions, so its position is unique.
  • Heavy water (D2O) is used as a moderator in nuclear reactors.
  • Hydrogen is studied as a clean fuel, producing only water on combustion.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why hydrogen has two positions

It loses one electron like alkali metals and gains one like halogens, so it can be placed with either group.

Example 2 Hydrogen as fuel

Combustion gives 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O, releasing energy with no carbon dioxide, but storage and production costs are challenges.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Heavy water contains:
    A. DeuteriumB. Tritium onlyC. Protium onlyD. Helium
    Show answer

    Answer: Deuterium

    D2O contains deuterium instead of protium.

  2. MCQ Hydrogen can act as:
    A. Both an oxidising and reducing agentB. Only oxidisingC. Only reducingD. Neither
    Show answer

    Answer: Both an oxidising and reducing agent

    It can gain or lose one electron.

  3. Short answer Why is hydrogen called a clean fuel?
    Show answer

    It burns to give only water, with no carbon dioxide or soot.
