Redox Titrations and Electrode Potentials

Redox reactions power titrations and electrochemical cells. Electrode potentials rank oxidising and reducing strength.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • In a redox titration, a standard solution of known concentration oxidises or reduces the analyte.
  • Standard electrode potentials E° rank half-reactions by tendency to gain electrons.
  • A species with a higher E° is a stronger oxidising agent.
  • Cell potential E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode.
  • Redox reactions are central to corrosion and biological respiration.

Worked examples

Example 1 Permanganate titration

KMnO4 in acidic medium oxidises Fe2+ to Fe3+, and the purple colour fades at the end-point.

Example 2 Why zinc displaces copper

Zinc has a more negative reduction potential, so it is oxidised while copper ions are reduced and deposited.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ In a galvanic cell, oxidation occurs at the:
    A. AnodeB. CathodeC. Salt bridgeD. Voltmeter
    Show answer

    Answer: Anode

    Anodes are where oxidation happens in all electrochemical cells.

  2. MCQ A stronger oxidising agent has:
    A. Higher reduction potentialB. Lower reduction potentialC. Zero potentialD. Negative mass
    Show answer

    Answer: Higher reduction potential

    Higher E° means greater tendency to be reduced.

  3. Short answer Define a redox reaction and give one example.
    Show answer

    A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously, e.g. Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu.
