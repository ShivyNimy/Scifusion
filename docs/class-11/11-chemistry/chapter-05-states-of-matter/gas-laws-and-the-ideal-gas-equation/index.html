Gas Laws and the Ideal Gas Equation

Gas behaviour is described by Boyle's, Charles' and Avogadro's laws, combined in the ideal gas equation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Boyle's law: PV = constant at constant T.
  • Charles' law: V/T = constant at constant P.
  • Avogadro's law: V ∝ n at constant T and P.
  • Ideal gas equation: PV = nRT, with R = 8.314 J/mol·K = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K.
  • Dalton's law: total pressure is the sum of partial pressures.

Worked examples

Example 1 Gas volume change

1 mol of gas at 1 atm occupies 22.4 L at 273 K. At 546 K and the same pressure it occupies 44.8 L.

Example 2 Using the ideal gas law

2 mol at 300 K in 10 L: P = nRT/V = 2 × 0.0821 × 300/10 ≈ 4.93 atm.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Boyle's law states that at constant temperature:
    A. PV is constantB. V/T is constantC. P/T is constantD. n is constant
    Show answer

    Answer: PV is constant

    Pressure and volume are inversely proportional.

  2. MCQ The value of R in SI units is:
    A. 8.314 J/mol·KB. 0.0821 J/mol·KC. 1.987 J/mol·KD. 22.4 J/mol·K
    Show answer

    Answer: 8.314 J/mol·K

    R = 8.314 J/mol·K in SI units.

  3. Short answer A gas occupies 2 L at 1 atm. What volume at 2 atm, temperature unchanged?
    Show answer

    PV = constant, so V = 2 × 1/2 = 1 L.
