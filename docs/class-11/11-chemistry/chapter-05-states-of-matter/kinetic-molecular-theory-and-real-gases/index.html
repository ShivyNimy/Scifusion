Kinetic Molecular Theory and Real Gases

Kinetic theory links molecular motion to pressure and temperature; real gases deviate from ideal behaviour and are described by the van der Waals equation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Gas pressure arises from molecular collisions with walls.
  • Average kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature.
  • Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature due to molecular volume and attractions.
  • van der Waals equation: (P + an2/V2)(V − nb) = nRT.
  • Critical temperature is the temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied by pressure.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why helium liquefies last

Helium has very weak intermolecular forces, so its critical temperature is extremely low (about 5.2 K).

Example 2 Deviation of a real gas

Attractions reduce pressure and molecular volume reduces free space, so real gases deviate most when molecules are close together.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Real gases behave most ideally:
    A. At low pressure and high temperatureB. At high pressureC. At low temperatureD. At critical point
    Show answer

    Answer: At low pressure and high temperature

    Molecules are far apart and interact little.

  2. MCQ The van der Waals constant 'b' accounts for:
    A. Molecular volumeB. Molecular attractionC. Kinetic energyD. Temperature
    Show answer

    Answer: Molecular volume

    b corrects for the finite volume of molecules.

  3. Short answer Why does a real gas deviate from ideal behaviour at high pressure?
    Show answer

    Molecules are forced close together, so their finite volume and intermolecular attractions become significant.
