Stoichiometry and Limiting Reagents

Balanced equations give mole ratios. The limiting reagent determines how much product can form.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A balanced equation gives the ratio in which reactants combine and products form.
  • The limiting reagent is completely consumed first and controls the yield.
  • Theoretical yield is calculated from stoichiometry; actual yield is measured.
  • Percentage yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100.
  • Excess reagent remains after the reaction completes.

Worked examples

Example 1 Hydrogen and oxygen

2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O. If 4 mol H2 reacts with 1 mol O2, hydrogen is in excess and only 2 mol of water can form from the oxygen available.

Example 2 Percentage yield

If a reaction should give 20 g but gives 16 g, the yield is (16/20) × 100 = 80%.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The limiting reagent is the one that:
    A. Is completely consumed firstB. Is in excessC. Has the largest massD. Has the largest molar mass
    Show answer

    Answer: Is completely consumed first

    It limits the amount of product formed.

  2. MCQ Percentage yield is calculated as:
    A. Actual/theoretical × 100B. Theoretical/actual × 100C. Actual × theoreticalD. Actual + theoretical
    Show answer

    Answer: Actual/theoretical × 100

    It compares actual and theoretical yields.

  3. Short answer In 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O, how many moles of water form from 2 mol H2 and 2 mol O2?
    Show answer

    H2 is limiting; 2 mol H2 gives 2 mol H2O, leaving 1 mol O2 unused.
