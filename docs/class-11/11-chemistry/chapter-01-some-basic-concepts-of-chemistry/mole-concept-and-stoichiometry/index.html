Mole Concept and Stoichiometry

Chemistry counts particles in moles. The mole links mass, number of particles and volumes of gases, making quantitative reactions possible.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • 1 mole = 6.022 × 1023 particles (Avogadro's number).
  • Molar mass is the mass in grams of one mole of a substance.
  • Moles n = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • At STP, 1 mole of an ideal gas occupies 22.4 L.
  • Percentage composition = (mass of element ÷ molar mass) × 100.

Worked examples

Example 1 Moles of water

36 g of water contains 36/18 = 2 mol, i.e. 1.204 × 1024 molecules.

Example 2 Empirical formula

A compound with 40% C, 6.7% H and 53.3% O gives a mole ratio 1 : 2 : 1, so the empirical formula is CH2O.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Avogadro's number is:
    A. 6.022 × 1023B. 6.022 × 1022C. 3.14 × 1023D. 1.6 × 1019
    Show answer

    Answer: 6.022 × 1023

    One mole contains 6.022 × 1023 particles.

  2. MCQ Number of moles in 4 g of helium (M = 4 g/mol) is:
    A. 1B. 2C. 4D. 0.5
    Show answer

    Answer: 1

    n = 4/4 = 1 mol.

  3. Short answer Calculate the number of molecules in 9 g of water.
    Show answer

    Moles = 9/18 = 0.5; molecules = 0.5 × 6.022 × 1023 = 3.011 × 1023.
