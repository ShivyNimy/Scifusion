Class 11 Chemistry · Chapter 14 · Topic 14.1

Air Pollution and Its Effects

Pollutants from combustion and industry damage health, ecosystems and buildings.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Primary pollutants are emitted directly; secondary pollutants form in the atmosphere.
  • CO binds to haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen, causing suffocation.
  • SO2 and NOx cause acid rain, damaging soil, water and monuments.
  • Photochemical smog forms when nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons react in sunlight.
  • Particulates cause respiratory disease and reduce visibility.

Worked examples

Example 1 Photochemical smog

In sunny cities, NOx and volatile organic compounds react to form ozone and other oxidants, creating brown smog that irritates lungs.

Example 2 Taj Mahal corrosion

Sulphur dioxide from industry forms acid rain that attacks the marble, turning it yellow and pitted.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Carbon monoxide is dangerous because it:
    A. Binds haemoglobin more strongly than oxygenB. Smells badC. Is an allergenD. Blocks sunlight
    Show answer

    Answer: Binds haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen

    It reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood.

  2. MCQ Acid rain is caused mainly by:
    A. SO2 and NOxB. CO2 aloneC. OzoneD. Hydrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: SO2 and NOx

    These oxides form acids with water.

  3. Short answer Why is photochemical smog worse on sunny days?
    Show answer

    Sunlight drives the reactions that produce ozone and other oxidants.
