Lenses and Dispersion of Light

Lenses refract light. Convex lenses converge rays to form real images; concave lenses diverge. White light splits into colours in a prism.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A convex lens is thicker in the middle; it can form real, inverted images and is used as a magnifier.
  • A concave lens is thinner in the middle and always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image.
  • White light is made of seven colours: violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange and red (VIBGYOR).
  • A rainbow is formed when sunlight is refracted and dispersed by water droplets.
  • The image in a convex lens depends on the distance of the object relative to the focal length.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why a magnifying glass works

Holding a convex lens close to a small object makes the object appear bigger because the image is formed farther away and subtends a larger angle at the eye.

Example 2 The rainbow

Sunlight entering a raindrop slows and bends; different colours bend by different amounts, so they separate and reflect back to our eyes as a spectrum.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A convex lens is also called a:
    A. Converging lensB. Diverging lensC. Plane mirrorD. Prism
    Show answer

    Answer: Converging lens

    Convex lenses bring parallel rays to a focus.

  2. MCQ Which colour bends the most in a prism?
    A. VioletB. RedC. YellowD. Green
    Show answer

    Answer: Violet

    Violet has the shortest wavelength and deviates the most.

  3. Short answer What is dispersion of light?
    Show answer

    Splitting of white light into its component colours by refraction.
