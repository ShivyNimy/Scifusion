Reflection of Light

Light travels in straight lines and reflects from surfaces. Plane mirrors form virtual, erect and laterally inverted images.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • At reflection, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection; both are measured from the normal.
  • The image in a plane mirror is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
  • The image is virtual, erect and of the same size, and it is laterally inverted.
  • A real image can be caught on a screen; a virtual image cannot.
  • Concave mirrors converge light; convex mirrors diverge it.

Worked examples

Example 1 Mirror writing

In a mirror a clock's numbers appear reversed. This is lateral inversion: the left side of the object appears on the left side of the image from the mirror's point of view — swapping left and right.

Example 2 Concave mirrors in headlights

A bulb at the focus of a concave mirror sends light out as a parallel beam, giving a bright, far-reaching headlight beam.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The image in a plane mirror is:
    A. Virtual and erectB. Real and invertedC. Real and erectD. Magnified
    Show answer

    Answer: Virtual and erect

    Plane mirrors produce upright virtual images.

  2. MCQ A concave mirror is used in headlights to:
    A. Send a parallel beamB. Block lightC. Make a real image on the roadD. Disperse light
    Show answer

    Answer: Send a parallel beam

    Parallel beams travel far because they do not spread.

  3. Short answer State the laws of reflection.
    Show answer

    The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, the normal and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.
