Speed, Distance and Time

Speed tells how fast something moves. Distance-time graphs let us compare motions visually.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Speed = distance ÷ time; the SI unit is metre per second (m/s).
  • 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s; to convert m/s to km/h, multiply by 18/5.
  • Uniform motion covers equal distances in equal time, so the distance-time graph is a straight line.
  • Non-uniform motion gives a curved distance-time graph.
  • Odometer measures distance travelled; speedometer measures instantaneous speed.

Worked examples

Example 1 A train's speed

A train covers 120 km in 2 hours, so its average speed is 60 km/h. Converting: 60 × 5/18 = 16.7 m/s.

Example 2 Reading a distance-time graph

A straight, rising line means constant speed. A steeper line means a faster object. A flat horizontal line means the object is at rest.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The SI unit of speed is:
    A. m/sB. km/hC. mD. s
    Show answer

    Answer: m/s

    Metres per second is the SI unit.

  2. MCQ A straight-line distance-time graph shows:
    A. Uniform speedB. RestC. AccelerationD. Random motion
    Show answer

    Answer: Uniform speed

    A constant slope means constant speed.

  3. Short answer A car travels 90 km in 1.5 hours. What is its average speed?
    Show answer

    Speed = distance ÷ time = 90 ÷ 1.5 = 60 km/h.
