Measuring Time and Simple Pendulums

Time is measured with clocks; before clocks, the motion of pendulums, sundials and water clocks was used. A simple pendulum's period depends on its length.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • One oscillation of a pendulum is one complete to-and-fro swing.
  • The time period of a simple pendulum depends on its length and not on the mass of the bob.
  • A grandfather clock uses a pendulum to keep time.
  • The second is an SI unit of time; 1 minute = 60 s, 1 hour = 3600 s.
  • Pendulum clocks need level ground and careful calibration.

Worked examples

Example 1 Timing oscillations

Time 20 oscillations of a pendulum and divide by 20 to get the time period. Timing many oscillations reduces the effect of human reaction time.

Example 2 Why a longer pendulum swings slower

The time period increases with length: T is proportional to the square root of length. A longer pendulum takes longer for each swing.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The time period of a simple pendulum depends on its:
    A. LengthB. Mass of the bobC. ColourD. Material
    Show answer

    Answer: Length

    Length is the key factor; the mass of the bob has no effect.

  2. MCQ 20 oscillations take 40 s. The time period is:
    A. 2 sB. 4 sC. 0.5 sD. 20 s
    Show answer

    Answer: 2 s

    Time period = 40 ÷ 20 = 2 s.

  3. Short answer Why is a pendulum clock called a periodic motion device?
    Show answer

    Because its bob repeats its to-and-fro motion at equal intervals of time.
