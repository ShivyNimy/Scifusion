Respiration and Breathing

Respiration releases energy from food; breathing brings oxygen in and carbon dioxide out. It happens at the cellular level in all living things.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Aerobic respiration needs oxygen: glucose + oxygen gives carbon dioxide + water + energy.
  • Anaerobic respiration happens without oxygen and releases less energy; in muscles it produces lactic acid.
  • Breathing rate at rest is about 15–18 breaths per minute in adults.
  • The diaphragm and rib muscles drive inhalation and exhalation.
  • Alveoli in lungs are tiny air sacs with a rich blood supply for gas exchange.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why we pant after running

Muscles need more energy, so breathing and heart rate rise to supply oxygen and remove carbon dioxide. If oxygen is short, muscles respire anaerobically and lactic acid builds up, causing cramps.

Example 2 Yeast and fermentation

Yeast respires anaerobically to produce alcohol and carbon dioxide from sugar — the basis of bread rising and of wine making.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ The chief organs of breathing in humans are the:
    A. LungsB. GillsC. SpiraclesD. Leaves
    Show answer

    Answer: Lungs

    Lungs exchange gases in humans.

  2. MCQ Anaerobic respiration in muscles produces:
    A. Lactic acidB. OxygenC. GlucoseD. Carbon dioxide only
    Show answer

    Answer: Lactic acid

    Lactic acid accumulates and causes fatigue.

  3. Short answer Write the word equation for aerobic respiration.

    Glucose + oxygen gives carbon dioxide + water + energy.
