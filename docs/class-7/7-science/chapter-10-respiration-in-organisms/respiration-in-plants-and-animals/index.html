Respiration in Plants and Animals

Different organisms have different respiratory surfaces: gills in fish, spiracles in insects, skin in earthworms, and stomata in plants.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Fish draw water over gills and absorb dissolved oxygen.
  • Insects breathe through small pores called spiracles connected to tracheae.
  • Earthworms absorb oxygen through moist skin.
  • Frogs breathe through skin on land and lungs in air.
  • Plants respire through stomata and root hair surfaces; oxygen is used day and night.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why fish die out of water

Gills need water to keep their delicate filaments moist and supported. In air they collapse and the fish cannot obtain oxygen.

Example 2 Breathing in an insect

Air enters spiracles along the abdomen and travels through branching tubes directly to cells. No lungs are needed.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Fish respire with:
    A. GillsB. LungsC. SpiraclesD. Stomata
    Show answer

    Answer: Gills

    Gills extract dissolved oxygen from water.

  2. MCQ The openings through which insects breathe are called:
    A. SpiraclesB. GillsC. NostrilsD. Stomata
    Show answer

    Answer: Spiracles

    Spiracles open into the tracheal system.

  3. Short answer How does an earthworm breathe?
    Show answer

    Through its moist skin by diffusion of gases.
