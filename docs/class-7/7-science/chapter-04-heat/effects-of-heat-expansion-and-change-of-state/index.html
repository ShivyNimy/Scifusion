Effects of Heat: Expansion and Change of State

Heating causes expansion and changes of state: solid melts, liquid boils, vapour condenses and liquid freezes. Every change of state happens at a fixed temperature for a given substance.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Solids expand on heating; bimetallic strips bend and are used in thermostats and fire alarms.
  • Melting point of ice is 0 °C; boiling point of water is 100 °C at sea level.
  • During melting or boiling the temperature stays constant even though heat is being supplied — the heat is used to break bonds between particles (latent heat).
  • Cooling curves mirror heating curves and are used in refrigeration and air conditioning.
  • Water is unusual: it expands on freezing, which is why ice floats and pipes burst in winter.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why ice melts at a constant 0 °C

A thermometer in melting ice stays at 0 °C until all the ice has melted, although heat is being added. The extra energy goes into changing the arrangement of molecules, not into raising temperature.

Example 2 Bimetallic strip in a fire alarm

Brass expands more than iron for the same temperature rise, so a brass-iron strip curls when heated and closes an electric circuit that rings the alarm.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ During boiling, the temperature of water:
    A. Remains constantB. Increases steadilyC. DecreasesD. Doubles
    Show answer

    Answer: Remains constant

    Heat supplied goes into changing state, so temperature does not rise.

  2. MCQ Which liquid expands the most on heating (typical)?
    A. AlcoholB. WaterC. MercuryD. Glycerine
    Show answer

    Answer: Alcohol

    Alcohol expands more than mercury, which is why it is used in cold-climate thermometers.

  3. Short answer Why does ice float on water?
    Show answer

    Ice is less dense than water because water expands on freezing.
