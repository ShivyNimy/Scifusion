Temperature, Thermometers and Heat Transfer

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. Temperature is measured with a thermometer, and heat moves in three ways: conduction, convection and radiation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A clinical thermometer reads 35–42 °C and has a kink to hold the mercury reading.
  • A laboratory thermometer reads −10 to 110 °C and has no kink.
  • Conduction: heat travels through solids by direct contact (metal spoon in hot tea).
  • Convection: heat moves in liquids and gases by circulation of currents.
  • Radiation: heat travels without a medium, as from the Sun, and dark surfaces absorb more.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reading a clinical thermometer

Wash it, shake the mercury below 35 °C, place it under the tongue for a minute and read at eye level. Normal body temperature is about 37 °C.

Example 2 Why coastal breezes blow

Land heats faster than sea. Warm air over land rises and cooler sea air moves in, making a sea breeze in the day; the direction reverses at night as land cools faster.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Heat transfer through a metal rod is called:
    A. ConductionB. ConvectionC. RadiationD. Insulation
    Show answer

    Answer: Conduction

    Particles pass energy along by collision without moving as a whole.

  2. MCQ A clinical thermometer usually reads from:
    A. 35 °C to 42 °CB. 0 °C to 100 °CC. −10 °C to 110 °CD. 30 °C to 60 °C
    Show answer

    Answer: 35 °C to 42 °C

    It is designed for body temperature range.

  3. Short answer Why do we wear light-coloured clothes in summer?
    Show answer

    Light colours reflect most radiation and absorb less heat.
