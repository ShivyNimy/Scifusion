Silk: The Life of the Silkworm

Silk is produced by the larvae of silkworm moths. Rearing silkworms to obtain silk is called sericulture, and each cocoon can yield a long continuous filament.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A female silk moth lays hundreds of eggs that hatch into larvae (caterpillars).
  • Larvae feed on mulberry leaves and grow through moults, then spin a cocoon of silk.
  • Cocoons are treated in hot water to remove the gum and unwind the filament — this kills the pupa, which raises ethical questions.
  • Silk is strong, lustrous, elastic and absorbs moisture well.
  • Ahimsa silk is obtained without killing the silkworm, though the filament is shorter.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why cocoons are boiled

The silk thread is glued together by sericin. Hot water softens the gum so filaments can be unwound without breaking. The same process prevents the moth from emerging, which is why some producers use ahimsa silk methods instead.

Example 2 Mulberry and the silk cycle

Butterflies and moths have a four-stage life cycle: egg, larva, pupa, adult. Silk is harvested at the pupa stage, so sericulture depends on healthy mulberry plants and careful temperature control.

  1. MCQ Sericulture is the rearing of:
    A. SilkwormsB. SheepC. HoneybeesD. Fish
    Show answer

    Answer: Silkworms

    Silk farming is called sericulture.

  2. MCQ Silkworms feed mainly on:
    A. Mulberry leavesB. GrassC. RiceD. Wheat
    Show answer

    Answer: Mulberry leaves

    Mulberry leaves are the preferred food of Bombyx mori larvae.

  3. Short answer Why is silk considered a strong fibre?
    Show answer

    Its long continuous filaments, twisted together, make a strong, flexible thread.
