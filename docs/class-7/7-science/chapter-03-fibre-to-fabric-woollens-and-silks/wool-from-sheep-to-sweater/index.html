Wool: From Sheep to Sweater

Wool comes from the fleece of sheep and goats. Its curly, crimped fibres trap air, making it warm.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Shearing removes the fleece; scouring washes it; sorting separates different qualities of fibre.
  • Wool from the sheep's undercoat is soft and fine; outer hair is coarse and used for carpets.
  • Wool is a poor conductor of heat, so it prevents body heat from escaping.
  • Woollen clothes are best in winter because they insulate; silk and cotton suit summer.
  • Angora wool comes from the Angora rabbit, mohair from the Angora goat, cashmere from the Kashmir goat.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why wool feels warm but not hot

Wool traps air between its crimped fibres. Air conducts heat poorly, so body heat stays in. Wool does not generate heat itself; it slows heat loss.

Example 2 From fleece to yarn

After shearing and scouring, fibres are carded to straighten them, spun into yarn, dyed and knitted or woven into sweaters. Each step improves fineness and strength.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The fleece of a sheep is removed by:
    A. ShearingB. SortingC. ScouringD. Dyeing
    Show answer

    Answer: Shearing

    Shearing is the removal of fleece.

  2. MCQ Wool is warm because it:
    A. Traps air between fibresB. Attracts sunlightC. Produces heatD. Contains water
    Show answer

    Answer: Traps air between fibres

    Trapped air insulates, slowing heat loss.

  3. Short answer Name two animals other than sheep that give wool.
    Show answer

    Goats (mohair, cashmere) and Angora rabbits.
