Conservation of Forests and Wildlife

Deforestation destroys habitats, disrupts water cycles and increases carbon dioxide. Conservation protects ecosystems and the people who depend on them.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Deforestation causes habitat loss, soil erosion, floods and loss of biodiversity.
  • Afforestation, protection of wildlife sanctuaries and community forest management help.
  • Protected areas include national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves.
  • Sacred groves are forests protected by local communities.
  • Reducing paper and wood use and recycling reduce pressure on forests.

Worked examples

Example 1 The chipko movement

Villagers in the Himalayan hills hugged trees to prevent them from being felled, showing that forest conservation can begin with communities.

Example 2 Why biodiversity matters

Each species has a role in the food web. Losing a predator can cause prey numbers to rise and damage vegetation, destabilising the whole forest.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ What is deforestation?
    A. Clearing of forests by cutting treesB. Growing new forestsC. Protecting forestsD. A type of soil
    Show answer

    Answer: Clearing of forests by cutting trees

    Deforestation is the large-scale removal of forest.

  2. MCQ A protected forest area for wildlife is called:
    A. A wildlife sanctuaryB. A pastureC. A plantationD. A garden
    Show answer

    Answer: A wildlife sanctuary

    Sanctuaries protect animals in their natural habitat.

  3. Short answer State two consequences of deforestation.
    Show answer

    Loss of habitat and increased soil erosion and floods.
