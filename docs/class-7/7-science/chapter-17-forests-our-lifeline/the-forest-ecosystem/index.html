The Forest Ecosystem

A forest is a community of plants, animals, microbes and the physical environment. Its layers interact to recycle nutrients, hold water and support life.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Forest layers: canopy, understorey, shrubs, herbs and forest floor.
  • Decomposers break down leaf litter into humus, returning nutrients to the soil.
  • Forests recharge groundwater, prevent floods and soil erosion and moderate climate.
  • Forest products include timber, fuelwood, honey, gum, resins, medicines and rubber.
  • The forest floor is dark and moist because very little light reaches it.

Worked examples

Example 1 Leaf litter to soil

Leaves fall, decompose with the help of microorganisms and insects, and form humus, which makes the soil fertile. Nutrients return to the roots of living trees.

Example 2 Forests and floods

A forest canopy slows rainfall and roots hold the soil, so water seeps into the ground instead of rushing down slopes and flooding rivers.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Nutrients of a forest are returned to the soil by:
    A. DecomposersB. HerbivoresC. SunlightD. Wind
    Show answer

    Answer: Decomposers

    Microorganisms break down litter into humus.

  2. MCQ Which layer of a forest receives the least sunlight?
    A. Forest floorB. CanopyC. UnderstoreyD. Shrub layer
    Show answer

    Answer: Forest floor

    The dense canopy blocks light from the floor.

  3. Short answer Give two ways forests help the environment.
    Show answer

    They recharge groundwater and prevent soil erosion and floods.
