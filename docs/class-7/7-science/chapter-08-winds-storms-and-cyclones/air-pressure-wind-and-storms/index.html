Air Pressure, Wind and Storms

Air has weight and exerts pressure. Wind is air moving from high pressure to low pressure, and storms are strong winds produced by big pressure differences.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The atmosphere exerts pressure in all directions; a balloon or the Magdeburg hemispheres show air pressure.
  • Warm air rises and cooler air moves in, creating convection currents and wind.
  • A cyclone forms over warm oceans when low pressure draws in moist air that rises and condenses.
  • Thunderstorms bring lightning, thunder and heavy rain; lightning is an electric discharge.
  • Cyclone warnings use satellites and radar to track storms and warn coastal areas.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why the balloon blows up

Blowing into a balloon pushes air inside; the air pressure increases and stretches the rubber. Puncture it and the higher-pressure air rushes out.

Example 2 How a cyclone forms

Warm ocean water evaporates; moist air rises and cools, forming clouds. The low pressure at the centre pulls in more air, and Earth's rotation gives the storm its spiral shape.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Wind blows from:
    A. High pressure to low pressureB. Low pressure to high pressureC. Cold to hot alwaysD. Land to sea always
    Show answer

    Answer: High pressure to low pressure

    Pressure differences drive air from high to low pressure.

  2. MCQ Cyclones form mainly over:
    A. Warm oceansB. Cold desertsC. MountainsD. Cities
    Show answer

    Answer: Warm oceans

    Warm, moist ocean air is the fuel for a cyclone.

  3. Short answer What is lightning?
    Show answer

    A huge electric spark caused by charge separation in thunderclouds.
