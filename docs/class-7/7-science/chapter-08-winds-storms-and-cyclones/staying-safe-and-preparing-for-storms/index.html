Staying Safe and Preparing for Storms

Understanding storms saves lives. Safety rules differ for lightning, thunderstorms, cyclones and floods.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • During lightning, stay indoors or inside a car; avoid trees, open fields and metal objects.
  • Cyclone warnings should be heeded: move to shelters and store water, food and medicines.
  • Storm surge flooding follows a cyclone on the coast.
  • Cyclone names are assigned by a regional committee from a list of names.
  • Doppler radar and satellites improve early warning and reduce casualties.

Worked examples

Example 1 Lightning safety at a picnic

If thunder follows a flash within seconds, lightning is close. Move into a building or a car. Never shelter under an isolated tree or hold a metal umbrella.

Example 2 Preparing for a cyclone

Fishermen return to harbour, families move valuables to safe heights, store drinking water and food, and follow radio or phone alerts until the all-clear.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ During lightning it is safest to:
    A. Go indoorsB. Stand under a treeC. Stand in waterD. Hold a metal pole
    Show answer

    Answer: Go indoors

    Buildings and closed cars conduct current safely around you.

  2. MCQ A storm surge is:
    A. Rise in sea level along the coastB. A river floodC. An earthquakeD. A heat wave
    Show answer

    Answer: Rise in sea level along the coast

    Low pressure and strong winds push sea water onto the coast.

  3. Short answer Give two safety measures during a thunderstorm.
    Show answer

    Stay indoors and avoid trees, water and metal objects.
