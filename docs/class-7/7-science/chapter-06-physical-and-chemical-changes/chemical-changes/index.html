Chemical Changes

A chemical change produces a new substance with different properties and is usually irreversible.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Burning, rusting, digesting food, curdling milk and electrolysis are chemical changes.
  • Signs include colour change, gas release, heat or light, smell and precipitate formation.
  • Rusting needs both oxygen and moisture; painting or oiling prevents it.
  • Chemical changes are used in cooking, metallurgy and medicine.

Worked examples

Example 1 Rusting iron

An iron nail left in moist air turns reddish-brown. Iron reacts with oxygen and water to form hydrated iron(III) oxide. Keeping it dry or coated stops the change.

Example 2 Burning magnesium

A magnesium ribbon burns with a bright white light and leaves a white powder, magnesium oxide — a new substance, so it is a chemical change.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Rusting requires:
    A. Oxygen and moistureB. Only nitrogenC. Only sunlightD. Only carbon dioxide
    Show answer

    Answer: Oxygen and moisture

    Both water and oxygen are needed for rust formation.

  2. MCQ Which evidence suggests a chemical change?
    A. Formation of a new substanceB. Change of shapeC. Change of stateD. Dissolving
    Show answer

    Answer: Formation of a new substance

    A new substance with new properties indicates a chemical change.

  3. Short answer Why is burning considered a chemical change?
    Show answer

    It produces new substances (ash, smoke, gases) that cannot be changed back.
