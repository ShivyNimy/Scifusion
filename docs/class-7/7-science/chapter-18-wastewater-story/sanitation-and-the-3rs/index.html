Sanitation and the 3Rs

Better sanitation requires good drains, toilets and habits. Reducing, reusing and recycling water and waste protects rivers and groundwater.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Drains should be covered and cleaned regularly to stop breeding of mosquitoes and flies.
  • Toilets help prevent open defecation and the spread of disease.
  • Rainwater harvesting reduces demand on rivers and groundwater.
  • Using less detergent and avoiding chemicals in drains prevents water pollution.
  • Wastewater can be recycled for gardening and industry after treatment.

Worked examples

Example 1 A clean school

Covered drains, clean toilets, handwashing with soap and bins for wet and dry waste keep the school healthy and prevent water pollution.

Example 2 Reusing greywater

Water from washing clothes or vegetables can be poured on plants after the soap is diluted, saving fresh water and reducing wastewater.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which practice prevents water pollution?
    A. Treating sewage before dischargeB. Dumping plastic in riversC. Washing clothes in pondsD. Open defecation
    Show answer

    Answer: Treating sewage before discharge

    Treatment removes or kills harmful substances before release.

  2. MCQ Open drains are unhealthy mainly because they:
    A. Breed mosquitoes and spread diseaseB. Look badC. Smell dryD. Use water
    Show answer

    Answer: Breed mosquitoes and spread disease

    Standing water becomes a breeding ground for disease vectors.

  3. Short answer Why should chemical waste not be poured into drains?
    Show answer

    It poisons water, kills useful microbes and can contaminate groundwater.
