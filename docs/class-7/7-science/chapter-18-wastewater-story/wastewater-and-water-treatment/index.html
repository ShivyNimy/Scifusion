Wastewater and Water Treatment

Wastewater from homes, industries and farms contains harmful substances. Treating it makes it safe enough to return to rivers or reuse.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Domestic sewage contains organic waste, detergents, soaps and human excreta.
  • Treatment steps: bar screening, grit removal, sedimentation, aeration (bacterial digestion), filtration and disinfection.
  • Aeration helps bacteria break down organic matter in activated sludge.
  • Sludge is used as fertiliser after treatment; the treated water can be used for irrigation.
  • Untreated sewage causes waterborne diseases such as cholera and typhoid.

Worked examples

Example 1 Following the water

Water from the kitchen sink flows into a drain, then to a sewage treatment plant. Screening removes solids, bacteria digest organic matter, and chlorine kills remaining germs before the water is released.

Example 2 Why do we boil drinking water

Boiling kills disease-causing microorganisms. Filtering and chlorination are other ways to make water safe.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Wastewater treatment plants use ______ to break down organic matter.
    A. BacteriaB. Oxygen gas onlyC. HeatD. Salt
    Show answer

    Answer: Bacteria

    Microorganisms digest organic waste in the aeration tank.

  2. MCQ Untreated sewage in drinking water causes:
    A. Waterborne diseasesB. Clean waterC. More oxygenD. More fish
    Show answer

    Answer: Waterborne diseases

    Cholera, typhoid and diarrhoea spread through contaminated water.

  3. Short answer List any three steps in wastewater treatment.
    Show answer

    Screening, sedimentation and aeration (followed by filtration and disinfection).
