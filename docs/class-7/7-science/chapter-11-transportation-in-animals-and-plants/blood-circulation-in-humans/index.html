Blood Circulation in Humans

Blood carries oxygen, nutrients and wastes. The heart pumps blood through arteries, capillaries and veins, and the circulatory system links all organs.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The heart has four chambers: two atria and two ventricles.
  • Blood flows: body -> right atrium -> right ventricle -> lungs -> left atrium -> left ventricle -> body.
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins return blood; capillaries allow exchange with cells.
  • Blood components: plasma, red blood cells (haemoglobin carries oxygen), white blood cells (defence) and platelets (clotting).
  • The average heart rate of an adult is about 72 beats per minute.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reading a pulse

Place two fingers on the wrist below the thumb. Each pulse is one heartbeat. After running, the pulse rate rises because muscles need more oxygen.

Example 2 Why veins have valves

Blood in veins is at low pressure; valves prevent backflow and ensure blood returns to the heart, especially from the legs.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Arteries carry blood:
    A. Away from the heartB. Towards the heartC. Only in lungsD. Only in legs
    Show answer

    Answer: Away from the heart

    Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart (except the pulmonary artery).

  2. MCQ Which blood cells carry oxygen?
    A. Red blood cellsB. White blood cellsC. PlateletsD. Plasma
    Show answer

    Answer: Red blood cells

    Haemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen.

  3. Short answer Name the four chambers of the human heart.
    Show answer

    Right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium and left ventricle.
