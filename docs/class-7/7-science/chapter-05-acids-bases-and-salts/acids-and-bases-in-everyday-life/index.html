Acids and Bases in Everyday Life

Acids taste sour and turn blue litmus red; bases taste bitter and feel soapy, and turn red litmus blue. Indicators tell us whether a substance is acidic or basic.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Common acids: lemon (citric), curd (lactic), vinegar (acetic), stomach (hydrochloric), battery (sulphuric).
  • Common bases: soap, lime water (calcium hydroxide), baking soda (sodium bicarbonate), milk of magnesia.
  • Litmus, turmeric, china rose and phenolphthalein are indicators.
  • Acids and bases react to neutralise each other, forming salt and water.
  • Farmers treat acidic soil with lime and basic soil with organic matter to adjust pH.

Worked examples

Example 1 Turmeric as an indicator

A paste of turmeric turns red-brown in soap solution (a base) and stays yellow in lemon juice. This is why a curry stain is hard to remove with soap but fades in acid.

Example 2 Neutralising a bee sting

A bee sting contains an acid, so rubbing baking soda (a mild base) soothes it. A wasp sting is basic, so vinegar (an acid) is used instead.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Litmus paper turns ______ in an acid.
    A. RedB. BlueC. GreenD. Yellow
    Show answer

    Answer: Red

    Acids turn blue litmus red.

  2. MCQ Which of these is a base?
    A. SoapB. Lemon juiceC. VinegarD. Curd
    Show answer

    Answer: Soap

    Soap is basic and feels soapy.

  3. Short answer What is neutralisation? Give one example.
    Show answer

    A reaction between an acid and a base that forms salt and water; for example, hydrochloric acid plus sodium hydroxide gives sodium chloride and water.
