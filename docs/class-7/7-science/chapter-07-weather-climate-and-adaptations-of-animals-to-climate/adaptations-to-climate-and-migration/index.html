Adaptations to Climate and Migration

Animals are adapted to their climate through body features and behaviour. When conditions become too harsh, some animals migrate.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Polar bears: thick fur, a layer of fat, white camouflage and strong swimming ability.
  • Penguins: dense feathers, huddling and fat for insulation.
  • Desert animals: camels store fat; many are nocturnal and conserve water.
  • Migration: Siberian cranes fly to India, and birds move between breeding and feeding grounds.
  • Hibernation: bears and frogs slow their metabolism to survive cold or dry spells.

Worked examples

Example 1 Polar bear adaptations

White fur blends with snow, a thick fat layer insulates, and a streamlined body helps swimming. Big paws spread weight on snow and ice.

Example 2 Siberian cranes and Bharatpur

Each winter the cranes fly thousands of kilometres to wetlands such as Bharatpur in India to escape the frozen Siberian winter. Migration is an adaptation of behaviour.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Animals that sleep through winter are said to:
    A. HibernateB. MigrateC. AcclimatiseD. Adapt
    Show answer

    Answer: Hibernate

    Hibernation lowers body activity to save energy during cold or food shortage.

  2. MCQ Thick fur and fat in polar bears help mainly with:
    A. InsulationB. CamouflageC. SwimmingD. Speed
    Show answer

    Answer: Insulation

    Fat and fur reduce heat loss in extreme cold.

  3. Short answer Why do some birds migrate?
    Show answer

    To escape harsh weather and find food and breeding grounds.
