Weather and Climate

Weather is the day-to-day condition of the atmosphere; climate is the average pattern of weather over many years. Elements include temperature, rainfall, humidity and wind.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Weather changes hour to hour; climate is a long-term average.
  • The Sun is the main source of energy that drives weather and climate.
  • The Indian Meteorological Department records and forecasts weather.
  • Coastal areas have a moderate climate because water heats and cools slowly.
  • Deserts have extremes of temperature; polar regions are cold and dry.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why the desert is hot by day and cold at night

Dry, sandy ground has little water to store heat. It heats fast in sunlight and radiates heat away fast after sunset, so the nights are very cold.

Example 2 Mumbai versus Delhi

Mumbai, on the coast, has a narrow temperature range because the sea moderates it. Delhi, inland, swings from very hot summers to cold winters.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Climate refers to:
    A. Average weather over many yearsB. Weather todayC. Temperature onlyD. Rainfall today
    Show answer

    Answer: Average weather over many years

    Climate is the long-term pattern of weather.

  2. MCQ Which place is likely to have a moderate climate?
    A. A coastal cityB. A desertC. A mountain peakD. The centre of a continent
    Show answer

    Answer: A coastal city

    Coastal areas are moderated by the sea.

  3. Short answer Differentiate weather and climate.
    Show answer

    Weather is short-term atmospheric conditions; climate is the long-term average.
