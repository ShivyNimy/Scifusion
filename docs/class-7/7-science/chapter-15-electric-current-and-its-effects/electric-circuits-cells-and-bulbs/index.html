Electric Circuits, Cells and Bulbs

A closed circuit allows current to flow. Cells supply energy, switches control the circuit and bulbs give light.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A cell has positive and negative terminals; current flows from + to − in the external circuit.
  • A bulb contains a tungsten filament with high resistance; it becomes white-hot and glows.
  • More cells in series increase the voltage and make a bulb glow brighter.
  • Symbols: cell, battery, bulb, switch, wire and ammeter.
  • Circuit diagrams are drawn with standard symbols and must be closed for current to flow.

Worked examples

Example 1 Brighter with two cells

Connect two cells in series with the same polarity and the bulb glows brighter, because the total voltage is greater and more current flows.

Example 2 Why a bulb filament is thin

A thin filament has high resistance, so it heats up strongly and emits light. A thick wire would heat too little to glow.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Current flows from ______ terminal of a cell in the external circuit.
    A. PositiveB. NegativeC. BothD. Neither
    Show answer

    Answer: Positive

    Conventional current flows from + to − outside the cell.

  2. MCQ The glowing wire in a bulb is made of:
    A. TungstenB. CopperC. IronD. Plastic
    Show answer

    Answer: Tungsten

    Tungsten has a very high melting point.

  3. Short answer Draw a circuit with a cell, a bulb and a switch.
    Show answer

    A rectangular loop of wire containing the cell symbol, the bulb symbol and a switch symbol, all connected in series.
