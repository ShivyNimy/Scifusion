Heating and Magnetic Effects of Current

Electric current produces heat and magnetism. These effects are the basis of heaters, fuses, electromagnets and electric bells.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The heating effect depends on current, resistance and time: more current means more heat.
  • A fuse is a thin wire that melts if current is too high, breaking the circuit.
  • A filament bulb glows because a thin tungsten filament becomes very hot.
  • A current-carrying coil behaves like a magnet; an electromagnet uses this effect.
  • The magnetic effect is used in electric bells, motors, loudspeakers and cranes.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why a fuse saves appliances

If a short circuit or overload occurs, a very large current flows. The fuse wire melts first and breaks the circuit, protecting the rest of the wiring.

Example 2 The electric bell

A current in a coil magnetises a soft-iron core, pulling the hammer. As the hammer moves it breaks the circuit, the electromagnet releases, a spring pulls the hammer back and the cycle repeats, producing the ring.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The heating effect of current is used in:
    A. Electric heaters and ironsB. Electric motorsC. LoudspeakersD. Electromagnets
    Show answer

    Answer: Electric heaters and irons

    Heaters and irons convert electrical energy into heat.

  2. MCQ An electromagnet is made by winding a coil around:
    A. A soft iron coreB. A wooden blockC. A copper wireD. A glass rod
    Show answer

    Answer: A soft iron core

    Soft iron becomes strongly magnetic while current flows.

  3. Short answer What is the purpose of a fuse?
    Show answer

    It melts and breaks the circuit when current is too high, protecting appliances and wiring.
