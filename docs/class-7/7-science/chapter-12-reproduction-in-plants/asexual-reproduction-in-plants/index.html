Asexual Reproduction in Plants

Some plants reproduce without seeds from a single parent, producing genetically identical offspring. Vegetative propagation, budding and fragmentation are common.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Vegetative propagation: potato (eyes), ginger (rhizome), sugarcane (stem cuttings), rose (stem cuttings).
  • Bryophyllum leaves produce plantlets on their margins.
  • Yeast reproduces by budding; Spirogyra by fragmentation.
  • Asexual reproduction is faster and preserves desirable traits but reduces variation.
  • Grafting, layering and tissue culture are artificial methods used by farmers and gardeners.

Worked examples

Example 1 Potato eyes

Each 'eye' on a potato is a bud. Cut a potato with an eye and plant it; a new plant grows from the bud, producing genetically identical potatoes.

Example 2 Tissue culture

A tiny piece of plant tissue is grown on a nutrient medium under sterile conditions to produce many identical plantlets quickly — useful for orchards and endangered plants.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which plant reproduces from leaf buds?
    A. BryophyllumB. PotatoC. SugarcaneD. Rose
    Show answer

    Answer: Bryophyllum

    Bryophyllum leaves develop tiny plantlets at the margin.

  2. MCQ Fragmentation is seen in:
    A. SpirogyraB. MangoC. WheatD. Moss
    Show answer

    Answer: Spirogyra

    The filaments of Spirogyra break into pieces, each growing into a new plant.

  3. Short answer What is vegetative propagation? Give one example.
    Show answer

    Growing a new plant from a vegetative part such as stem, root or leaf; for example, potato from a tuber.
