Soil Formation and Composition

Soil forms slowly from weathered rock mixed with organic matter. It contains mineral particles, humus, water, air and living organisms.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Soil types: sandy (large particles, drains fast), clayey (small particles, holds water) and loamy (a mix, best for crops).
  • Humus is dark decomposed organic matter that makes soil fertile.
  • Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by wind or water; terracing and cover crops reduce it.
  • About 45% of soil volume is mineral matter, 25% water, 25% air and a small percentage organic matter.
  • Loamy soil is best for most plants because it holds both water and air.

Worked examples

Example 1 Testing soil texture

Rub moist soil between your fingers. Sandy soil feels gritty; clayey soil feels smooth and sticky; loamy soil feels crumbly and forms a weak ball.

Example 2 Erosion on a bare slope

On a slope without plants, rainwater runs fast and carries topsoil away. Planting grass or building terraces slows the water and holds the soil.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which soil is best for growing most crops?
    A. LoamyB. SandyC. ClayeyD. Gravel
    Show answer

    Answer: Loamy

    Loam balances drainage and water retention.

  2. MCQ Humus is:
    A. Decomposed organic matterB. Weathered rockC. Pure clayD. A mineral
    Show answer

    Answer: Decomposed organic matter

    Dead plants and animals decompose to form humus.

  3. Short answer How can soil erosion be reduced?
    Show answer

    Contour ploughing, terracing, cover crops, planting trees and avoiding overgrazing.
