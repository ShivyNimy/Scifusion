Soil as a Living System and Soil Pollution

Soil teems with life: bacteria, fungi, worms and insects break down matter and recycle nutrients. Pollution destroys this living system.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Earthworms aerate the soil and mix organic matter; they are indicators of healthy soil.
  • Rhizobium and other microbes fix nitrogen and decompose waste.
  • Pesticides, plastics and industrial waste pollute soil and enter the food chain.
  • Removing vegetation causes desertification; sandy soils become arid and unproductive.
  • Crop rotation and organic farming maintain soil fertility.

Worked examples

Example 1 The role of earthworms

Earthworms pull dead leaves underground, digest them and leave nutrient-rich castings. Their burrows let air and water into the soil, which is why gardeners value them.

Example 2 Plastic in soil

Polythene bags block air and water movement, and roots cannot penetrate them. They remain in soil for centuries, reducing fertility.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Which organisms improve soil fertility by decomposing waste?
    A. MicroorganismsB. MammalsC. BirdsD. Fish
    Show answer

    Answer: Microorganisms

    Bacteria and fungi break down organic matter into nutrients.

  2. MCQ Soil pollution may be caused by:
    A. Excess pesticides and plasticB. RainC. SunlightD. Air movement
    Show answer

    Answer: Excess pesticides and plastic

    Chemicals and non-biodegradable waste contaminate the soil.

  3. Short answer Why is soil considered a living resource?
    Show answer

    It contains many living organisms that decompose waste and cycle nutrients.
