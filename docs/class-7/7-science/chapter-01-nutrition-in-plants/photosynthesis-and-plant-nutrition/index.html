Photosynthesis and Plant Nutrition

Green plants make their own food by photosynthesis, using carbon dioxide, water and sunlight, with chlorophyll capturing the light energy.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Photosynthesis happens mainly in leaves, in the chloroplasts that contain chlorophyll.
  • Raw materials: carbon dioxide through stomata, water and minerals through roots, sunlight as energy.
  • Products: glucose (food) and oxygen (released).
  • Stomata are tiny pores, usually more numerous on the lower surface of a leaf.
  • The overall equation is 6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2 in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.

Worked examples

Example 1 Testing a leaf for starch

Boil the leaf in water, then in alcohol to remove chlorophyll, wash it and add iodine. A blue-black colour shows starch was formed. A leaf kept in the dark shows no blue-black, proving light is needed.

Example 2 Why a potted plant in a dark cupboard yellows

Without light, chlorophyll is not produced and photosynthesis stops. The plant uses up stored food and its leaves become pale.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which gas is taken in during photosynthesis?
    A. Carbon dioxideB. OxygenC. NitrogenD. Hydrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: Carbon dioxide

    Carbon dioxide enters through stomata and provides carbon for glucose.

  2. MCQ The green pigment in leaves is:
    A. ChlorophyllB. HaemoglobinC. CaroteneD. Melanin
    Show answer

    Answer: Chlorophyll

    Chlorophyll absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.

  3. Short answer List the raw materials needed for photosynthesis.
    Show answer

    Carbon dioxide, water, sunlight and chlorophyll (with minerals from the soil).
