Teeth and Digestion in Other Animals

Teeth are adapted to diet: incisors cut, canines tear, premolars and molars grind. Ruminants and amoeba digest food differently from humans.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Humans have 32 permanent teeth: 8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 premolars and 12 molars.
  • Dental caries is caused by acid produced by bacteria acting on sugar stuck to teeth.
  • Ruminants such as cows swallow grass into the rumen, where bacteria partly digest cellulose; the cud is then chewed again.
  • Amoeba engulfs food with pseudopodia and digests it in a food vacuole.
  • Starfish push out their stomach to digest prey outside the body.

Worked examples

Example 1 Chewing the cud

A cow eats grass quickly, stores it in the rumen, then later brings it back to the mouth to chew again. Cellulose is tough, so double processing extracts far more energy.

Example 2 How an amoeba eats

The amoeba pushes out pseudopodia around a food particle, surrounds it in a food vacuole, digests it with enzymes and absorbs the nutrients — digestion without a digestive system.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Incisors are used for:
    A. Cutting and bitingB. TearingC. GrindingD. Chewing the cud
    Show answer

    Answer: Cutting and biting

    Flat chisel-shaped incisors cut food.

  2. MCQ Amoeba digests food in a:
    A. Food vacuoleB. StomachC. RumenD. Pseudopodium
    Show answer

    Answer: Food vacuole

    Food is engulfed into a vacuole where enzymes break it down.

  3. Short answer Why do ruminants chew the cud?
    Show answer

    To digest cellulose more fully by reprocessing partly digested grass.
