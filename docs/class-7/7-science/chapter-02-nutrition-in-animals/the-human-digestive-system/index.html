The Human Digestive System

Digestion breaks food into small soluble molecules the body can absorb. In humans it follows a tube from mouth to anus, with glands adding enzymes along the way.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Mouth: teeth grind food and saliva (amylase) begins starch digestion.
  • Oesophagus: peristalsis pushes the food bolus down to the stomach.
  • Stomach: hydrochloric acid kills germs and pepsin digests protein.
  • Small intestine: bile (from the liver) emulsifies fats, pancreatic and intestinal enzymes finish digestion, and villi absorb nutrients.
  • Large intestine: absorbs water; undigested waste is removed through the anus.

Worked examples

Example 1 Follow a piece of bread

Chewed bread mixes with amylase in the mouth, travels to the stomach, then the small intestine, where starch is finally broken into glucose and absorbed into blood through villi.

Example 2 Why the small intestine is long and folded

Its length and millions of finger-like villi create enormous surface area for absorbing nutrients quickly. A short smooth tube would absorb far less.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Digestion of starch begins in the:
    A. MouthB. StomachC. Small intestineD. Large intestine
    Show answer

    Answer: Mouth

    Salivary amylase starts breaking starch into sugars in the mouth.

  2. MCQ Villi are found in the:
    A. Small intestineB. StomachC. OesophagusD. Large intestine
    Show answer

    Answer: Small intestine

    Villi increase the absorbing surface of the small intestine.

  3. Short answer What is the role of bile in digestion?
    Show answer

    It emulsifies fats, breaking them into small droplets for enzyme action.
