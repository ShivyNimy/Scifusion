Reactions and Diazonium Salts

Amines undergo acylation, alkylation and coupling reactions. Diazonium salts are versatile intermediates.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Acylation gives amides; alkylation adds alkyl groups.
  • The Hinsberg test distinguishes primary, secondary and tertiary amines.
  • Diazonium salts form from aniline with nitrous acid at low temperature.
  • Diazonium salts undergo coupling to give azo dyes.
  • Amines are found in medicines, dyes and proteins.

Worked examples

Example 1 Azo dye formation

Benzenediazonium chloride couples with phenol to give an orange azo dye.

Example 2 Hinsberg test

Primary amines form a soluble product with benzenesulphonyl chloride; tertiary amines do not react.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Diazonium salts are prepared from aniline at:
    A. Low temperatureB. High temperatureC. Boiling waterD. Strong base only
    Show answer

    Answer: Low temperature

    Low temperature (0–5 °C) stabilises the diazonium ion.

  2. MCQ Azo dyes are formed by:
    A. Coupling of diazonium salts with phenolsB. Oxidation of aminesC. Reduction of nitro compoundsD. Hydrolysis of amides
    Show answer

    Answer: Coupling of diazonium salts with phenols

    Coupling produces the coloured azo group.

  3. Short answer What is the Hinsberg test used for?
    Show answer

    It distinguishes primary, secondary and tertiary amines by their reaction with benzenesulphonyl chloride.
