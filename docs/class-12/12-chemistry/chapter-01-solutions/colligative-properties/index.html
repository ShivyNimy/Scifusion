Colligative Properties

Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles: vapour pressure lowering, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression and osmotic pressure.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • ΔTb = Kbm; ΔTf = Kfm.
  • Osmotic pressure π = CRT for dilute solutions.
  • Van't Hoff factor i accounts for association or dissociation.
  • Colligative properties are used to determine molar mass.
  • Winter salt spreading lowers the freezing point of water.

Worked examples

Example 1 Freezing point depression

Adding 1 mol of a non-electrolyte to 1 kg water gives ΔTf = 1.86 °C, so the freezing point falls to −1.86 °C.

Example 2 Van't Hoff factor

NaCl dissociates into two ions, so i ≈ 2 and the colligative effect nearly doubles.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Osmotic pressure is given by:
    A. π = CRTB. π = RT/CC. π = C/RTD. π = CV
    Show answer

    Answer: π = CRT

    π = CRT for dilute solutions.

  2. MCQ Which property is NOT colligative?
    A. ViscosityB. Boiling point elevationC. Osmotic pressureD. Freezing point depression
    Show answer

    Answer: Viscosity

    Viscosity depends on the nature of the solute, not just the number of particles.

  3. Short answer Why does adding salt lower the freezing point of water?
    Show answer

    The solute particles disrupt the crystal lattice, so more energy must be removed before ice forms.
