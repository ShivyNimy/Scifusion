Concentration Terms and Raoult's Law

Solutions are homogeneous mixtures. Concentration can be expressed as molarity, molality or mole fraction, and vapour pressure depends on composition.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Molarity M = moles of solute per litre of solution; molality m = moles per kg of solvent.
  • Mole fraction xi = ni/Σn; the sum of mole fractions is 1.
  • Raoult's law: partial vapour pressure pi = xi pi°.
  • Ideal solutions obey Raoult's law and have zero enthalpy of mixing.
  • Positive deviation gives higher vapour pressure; negative gives lower.

Worked examples

Example 1 Molality calculation

10 g of NaCl (M = 58.5) in 100 g of water gives 0.171 mol in 0.1 kg, so m = 1.71 mol/kg.

Example 2 Raoult's law

A mixture with xbenzene = 0.4 and pbenzene° = 75 mmHg has pbenzene = 30 mmHg.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which concentration term is independent of temperature?
    A. MolalityB. MolarityC. NormalityD. Formality
    Show answer

    Answer: Molality

    Molality uses mass of solvent, which does not change with temperature.

  2. MCQ An ideal solution has enthalpy of mixing:
    A. ZeroB. PositiveC. NegativeD. Infinite
    Show answer

    Answer: Zero

    No heat is evolved or absorbed on mixing ideal solutions.

  3. Short answer State Raoult's law.
    Show answer

    The partial vapour pressure of each component is proportional to its mole fraction in the solution.
