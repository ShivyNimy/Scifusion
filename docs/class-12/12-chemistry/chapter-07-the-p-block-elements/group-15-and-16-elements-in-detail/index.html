Group 15 and 16 Elements in Detail

The p-block groups show trends in oxidation states, hydrides and oxides as we move down each group.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Group 15: N, P, As, Sb, Bi; oxidation states −3 to +5, with +3 becoming more stable down the group (inert pair effect).
  • Nitrogen forms stable N2 with a triple bond, making it inert.
  • Phosphorus exists as white (reactive, toxic) and red (stable) allotropes.
  • Group 16: O, S, Se, Te, Po; oxygen shows only −2 and −1, while sulphur can show +4 and +6.
  • Oxides of these groups are acidic; nitrogen oxides contribute to pollution.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why nitrogen is inert

N≡N has a very high bond dissociation energy, so nitrogen gas reacts only under extreme conditions or with catalysts.

Example 2 Allotropes of phosphorus

White phosphorus burns spontaneously in air and is stored under water; red phosphorus is stable and used in matchboxes.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Which allotrope of phosphorus is stored under water?
    A. White phosphorusB. Red phosphorusC. Black phosphorusD. Violet phosphorus
    Show answer

    Answer: White phosphorus

    White phosphorus is reactive and is kept under water.

  2. MCQ The inert pair effect makes which oxidation state more stable down group 15?
    A. +3B. +5C. +1D. −3
    Show answer

    Answer: +3

    Lower oxidation states become more stable down the group.

  3. Short answer Why is nitrogen gas chemically inert?
    Show answer

    Its triple bond has very high dissociation energy, so breaking it requires extreme conditions.
