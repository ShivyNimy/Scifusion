Halogens and Noble Gases

Group 17 elements are reactive non-metals with important industrial uses; group 18 elements are notably inert.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Halogens: F2, Cl2, Br2, I2; reactivity decreases down the group.
  • Chlorine is used for water disinfection and in making PVC.
  • The bleaching action of chlorine comes from oxidation (hypochlorous acid).
  • Noble gases have filled outer shells; xenon forms compounds such as XeF2 and XeF4.
  • Where the group 17 and 18 elements are located matters for periodic trends.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why fluorine is the most reactive halogen

Its small size and high electronegativity make it eager to gain an electron, so it reacts with almost every element.

Example 2 Xenon fluorides

Xenon reacts with fluorine under controlled conditions to form compounds such as XeF4, showing that noble gases are not completely inert.

  1. MCQ Which halogen is most reactive?
    A. FluorineB. ChlorineC. BromineD. Iodine

    Answer: Fluorine

  2. MCQ Chlorine bleaches by:
    A. OxidationB. ReductionC. AdsorptionD. Neutralisation
    Show answer

    Answer: Oxidation

    Hypochlorous acid oxidises colouring matter.

  3. Short answer Why are noble gases largely inert?
    Show answer

    Their filled outer shells give very high ionisation energies and little tendency to gain electrons.
