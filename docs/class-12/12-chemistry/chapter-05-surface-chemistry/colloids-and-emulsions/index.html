Colloids and Emulsions

Colloidal particles are larger than molecules but too small to settle. Their surfaces give them special properties.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Colloids are classified as lyophilic or lyophobic depending on affinity for the medium.
  • The Tyndall effect, Brownian motion and electrophoresis reveal colloidal nature.
  • Coagulation destroys a colloid; electrolytes cause coagulation.
  • Emulsions are colloids of one liquid in another, stabilised by emulsifiers.
  • Colloids are used in medicine, food, paints and water purification.

Worked examples

Example 1 Milk as an emulsion

Fat droplets are dispersed in water and stabilised by proteins, making milk an oil-in-water emulsion.

Example 2 River delta formation

River water carrying clay colloids meets salty sea water; ions coagulate the clay, which settles as a delta.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which effect confirms the colloidal nature of a mixture?
    A. Tyndall effectB. Photoelectric effectC. Doppler effectD. Zeeman effect
    Show answer

    Answer: Tyndall effect

    Colloidal particles scatter light.

  2. MCQ An emulsion is a colloid of:
    A. One liquid in anotherB. Gas in liquid onlyC. Solid in solidD. Gas in gas
    Show answer

    Answer: One liquid in another

    Emulsions involve two immiscible liquids.

  3. Short answer Why is milk called an emulsion?
    Show answer

    Fat droplets are dispersed throughout water and stabilised by emulsifying proteins.
