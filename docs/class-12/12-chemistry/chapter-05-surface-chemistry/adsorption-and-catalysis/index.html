Adsorption and Catalysis

Surface chemistry studies phenomena at interfaces. Adsorption concentrates molecules on a surface; catalysis speeds reactions at surfaces.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Adsorption may be physisorption (weak van der Waals forces) or chemisorption (chemical bonds).
  • Physisorption is reversible and has low enthalpy; chemisorption is often irreversible with high enthalpy.
  • Freundlich isotherm: x/m = kp1/n describes adsorption at a given temperature.
  • Catalysts may be homogeneous or heterogeneous; heterogeneous catalysis occurs at surfaces.
  • Catalyst promoters increase activity; poisons reduce it.

Worked examples

Example 1 Activated charcoal

Charcoal has a huge surface area and adsorbs gases and colouring matter, which is why it is used in gas masks and water filters.

Example 2 Iron in ammonia synthesis

The solid iron catalyst provides a surface where nitrogen and hydrogen adsorb and react more easily, lowering activation energy.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Physisorption is characterised by:
    A. Weak van der Waals forcesB. Strong chemical bondsC. IrreversibilityD. High enthalpy
    Show answer

    Answer: Weak van der Waals forces

    Physical adsorption involves weak forces and is reversible.

  2. MCQ A promoter in catalysis:
    A. Increases catalyst activityB. Poisons the catalystC. Stops the reactionD. Is a product
    Show answer

    Answer: Increases catalyst activity

    Promoters enhance catalytic efficiency.

  3. Short answer Differentiate physisorption and chemisorption.
    Show answer

    Physisorption uses weak forces and is reversible; chemisorption involves chemical bonds and is usually irreversible.
