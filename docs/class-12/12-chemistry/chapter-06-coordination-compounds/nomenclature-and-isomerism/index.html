Nomenclature and Isomerism

Coordination compounds contain a central metal bonded to ligands. Naming follows IUPAC rules and several isomerism types are possible.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A coordination entity has a central atom and ligands in a definite ratio.
  • Ligand names end with -o (chlorido, cyanido); anionic complexes end with -ate.
  • The coordination number is the number of donor atoms bonded to the metal.
  • Isomers include geometrical (cis/trans), optical and structural forms.
  • Chelate ligands bind through more than one atom and increase stability.

Worked examples

Example 1 Naming [Co(NH3)6]Cl3

The octahedral complex of cobalt(III) with six ammine ligands is hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride.

Example 2 Cis-platin

cis-[PtCl2(NH3)2] binds DNA and is used in cancer chemotherapy; the trans isomer is inactive.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ In [Fe(CN)6]4−, the coordination number of iron is:
    A. 6B. 4C. 2D. 8
    Show answer

    Answer: 6

    Six cyanide ligands bind to iron.

  2. MCQ A chelating ligand:
    A. Binds through more than one donor atomB. Binds through one atomC. Cannot bindD. Only forms ionic bonds
    Show answer

    Answer: Binds through more than one donor atom

    Chelates form stable ring structures.

  3. Short answer Why is cis-platin an effective anticancer drug?
    Show answer

    It binds to DNA and prevents replication of cancer cells.
