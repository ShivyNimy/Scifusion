Crystal Field Theory and Colour

Crystal field theory explains colour and magnetism by splitting d orbitals in a ligand field.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Ligands split d orbitals into lower-energy t2g and higher-energy eg sets.
  • The splitting energy Δo depends on the metal and ligand.
  • Strong-field ligands produce large splitting and low-spin complexes.
  • Colour arises when electrons absorb visible light to jump between the sets.
  • The spectrochemical series orders ligands by field strength: I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < CN−.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why [Ni(H2O)6]2+ is green

Absorption of light corresponding to Δo promotes d electrons, and the transmitted colour is green.

Example 2 High spin versus low spin

Weak-field ligands like Cl− give high-spin complexes with unpaired electrons; strong-field CN− gives low-spin.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The colour of coordination compounds is due to:
    A. d-d transitionsB. s-s transitionsC. Nuclear spinD. Rotation
    Show answer

    Answer: d-d transitions

    Electron transitions between split d orbitals absorb visible light.

  2. MCQ Which ligand produces the largest crystal field splitting?
    A. CN−B. Cl−C. H2OD. F−
    Show answer

    Answer: CN−

    CN− is high in the spectrochemical series.

  3. Short answer Why is [Cu(H2O)6]2+ blue?
    Show answer

    It absorbs red-orange light in d-d transitions and transmits blue light.
