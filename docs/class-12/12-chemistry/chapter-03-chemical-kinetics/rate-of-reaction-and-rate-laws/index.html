Rate of Reaction and Rate Laws

Kinetics studies reaction rates. Rate laws relate rate to reactant concentrations and reveal reaction order.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Average rate = change in concentration ÷ time.
  • Order is the sum of concentration exponents in the experimental rate law.
  • First-order rate constant k = (2.303/t) log(a/(a − x)).
  • Half-life of a first-order reaction is independent of initial concentration.
  • Zero-order reactions have a constant rate.

Worked examples

Example 1 First-order half-life

A first-order reaction with k = 0.0693 min−1 has t1/2 = 0.693/0.0693 = 10 min.

Example 2 Determining order

Doubling one reactant doubles the rate means first order in that reactant.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ For a first-order reaction, half-life is:
    A. Independent of concentrationB. Proportional to concentrationC. Proportional to concentration squaredD. Zero
    Show answer

    Answer: Independent of concentration

    t1/2 = 0.693/k for first order.

  2. MCQ The unit of a zero-order rate constant is:
    A. mol L−1 s−1B. s−1C. L mol−1 s−1D. mol−2 L2 s−1
    Show answer

    Answer: mol L−1 s−1

    A zero-order rate is constant in concentration units per time.

  3. Short answer A first-order reaction has k = 0.693 s−1. Find its half-life.
    Show answer

    t1/2 = 0.693/k = 1 s.
