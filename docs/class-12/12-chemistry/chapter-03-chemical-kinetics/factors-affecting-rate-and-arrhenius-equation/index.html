Factors Affecting Rate and Arrhenius Equation

Temperature, catalysts, surface area and concentration affect rate. The Arrhenius equation links rate constant and activation energy.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Arrhenius equation: k = A e−Ea/(RT).
  • A catalyst lowers activation energy without being consumed.
  • A 10 °C rise in temperature roughly doubles many reaction rates.
  • Collision theory: only molecules with energy above Ea react.
  • Increasing surface area increases the frequency of collisions.

Worked examples

Example 1 Temperature effect

If Ea = 50 kJ/mol, raising temperature from 300 K to 310 K increases k by roughly a factor of 2.

Example 2 Catalyst in industry

Iron catalyses ammonia synthesis by lowering activation energy, so the process works at more moderate conditions.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A catalyst works by:
    A. Lowering activation energyB. Increasing activation energyC. Changing ΔHD. Changing equilibrium
    Show answer

    Answer: Lowering activation energy

    Lower Ea gives faster reaction at the same temperature.

  2. MCQ The Arrhenius equation relates k to:
    A. Temperature and activation energyB. Pressure onlyC. VolumeD. Concentration only
    Show answer

    Answer: Temperature and activation energy

    k depends exponentially on temperature and Ea.

  3. Short answer Why does a catalyst not change the equilibrium constant?
    Show answer

    It speeds up forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner but its position is unchanged.
