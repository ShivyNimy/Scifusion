Conductance, Kohlrausch's Law and Faraday's Laws

Ionic conductivity depends on concentration and ion mobility. Faraday's laws quantify electrolysis.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Molar conductivity increases with dilution for weak electrolytes.
  • Kohlrausch's law: limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of ion contributions.
  • Faraday's first law: mass deposited is proportional to charge passed.
  • Faraday's second law: masses deposited by the same charge are proportional to equivalent weights.
  • 1 Faraday = 96,500 C/mol.

Worked examples

Example 1 Mass deposited

Depositing copper from Cu2+ with 2 × 96,500 C gives 1 mol of copper (63.5 g).

Example 2 Why conductivity falls with concentration

Ion-ion interactions reduce mobility at higher concentrations.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ One Faraday equals:
    A. 96,500 CB. 1 CC. 96,500 molD. 9.65 C
    Show answer

    Answer: 96,500 C

    One mole of electrons carries 96,500 C.

  2. MCQ Molar conductivity of a weak electrolyte increases on dilution because:
    A. Ionisation increasesB. Ions disappearC. Temperature dropsD. Viscosity rises
    Show answer

    Answer: Ionisation increases

    More ions form as the solution is diluted.

  3. Short answer State Faraday's first law of electrolysis.
    Show answer

    The mass of substance deposited at an electrode is proportional to the quantity of charge passed.
