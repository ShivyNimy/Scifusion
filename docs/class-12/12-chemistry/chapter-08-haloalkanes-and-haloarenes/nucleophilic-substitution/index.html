Nucleophilic Substitution

Haloalkanes undergo substitution. SN1 and SN2 mechanisms differ in rate law and stereochemistry.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • SN2^ is a one-step reaction with a rate depending on both substrate and nucleophile.
  • SN1^ goes through a carbocation intermediate and shows first-order kinetics.
  • Tertiary substrates favour SN1; primary favour SN2.
  • SN2^ causes inversion of configuration (Walden inversion).
  • Haloarenes are unreactive toward SN2^ because of partial double-bond character of the C–X bond.

Worked examples

Example 1 SN2^ inversion

Hydrolysis of an optically active halide by SN2^ gives the product with inverted configuration.

Example 2 Why chlorobenzene is inert

Resonance shortens and strengthens the C–Cl bond, so nucleophiles cannot easily displace chloride.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ SN1^ reactions are favoured by:
    A. Tertiary halidesB. Primary halidesC. Methyl halidesD. Haloarenes
    Show answer

    Answer: Tertiary halides

    Tertiary carbocations are more stable, favouring SN1^.

  2. MCQ SN2^ substitution produces:
    A. Inversion of configurationB. Retention onlyC. Racemisation onlyD. No change
    Show answer

    Answer: Inversion of configuration

    The nucleophile attacks the opposite side of the leaving group.

  3. Short answer Why are haloarenes less reactive than haloalkanes?
    Show answer

    Partial double-bond character of the C–X bond makes it stronger and harder to break.
