Structure, Nomenclature and Reactions

Aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl group. Nucleophilic addition and oxidation distinguish them.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Aldehydes are easily oxidised; ketones are not.
  • Tollens' and Fehling's tests detect aldehydes.
  • Nucleophilic addition is the key reaction of carbonyls.
  • Aldol condensation and Cannizzaro reactions are important syntheses.
  • Carboxylic acids are weakly acidic and give esters with alcohols.

Worked examples

Example 1 Tollens' silver mirror

An aldehyde reduces [Ag(NH3)2]+ to silver metal, forming a mirror on the test tube.

Example 2 Esterification

Ethanoic acid and ethanol with acid catalyst give ethyl ethanoate, an ester with fruity smell.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Tollens' reagent gives a silver mirror with:
    A. AldehydesB. KetonesC. AlcoholsD. Ethers
    Show answer

    Answer: Aldehydes

    Aldehydes are oxidised while silver ions are reduced.

  2. MCQ Carboxylic acids are:
    A. Weakly acidicB. Strongly basicC. NeutralD. Strongly acidic
    Show answer

    Answer: Weakly acidic

    They ionise partially to give a carboxylate ion.

  3. Short answer Why are carboxylic acids more acidic than phenols?
    Show answer

    The carboxylate ion is stabilised by resonance over two equivalent oxygens.
