Classification and Polymerisation

Polymers are large molecules formed by joining monomers. Addition and condensation polymerisation produce different types.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Addition polymers form from unsaturated monomers, e.g. polythene from ethene.
  • Condensation polymers form with loss of a small molecule, e.g. nylon from a diamine and diacid.
  • Homopolymers have one monomer type; copolymers have more than one.
  • Thermoplastics soften on heating; thermosetting plastics do not.
  • Natural polymers include starch, cellulose and proteins.

Worked examples

Example 1 Nylon formation

Hexamethylenediamine plus adipic acid condense to nylon-6,6, losing water and forming strong amide bonds.

Example 2 Polythene

Ethene molecules add to each other to form long chains; the polymer is used for bags and bottles.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Polythene is formed by:
    A. Addition polymerisationB. Condensation polymerisationC. HydrolysisD. Esterification
    Show answer

    Answer: Addition polymerisation

    Ethene monomers add without losing atoms.

  2. MCQ Nylon is a:
    A. Condensation polymerB. Addition polymerC. Natural polymerD. Monomer
    Show answer

    Answer: Condensation polymer

    It forms with loss of water.

  3. Short answer Differentiate addition and condensation polymerisation.
    Show answer

    Addition joins unsaturated monomers without loss of molecules; condensation joins monomers with loss of a small molecule such as water.
