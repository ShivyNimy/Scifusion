Transition Metal Properties

Transition metals show variable oxidation states, form coloured compounds and act as catalysts.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Variable oxidation states arise from similar 4s and 3d energies.
  • Coloured ions come from d-d electronic transitions.
  • Many transition metals and their compounds catalyse reactions.
  • They form complex compounds with ligands.
  • Trends across the series reflect increasing nuclear charge and 3d filling.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why Mn shows many oxidation states

Manganese has five 3d electrons and two 4s electrons, so it can lose different numbers, giving states from +2 to +7.

Example 2 Coloured Cu2+ solutions

Hydrated Cu2+ absorbs red-orange light from d-d transitions, so the solution appears blue.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Transition metals show variable oxidation states because:
    A. 3d and 4s energies are closeB. They have only s electronsC. They lack d electronsD. They are non-metals
    Show answer

    Answer: 3d and 4s energies are close

    Electrons from both 3d and 4s can be used.

  2. MCQ Colour of transition metal ions is due to:
    A. d-d transitionsB. Nuclear transitionsC. Vibrations onlyD. Ionisation
    Show answer

    Answer: d-d transitions

    Absorption of visible light excites d electrons.

  3. Short answer Why is Zn not considered a typical transition metal?
    Show answer

    Its 3d subshell is full, so it does not show variable oxidation states or coloured compounds.
