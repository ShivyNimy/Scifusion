Food Additives, Cleansing Agents and Environmental Impact

Chemicals improve food quality and hygiene but can have environmental consequences.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Preservatives prevent microbial spoilage; antioxidants prevent oxidation.
  • Artificial sweeteners add sweetness without much energy.
  • Soaps are salts of fatty acids; detergents are synthetic surfactants.
  • Detergents work in hard water; soaps do not lather well in hard water.
  • Non-biodegradable detergents and fertilisers pollute water bodies.

Worked examples

Example 1 Soap in hard water

Calcium ions form an insoluble scum with soap, wasting soap and reducing cleaning.

Example 2 Eutrophication from detergents

Phosphate-rich detergents enrich lakes with nutrients, causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Soaps are salts of:
    A. Fatty acidsB. Petroleum sulphonatesC. AlcoholsD. Amines
    Show answer

    Answer: Fatty acids

    Soap is a sodium or potassium salt of a fatty acid.

  2. MCQ Detergents are better than soaps in hard water because:
    A. They do not form scumB. They are cheaperC. They dissolve fasterD. They are natural
    Show answer

    Answer: They do not form scum

    Synthetic surfactants remain soluble in hard water.

  3. Short answer Why are phosphates in detergents harmful?
    Show answer

    They promote eutrophication, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion in water bodies.
