Alcohols and Phenols

Alcohols contain −OH on saturated carbon; phenols have −OH on an aromatic ring. Their reactivity differs sharply.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Alcohols hydrogen-bond, giving high boiling points.
  • Phenols are acidic because the phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised.
  • Alcohols are acidic but weaker than water; phenols are much more acidic.
  • Primary alcohols oxidise to aldehydes then carboxylic acids.
  • Phenols give characteristic colour with FeCl3 and are easily brominated.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why phenol is acidic

The negative charge on the phenoxide ion is delocalised into the ring, stabilising the conjugate base.

Example 2 Lucas test

Tertiary alcohols react immediately with Lucas reagent; primary alcohols react only on heating, distinguishing the classes.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Phenol is more acidic than ethanol because:
    A. Phenoxide is resonance-stabilisedB. It is aromaticC. It dissolves in waterD. It boils higher
    Show answer

    Answer: Phenoxide is resonance-stabilised

    Resonance stabilises the phenoxide anion.

  2. MCQ Oxidation of a primary alcohol gives first:
    A. An aldehydeB. A ketoneC. A carboxylic acidD. An ester
    Show answer

    Answer: An aldehyde

    Controlled oxidation stops at the aldehyde stage.

  3. Short answer Why do alcohols have higher boiling points than ethers of similar mass?
    Show answer

    Alcohols form intermolecular hydrogen bonds; ethers do not.
