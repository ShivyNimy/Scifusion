Cells, EMF and Kirchhoff's Rules

Circuits are analysed with EMF, internal resistance and Kirchhoff's rules.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • EMF ε is the maximum potential difference a cell can provide.
  • Terminal voltage V = ε − Ir, where r is internal resistance.
  • Kirchhoff's junction rule: sum of currents entering a junction equals sum leaving (charge conservation).
  • Kirchhoff's loop rule: sum of potential changes around a loop is zero (energy conservation).
  • A potentiometer and Wheatstone bridge measure EMF and resistance precisely.

Worked examples

Example 1 Internal resistance

A cell of EMF 2 V with internal resistance 0.5 Ω delivering 1 A has terminal voltage 2 − 0.5 = 1.5 V.

Example 2 Wheatstone bridge balance

The bridge is balanced when P/Q = R/S, so an unknown resistance can be found from three known ones.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Terminal voltage of a cell is:
    A. EMF minus IrB. EMF plus IrC. Always equal to EMFD. Zero
    Show answer

    Answer: EMF minus Ir

    Internal resistance causes a voltage drop.

  2. MCQ Kirchhoff's junction rule is based on:
    A. Conservation of chargeB. Conservation of energyC. Ohm's lawD. Faraday's law
    Show answer

    Answer: Conservation of charge

    Charge entering a junction must leave it.

  3. Short answer What is the internal resistance of a cell?
    Show answer

    The resistance offered by the electrolyte and electrodes to current flow inside the cell.
