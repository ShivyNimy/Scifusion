Ohm's Law, Resistance and Drift Velocity

Current flows due to potential difference. Resistance depends on material, length and area.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Current I = dQ/dt; conventional current flows opposite to electron flow.
  • Ohm's law: V = IR at constant temperature.
  • Resistance R = ρL/A; resistivity ρ is a material property.
  • Drift velocity vd = I/(neA); typical values are very small.
  • Current density J = I/A = nevd.

Worked examples

Example 1 Resistance of a wire

A copper wire of length 2 m, area 10−6 m2 and resistivity 1.7 × 10−8 Ω·m has R = 1.7 × 10−8 × 2/10−6 = 0.034 Ω.

Example 2 Drift velocity

A 1 A current in a wire of area 10−6 m2 with n = 8 × 1028 m−3 has vd = 1/(8 × 1028 × 1.6 × 10−19 × 10−6) ≈ 7.8 × 10−5 m/s.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Resistance of a wire is directly proportional to:
    A. LengthB. AreaC. CurrentD. Voltage
    Show answer

    Answer: Length

    R = ρL/A.

  2. MCQ The SI unit of resistivity is:
    A. Ω·mB. ΩC. V/mD. A/m2
    Show answer

    Answer: Ω·m

    Resistivity has units of ohm-metre.

  3. Short answer Why is drift velocity small even though lights turn on instantly?
    Show answer

    The electric field propagates near light speed, so all electrons start moving almost simultaneously.
