Nuclear Structure and Binding Energy

Nuclei contain protons and neutrons. Mass defect and binding energy explain nuclear stability.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Atomic number Z = protons; mass number A = protons + neutrons.
  • Mass defect Δm = mass of nucleons − mass of nucleus.
  • Binding energy = Δm c2; binding energy per nucleon measures stability.
  • Iron-56 has near maximum binding energy per nucleon.
  • Nuclear forces are short-range and strong, overcoming proton repulsion.

Worked examples

Example 1 Binding energy per nucleon

Iron-56 has about 8.8 MeV per nucleon, the highest among common nuclei, making it very stable.

Example 2 Why heavy nuclei fission

Splitting a heavy nucleus increases binding energy per nucleon, releasing energy.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The mass number of a nucleus is:
    A. Protons + neutronsB. Protons onlyC. Neutrons onlyD. Electrons + protons
    Show answer

    Answer: Protons + neutrons

    Mass number counts nucleons.

  2. MCQ Which nucleus has the highest binding energy per nucleon?
    A. Iron-56B. Uranium-238C. Hydrogen-1D. Helium-4
    Show answer

    Answer: Iron-56

    Iron-56 is near the peak of the binding energy curve.

  3. Short answer What is mass defect?
    Show answer

    The difference between the sum of masses of nucleons and the mass of the nucleus, appearing as binding energy.
