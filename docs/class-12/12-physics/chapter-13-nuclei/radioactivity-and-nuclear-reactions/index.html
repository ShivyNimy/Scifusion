Radioactivity and Nuclear Reactions

Unstable nuclei decay by alpha, beta or gamma emission. Fission and fusion release energy.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Alpha decay: emission of a helium nucleus; A decreases by 4 and Z by 2.
  • Beta decay: a neutron converts to a proton, emitting an electron and antineutrino.
  • Gamma decay: emission of a high-energy photon without change in A or Z.
  • Half-life is the time for half the nuclei to decay.
  • Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion joins light nuclei.

Worked examples

Example 1 Half-life calculation

After 3 half-lives, 1/8 of the original sample remains.

Example 2 Carbon dating

Living things maintain a constant carbon-14 level; after death it decays with a 5730-year half-life, dating organic remains.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Alpha decay changes the mass number by:
    A. −4B. −1C. 0D. +4
    Show answer

    Answer: −4

    An alpha particle removes 4 nucleons.

  2. MCQ In beta-minus decay, a neutron becomes a:
    A. ProtonB. ElectronC. Gamma rayD. Alpha particle
    Show answer

    Answer: Proton

    A neutron converts to a proton, emitting an electron.

  3. Short answer Why does fusion release energy?
    Show answer

    Light nuclei combine into a more tightly bound nucleus, so the mass defect converts into energy.
