Semiconductors and Doping

Semiconductors have conductivity between conductors and insulators, controlled by doping.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Silicon and germanium are the common semiconductors.
  • N-type semiconductors are doped with pentavalent atoms, adding electrons.
  • P-type semiconductors are doped with trivalent atoms, creating holes.
  • The depletion region forms at a p-n junction.
  • A p-n junction diode conducts in one direction.

Worked examples

Example 1 Doping silicon

Adding phosphorus (5 valence electrons) creates free electrons, making n-type silicon.

Example 2 Holes as current carriers

In p-type silicon, missing electrons behave as positive holes that move under an electric field.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ N-type semiconductors have:
    A. Extra electronsB. Extra holesC. No charge carriersD. Only holes
    Show answer

    Answer: Extra electrons

    Pentavalent doping provides electrons.

  2. MCQ A diode conducts when:
    A. Forward biasedB. Reverse biasedC. UnbiasedD. Broken
    Show answer

    Answer: Forward biased

    Forward bias reduces the depletion barrier.

  3. Short answer Why do p-n junctions act as one-way valves?
    Show answer

    They allow current in forward bias but block it in reverse bias.
