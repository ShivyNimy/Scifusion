Capacitors and Dielectrics

Capacitors store charge and energy. Their capacitance depends on geometry and the dielectric between plates.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Capacitance C = Q/V; the unit is the farad (F).
  • Parallel plate capacitor: C = ε0A/d; with dielectric C = Kε0A/d.
  • Energy stored U = ½CV2 = Q2/2C.
  • Series: 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2; parallel: C = C1 + C2.
  • A dielectric polarises and reduces the field, increasing capacitance.

Worked examples

Example 1 Energy in a capacitor

A 10 μF capacitor charged to 100 V stores U = ½ × 10−5 × 104 = 0.5 J.

Example 2 Effect of a dielectric

Inserting a dielectric with K = 4 multiplies the capacitance by 4 and stores more charge at the same voltage.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Capacitance is measured in:
    A. FaradsB. CoulombsC. VoltsD. Ohms
    Show answer

    Answer: Farads

    The farad is the unit of capacitance.

  2. MCQ Energy stored in a capacitor is:
    A. ½CV2B. CVC. QVD. V/C
    Show answer

    Answer: ½CV2

    U = ½CV2.

  3. Short answer Why does a dielectric increase capacitance?
    Show answer

    It polarises and reduces the effective field between plates, allowing more charge at the same voltage.
