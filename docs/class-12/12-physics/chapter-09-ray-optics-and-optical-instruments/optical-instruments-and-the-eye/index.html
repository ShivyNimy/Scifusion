Optical Instruments and the Eye

Microscopes and telescopes use lenses to magnify. The human eye is an adjustable optical system.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A simple microscope uses a convex lens to give angular magnification.
  • A compound microscope uses objective and eyepiece lenses.
  • An astronomical telescope produces a magnified image of distant objects.
  • The eye's accommodation adjusts for near and far vision.
  • Defects: myopia (concave lens), hypermetropia (convex lens), astigmatism (cylindrical lens).

Worked examples

Example 1 Telescope magnification

M = fo/fe for a telescope at normal adjustment; a longer objective and shorter eyepiece give more magnification.

Example 2 Why near objects strain the eye

The ciliary muscles must contract more to increase lens power, which tires the eye over time.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A myopic eye is corrected by a:
    A. Concave lensB. Convex lensC. Plane mirrorD. Prism
    Show answer

    Answer: Concave lens

    A diverging lens moves the focus back to the retina.

  2. MCQ Microscope magnification is increased by:
    A. Shorter focal length objectiveB. Longer eyepieceC. Bigger apertureD. Stronger light
    Show answer

    Answer: Shorter focal length objective

    Magnification is inversely related to focal length.

  3. Short answer Why does a compound microscope use two lenses?
    Show answer

    The objective magnifies the specimen and the eyepiece magnifies that image further.
