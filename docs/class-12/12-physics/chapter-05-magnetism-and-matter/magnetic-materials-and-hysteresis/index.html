Magnetic Materials and Hysteresis

Materials respond differently to magnetic fields: diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferromagnetic. Hysteresis affects energy loss in transformers.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Diamagnetic materials are weakly repelled by magnetic fields.
  • Paramagnetic materials are weakly attracted; alignment is lost when the field is removed.
  • Ferromagnetic materials can be strongly magnetised and retain magnetism (domains).
  • Soft iron has a narrow hysteresis loop and is used in electromagnets.
  • Steel has a wide loop, retaining magnetism — used for permanent magnets.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why soft iron for transformer cores

Its narrow hysteresis loop means little energy is lost as heat during repeated magnetisation cycles.

Example 2 Why steel holds magnetism

Domain walls resist movement in steel, so the magnetisation remains after the external field is removed.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Ferromagnetic materials:
    A. Are strongly attracted to magnetsB. Are repelledC. Do not interactD. Are always magnets
    Show answer

    Answer: Are strongly attracted to magnets

    They have domains that align strongly with fields.

  2. MCQ A material with a narrow hysteresis loop is good for:
    A. Electromagnet coresB. Permanent magnetsC. InsulationD. Dielectrics
    Show answer

    Answer: Electromagnet cores

    Low hysteresis loss suits repeated magnetisation.

  3. Short answer What is hysteresis?
    Show answer

    The lag between magnetisation and the applied magnetic field in a ferromagnetic material.
