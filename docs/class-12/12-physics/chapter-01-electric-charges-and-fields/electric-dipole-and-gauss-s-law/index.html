Electric Dipole and Gauss's Law

A dipole consists of two equal and opposite charges. Gauss's law relates electric flux through a closed surface to enclosed charge.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Dipole moment p = q × 2a, directed from −q to +q.
  • Torque on a dipole in a uniform field: τ = pE sin θ.
  • Gauss's law: φ = qenclosed/ε0.
  • The field inside a conductor is zero in electrostatic equilibrium.
  • Field due to an infinite sheet: E = σ/(2ε0); inside a spherical shell: zero.

Worked examples

Example 1 Field of a charged sheet

A sheet with σ = 10−6 C/m2 produces E = 10−6/(2 × 8.85 × 10−12) ≈ 5.6 × 104 N/C, independent of distance.

Example 2 Torque on a dipole

A dipole of moment 2 × 10−6 C·m in a field of 104 N/C at 90° feels τ = 0.02 N·m.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Gauss's law relates electric flux to:
    A. Enclosed chargeB. Surface areaC. PotentialD. Current
    Show answer

    Answer: Enclosed charge

    Flux depends only on the charge inside the closed surface.

  2. MCQ The electric field inside a charged conductor is:
    A. ZeroB. MaximumC. σ/ε0D. Infinite
    Show answer

    Answer: Zero

    Charges reside on the surface in equilibrium.

  3. Short answer Why does a charged spherical shell produce no field inside?
    Show answer

    By Gauss's law no charge is enclosed by an interior surface, so the flux and the field are zero.
