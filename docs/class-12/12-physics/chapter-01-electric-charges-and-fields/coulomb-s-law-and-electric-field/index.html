Coulomb's Law and Electric Field

Charged objects exert forces described by Coulomb's law. The electric field describes the force per unit charge at a point.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Coulomb's law: F = kq1q2/r2, with k = 9 × 109 N·m2/C2.
  • Electric field E = F/q = kq/r2, a vector pointing away from positive charge.
  • Field lines point from positive to negative and never cross.
  • Superposition: the net field is the vector sum of individual fields.
  • Electric flux φ = E·A cos θ, measured in N·m2/C.

Worked examples

Example 1 Force between two charges

Two 1 μC charges 1 m apart repel with F = 9 × 109 × 10−6 × 10−6/1 = 9 × 10−3 N.

Example 2 Electric field of a point charge

At 0.3 m from a 2 μC charge, E = 9 × 109 × 2 × 10−6/(0.3)2 = 2 × 105 N/C.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The SI unit of electric field is:
    A. N/CB. C/NC. N·mD. J/C
    Show answer

    Answer: N/C

    Electric field is force per unit charge.

  2. MCQ Electric field lines due to a positive charge point:
    A. Away from the chargeB. Toward the chargeC. Along a circleD. Nowhere
    Show answer

    Answer: Away from the charge

    Field lines point away from positive charges.

  3. Short answer State Coulomb's law.
    Show answer

    The force between two point charges is proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.
