De Broglie Waves and Matter Waves

Particles also show wave behaviour. The de Broglie wavelength connects momentum and wavelength.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p = h/(mv).
  • Heavier or faster particles have shorter wavelengths.
  • Electron diffraction experiments confirm matter waves.
  • Matter waves explain the Bohr quantisation condition.
  • Wave-particle duality applies to both light and matter.

Worked examples

Example 1 Electron wavelength

An electron accelerated through 100 V has λ ≈ 0.12 nm, comparable to atomic spacing, so crystals diffract electrons.

Example 2 Why we do not see a cricket ball diffract

Its momentum is enormous, so its de Broglie wavelength is far too small to notice.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ de Broglie wavelength is:
    A. h/pB. hpC. p/hD. h/m
    Show answer

    Answer: h/p

    Wavelength is inversely proportional to momentum.

  2. MCQ A heavier particle has a ______ de Broglie wavelength.
    A. ShorterB. LongerC. ZeroD. Infinite
    Show answer

    Answer: Shorter

    λ = h/(mv), so larger mass means shorter wavelength.

  3. Short answer Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of a 0.1 kg ball moving at 10 m/s.
    Show answer

    λ = h/(mv) = 6.6 × 10−34/(0.1 × 10) = 6.6 × 10−34 m, unmeasurably small.
