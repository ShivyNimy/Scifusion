Photoelectric Effect

Light behaves as particles called photons. The photoelectric effect shows that light of sufficient frequency ejects electrons.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A photon has energy E = hf = hc/λ.
  • Photoelectric emission needs frequency above a threshold, regardless of intensity.
  • Kinetic energy: Kmax = hf − φ, where φ is the work function.
  • Stopping potential V0: eV0 = Kmax.
  • Intensity affects the number of electrons, not their maximum energy.

Worked examples

Example 1 Threshold frequency

A metal with work function 2 eV needs light above f = φ/h ≈ 4.8 × 1014 Hz.

Example 2 Photon energy calculation

A 400 nm photon has E = hc/λ = 1240/400 ≈ 3.1 eV.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ The photoelectric effect shows that light:
    A. Behaves as particlesB. Is continuousC. Has no energyD. Is a sound wave
    Show answer

    Answer: Behaves as particles

    Photons explain the threshold frequency.

  2. MCQ Maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends on:
    A. Frequency of lightB. Intensity onlyC. DistanceD. Angle
    Show answer

    Answer: Frequency of light

    Kmax = hf − φ.

  3. Short answer Why does increased intensity not increase maximum kinetic energy?
    Show answer

    Intensity increases the number of photons, not the energy per photon.
