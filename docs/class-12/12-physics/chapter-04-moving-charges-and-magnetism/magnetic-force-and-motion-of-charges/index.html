Magnetic Force and Motion of Charges

Moving charges experience magnetic forces and produce magnetic fields. The force is perpendicular to velocity and field.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Force F = qvB sin θ; a charge at rest experiences no magnetic force.
  • A charge moving along the field direction feels no force.
  • Circular motion in a uniform field: r = mv/(qB).
  • Cyclotron frequency f = qB/(2πm).
  • Biot-Savart law gives the field of a current element.

Worked examples

Example 1 Radius of a circular path

An electron with v = 106 m/s in B = 0.01 T has r = 9.1 × 10−31 × 106/(1.6 × 10−19 × 0.01) ≈ 5.7 × 10−4 m.

Example 2 Why a charge follows a helix

If velocity has a component along the field, that component stays constant while the perpendicular component circles, producing a helical path.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A stationary charge in a magnetic field experiences:
    A. No forceB. Maximum forceC. Electric forceD. Centripetal force
    Show answer

    Answer: No force

    Magnetic force requires motion.

  2. MCQ Radius of circular motion in a magnetic field is:
    A. mv/(qB)B. qB/(mv)C. mvB/qD. qv/(mB)
    Show answer

    Answer: mv/(qB)

    r = mv/(qB).

  3. Short answer Why does a magnetic force do no work on a moving charge?
    Show answer

    The force is always perpendicular to velocity, so it changes direction but not speed.
