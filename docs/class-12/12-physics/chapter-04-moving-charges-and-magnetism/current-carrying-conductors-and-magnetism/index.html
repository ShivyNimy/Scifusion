Current-Carrying Conductors and Magnetism

Current-carrying wires produce magnetic fields and experience forces in external fields. This is the basis of motors and meters.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Field of a long straight wire: B = μ0I/(2πr).
  • Field inside a solenoid: B = μ0nI.
  • Force on a wire: F = BIL sin θ.
  • Two parallel wires attract if currents are in the same direction.
  • A moving-coil galvanometer uses torque on a coil in a magnetic field.

Worked examples

Example 1 Field near a wire

A 10 A current at 0.05 m gives B = 4π × 10−7 × 10/(2π × 0.05) = 4 × 10−5 T.

Example 2 Motor principle

A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences torque, making it rotate — the basis of an electric motor.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The magnetic field inside a long solenoid is:
    A. μ0nIB. μ0I/(2πr)C. ZeroD. BIL
    Show answer

    Answer: μ0nI

    The field is uniform and equals μ0nI.

  2. MCQ Two parallel wires with currents in the same direction:
    A. AttractB. RepelC. Do not interactD. Rotate
    Show answer

    Answer: Attract

    Each wire's field exerts an attractive force on the other.

  3. Short answer State Ampere's circuital law.
    Show answer

    The line integral of the magnetic field around a closed loop equals μ0 times the total current through the loop.
