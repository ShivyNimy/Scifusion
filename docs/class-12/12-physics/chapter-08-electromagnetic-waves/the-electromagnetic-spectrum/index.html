The Electromagnetic Spectrum

EM waves span radio to gamma rays. Wavelength determines energy and applications.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Radio waves: long wavelength, communication and broadcasting.
  • Microwaves: radar, microwave ovens and satellite communication.
  • Infrared: remote controls and thermal imaging.
  • Visible light: 400–700 nm; ultraviolet: sterilisation and vitamin D synthesis.
  • X-rays and gamma rays are ionising and used in imaging and cancer therapy.

Worked examples

Example 1 Microwave oven

Microwaves match the natural frequency of water molecules, heating food from within.

Example 2 Why UV is harmful

Ultraviolet photons carry enough energy to break chemical bonds, so they damage DNA and skin.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which waves have the shortest wavelength?
    A. Gamma raysB. Radio wavesC. InfraredD. Microwaves
    Show answer

    Answer: Gamma rays

    Gamma rays have the highest frequency and shortest wavelength.

  2. MCQ Microwave ovens work by:
    A. Heating water moleculesB. Cooling foodC. Emitting soundD. Using X-rays
    Show answer

    Answer: Heating water molecules

    Microwaves excite water molecules, generating heat.

  3. Short answer Why are X-rays used in medical imaging?
    Show answer

    They penetrate soft tissue but are absorbed by dense bone, producing contrast images.
