Faraday's and Lenz's Laws

Changing magnetic flux induces an EMF. Lenz's law gives the direction of the induced current.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Magnetic flux φ = BA cos θ.
  • Faraday's law: induced EMF ε = −dφ/dt.
  • Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the change producing it.
  • A changing field or changing area can change flux.
  • Induced EMF appears in a conductor moving through a field: ε = Bvl.

Worked examples

Example 1 Motional EMF

A rod 0.5 m long moving at 2 m/s perpendicular to a 0.1 T field develops ε = 0.1 × 2 × 0.5 = 0.1 V.

Example 2 Dropping a magnet through a coil

The changing flux induces a current whose field opposes the magnet's motion, so the magnet falls more slowly.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Faraday's law says the induced EMF equals:
    A. Negative rate of change of fluxB. Flux times areaC. Field times distanceD. Current times resistance
    Show answer

    Answer: Negative rate of change of flux

    ε = −dφ/dt.

  2. MCQ Lenz's law is a consequence of conservation of:
    A. EnergyB. ChargeC. MassD. Momentum
    Show answer

    Answer: Energy

    The induced current opposes the change, conserving energy.

  3. Short answer Why does a magnet fall slowly through a copper tube?
    Show answer

    The induced eddy currents create a field opposite to the magnet's motion, producing a retarding force.
