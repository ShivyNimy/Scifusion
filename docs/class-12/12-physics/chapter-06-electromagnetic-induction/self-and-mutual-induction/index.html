Self and Mutual Induction

Changing current in a coil induces EMF in the same or a nearby coil. This is inductance.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Self-inductance ε = −L dI/dt; L is measured in henries (H).
  • Energy stored in an inductor U = ½LI2.
  • Mutual inductance M relates flux in one coil to current in another.
  • Inductors oppose changes in current, analogue to inertia.
  • Transformers use mutual induction to change AC voltage.

Worked examples

Example 1 Energy in an inductor

A 2 H inductor carrying 3 A stores U = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.

Example 2 Transformer turns ratio

A transformer with 100 primary turns and 500 secondary turns steps 220 V up to 1100 V (ignoring losses).

    Answer: Henry

  2. MCQ Energy stored in an inductor is:
    A. ½LI2B. LIC. LI2D. I2/L
    Show answer

    Answer: ½LI2

    U = ½LI2.

  3. Short answer Why is mutual induction useful in transformers?
    Show answer

    Changing current in the primary creates a changing flux that induces a voltage in the secondary.
