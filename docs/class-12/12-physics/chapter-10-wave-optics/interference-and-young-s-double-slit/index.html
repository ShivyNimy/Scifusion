Interference and Young's Double Slit

Light behaves as a wave in interference and diffraction. Coherent sources produce a stable interference pattern.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Interference produces bright and dark fringes from path differences.
  • Path difference for bright fringe: Δ = nλ; for dark fringe: Δ = (2n − 1)λ/2.
  • Fringe width in YDSE: β = λD/d.
  • Coherent sources have constant phase difference and the same frequency.
  • Diffraction also confirms the wave nature of light.

Worked examples

Example 1 Fringe width

With λ = 600 nm, D = 1 m and d = 0.5 mm, β = 6 × 10−7 × 1/(5 × 10−4) = 1.2 mm.

Example 2 Why fringes are coloured in white light

Different wavelengths give different fringe widths and positions, so colours separate.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Fringe width in YDSE is directly proportional to:
    A. WavelengthB. Slit separationC. 1/DD. Frequency
    Show answer

    Answer: Wavelength

    β = λD/d.

  2. MCQ Coherent sources must have:
    A. Constant phase differenceB. Different frequenciesC. Different wavelengthsD. Random phase
    Show answer

    Answer: Constant phase difference

    Coherence is required for stable interference.

  3. Short answer What is diffraction?
    Show answer

    The spreading of light around obstacles or edges, showing its wave nature.
