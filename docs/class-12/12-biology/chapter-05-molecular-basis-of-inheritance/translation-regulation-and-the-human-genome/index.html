Translation, Regulation and the Human Genome

Genes are expressed through translation. Gene regulation controls when proteins are made.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Translation uses ribosomes, tRNA and mRNA to assemble amino acids.
  • The lac operon is a classic model of gene regulation in bacteria.
  • Regulation can occur at transcription, processing, translation or protein level.
  • The Human Genome Project mapped the sequence of human DNA.
  • Mutations change DNA sequence and may alter protein function.

Worked examples

Example 1 Lac operon

Lactose binds the repressor, allowing RNA polymerase to express genes for lactose metabolism only when lactose is present.

Example 2 Human Genome Project finding

Only about 1.5–2% of the human genome codes for proteins, revealing many regulatory sequences.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Translation occurs on the:
    A. RibosomeB. NucleusC. LysosomeD. Golgi body
    Show answer

    Answer: Ribosome

    Ribosomes translate mRNA into protein.

  2. MCQ The lac operon is induced by:
    A. LactoseB. GlucoseC. GalactoseD. Sucrose
    Show answer

    Answer: Lactose

    Lactose inactivates the repressor.

  3. Short answer What is a mutation?
    Show answer

    A change in the DNA sequence that may alter the protein produced.
