Species Interactions and Adaptations

Organisms interact with each other and their environment, shaping distributions and adaptations.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Competition occurs when resources are limited.
  • Predation controls prey populations and drives adaptations.
  • Parasitism benefits one organism at the expense of another.
  • Mutualism benefits both partners, as in lichens and mycorrhizae.
  • Adaptations allow organisms to survive extremes of temperature and water.

Worked examples

Example 1 Mycorrhiza

Fungi extend the root's reach for water and phosphorus while receiving sugars from the plant.

Example 2 Desert adaptations

Nocturnal habits, water storage and reduced leaves help desert plants and animals survive.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Mycorrhiza is an example of:
    A. MutualismB. ParasitismC. CompetitionD. Predation
    Show answer

    Answer: Mutualism

    Both fungus and plant benefit.

  2. MCQ Predation typically:
    A. Controls prey population sizeB. Increases prey unlimitedlyC. Has no effectD. Only affects predators
    Show answer

    Answer: Controls prey population size

    Predators reduce prey numbers and shape adaptations.

  3. Short answer Why are mutualistic relationships important in ecosystems?
    Show answer

    They improve survival, nutrition and reproduction for both partners, supporting ecosystem stability.
