Double Fertilisation and Seed Development

Angiosperms show double fertilisation: one sperm fertilises the egg and another fuses with polar nuclei, forming endosperm.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • After pollination, pollen germinates and a pollen tube carries male gametes to the ovule.
  • One gamete fuses with the egg to form the zygote; the other with two polar nuclei to form triploid endosperm.
  • The ovule becomes the seed and the ovary becomes the fruit.
  • Parthenocarpy is fruit development without fertilisation (seedless banana).
  • Apomixis produces seeds without fertilisation, preserving hybrid traits.

Worked examples

Example 1 Coconut structure

The coconut's liquid endosperm nourishes the embryo during development, and the hard shell protects the seed.

Example 2 Seedless grapes

Growers induce parthenocarpy with hormones so fruits develop without seeds.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Endosperm in angiosperms is:
    A. TriploidB. DiploidC. HaploidD. Tetraploid
    Show answer

    Answer: Triploid

    Double fertilisation produces triploid endosperm.

  2. MCQ A fruit develops from the:
    A. OvaryB. OvuleC. StigmaD. Anther
    Show answer

    Answer: Ovary

    The ovary wall becomes the fruit.

  3. Short answer What is double fertilisation?
    Show answer

    Two male gametes fuse: one with the egg (zygote) and one with polar nuclei (endosperm).
