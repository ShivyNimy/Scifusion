Levels and Patterns of Biodiversity

Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity. It is unevenly distributed and threatened.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Species diversity is highest in the tropics.
  • The species-area relationship shows more species in larger areas.
  • Biodiversity hotspots are regions of high endemism and high threat.
  • Losses are driven by habitat destruction, overexploitation, invasive species and pollution.
  • Extinction is a natural process, but human activity accelerates it.

Worked examples

Example 1 Species-area curve

Larger islands and habitat patches usually hold more species, following a log relationship.

Example 2 Invasive species

Water hyacinth spreads rapidly in Indian water bodies, crowding out native plants and harming aquatic life.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which region has the greatest species diversity?
    A. TropicsB. PolesC. DesertsD. Deep ocean only
    Show answer

    Answer: Tropics

    Tropical regions hold the highest species richness.

  2. MCQ A biodiversity hotspot has:
    A. High endemism and high threatB. Low endemismC. Only desertsD. No threat
    Show answer

    Answer: High endemism and high threat

    Hotspots combine richness with vulnerability.

  3. Short answer Name three causes of biodiversity loss.
    Show answer

    Habitat destruction, overexploitation and invasive species (also pollution and climate change).
