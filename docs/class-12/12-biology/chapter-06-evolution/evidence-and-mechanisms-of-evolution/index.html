Evidence and Mechanisms of Evolution

Evolution is change in populations over time. Evidence comes from fossils, comparative anatomy and molecular data.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Fossils document extinct forms and transitional species.
  • Homologous structures indicate common ancestry; analogous structures indicate convergent evolution.
  • Natural selection is the main mechanism of adaptive evolution.
  • Mutation, recombination and migration generate genetic variation.
  • Genetic drift changes allele frequencies in small populations.

Worked examples

Example 1 Darwin's finches

Different beak shapes on the Galápagos Islands reflect adaptation to different food sources.

Example 2 Antibiotic resistance

Bacteria with resistance survive treatment and reproduce, so resistance spreads rapidly.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Homologous organs indicate:
    A. Common ancestryB. Convergent evolutionC. No evolutionD. Random mutation
    Show answer

    Answer: Common ancestry

    Similar structure in different species suggests shared origin.

  2. MCQ The main mechanism of adaptive evolution is:
    A. Natural selectionB. Genetic driftC. Mutation onlyD. Migration only
    Show answer

    Answer: Natural selection

    Selection favours traits that improve survival and reproduction.

  3. Short answer How does antibiotic resistance illustrate natural selection?
    Show answer

    Resistant bacteria survive treatment and reproduce, so the resistant trait increases in the population.
