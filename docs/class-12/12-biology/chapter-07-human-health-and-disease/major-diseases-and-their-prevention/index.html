Major Diseases and Their Prevention

Diseases differ in transmission and control. HIV, malaria, cancer and drug abuse are major health concerns.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • HIV attacks helper T cells, weakening immunity and leading to AIDS.
  • Malaria is transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes.
  • Cancer is uncontrolled cell division caused by genetic and environmental factors.
  • Drug and alcohol abuse damages health and society; awareness and support are important.
  • Prevention includes vaccination, sanitation, safe sex and vector control.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why HIV leads to AIDS

HIV destroys helper T cells, so the immune system loses coordination and opportunistic infections appear.

Example 2 Malaria control

Using mosquito nets, draining stagnant water and prompt treatment reduce malaria transmission.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Malaria is transmitted by:
    A. Female Anopheles mosquitoB. HouseflyC. Aedes aegyptiD. Tsetse fly
    Show answer

    Answer: Female Anopheles mosquito

    Anopheles mosquitoes transmit the malaria parasite.

  2. MCQ HIV primarily attacks:
    A. Helper T cellsB. Red blood cellsC. PlateletsD. Neurons
    Show answer

    Answer: Helper T cells

    Loss of helper T cells cripples immunity.

  3. Short answer Why is cancer called a disease of uncontrolled cell division?
    Show answer

    Control mechanisms fail, so cells divide continuously and can spread to other tissues.
