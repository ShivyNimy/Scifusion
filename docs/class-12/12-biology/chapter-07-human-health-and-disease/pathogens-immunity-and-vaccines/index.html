Pathogens, Immunity and Vaccines

Pathogens cause disease. The immune system defends the body through innate and acquired immunity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Innate immunity is non-specific and present from birth.
  • Acquired immunity is specific and develops after exposure.
  • Antibodies are produced by B cells; T cells kill infected cells.
  • Active immunity develops after infection or vaccination; passive immunity is transferred.
  • Vaccines train the immune system without causing disease.

Worked examples

Example 1 How a vaccine works

A weakened or inactivated pathogen triggers antibody production and memory cells, giving rapid protection on future exposure.

Example 2 Why colostrum matters

Breast milk contains antibodies that provide passive immunity to newborns.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Antibodies are produced by:
    A. B cellsB. T cellsC. Red blood cellsD. Platelets
    Show answer

    Answer: B cells

    B lymphocytes produce antibodies.

  2. MCQ Vaccination provides:
    A. Active acquired immunityB. Passive immunityC. Innate immunity onlyD. No immunity
    Show answer

    Answer: Active acquired immunity

    The immune system actively responds to the vaccine.

  3. Short answer Differentiate active and passive immunity.
    Show answer

    Active immunity is produced by the body after exposure; passive immunity is received ready-made from another source.
