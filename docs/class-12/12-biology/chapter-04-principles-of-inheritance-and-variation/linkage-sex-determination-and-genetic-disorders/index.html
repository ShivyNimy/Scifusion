Linkage, Sex Determination and Genetic Disorders

Genes on the same chromosome are linked. Sex-linked traits and chromosomal abnormalities show characteristic inheritance patterns.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Linked genes tend to be inherited together.
  • Sex determination in humans is XY: males XY, females XX.
  • Haemophilia and colour blindness are X-linked recessive disorders.
  • Down syndrome is caused by trisomy of chromosome 21.
  • Pedigree analysis traces inheritance through families.

Worked examples

Example 1 Haemophilia inheritance

A carrier mother (XHXh) has a 50% chance of an affected son (XhY) with each pregnancy.

Example 2 Down syndrome

An extra copy of chromosome 21 causes developmental differences; risk increases with maternal age.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Colour blindness is:
    A. X-linked recessiveB. Autosomal dominantC. Y-linked onlyD. Mitochondrial
    Show answer

    Answer: X-linked recessive

    The gene is on the X chromosome.

  2. MCQ Down syndrome is due to:
    A. Trisomy of chromosome 21B. Monosomy of 21C. Sex chromosome lossD. Mutation in one gene
    Show answer

    Answer: Trisomy of chromosome 21

    Three copies of chromosome 21 cause the condition.

  3. Short answer Why are more males affected by X-linked recessive disorders?
    Show answer

    Males have only one X chromosome, so a single recessive allele is expressed.
