Mendelian Genetics and Deviations

Inheritance follows patterns described by Mendel. Some traits deviate from simple dominance.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Law of dominance, segregation and independent assortment describe Mendelian inheritance.
  • Incomplete dominance blends traits (pink flowers in snapdragon).
  • Codominance expresses both alleles (AB blood group).
  • Multiple alleles exist for blood groups (IA, IB, i).
  • Pleiotropy: one gene affects many traits.

Worked examples

Example 1 Blood group inheritance

A child of parents with blood groups A (IAi) and B (IBi) can be AB (IAIB), A, B or O.

Example 2 Incomplete dominance

Red and white snapdragons cross to give pink F1 flowers because neither allele completely dominates.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ In incomplete dominance, the F1 phenotype is:
    A. IntermediateB. DominantC. RecessiveD. Absent
    Show answer

    Answer: Intermediate

    Neither allele masks the other fully.

  2. MCQ AB blood group is an example of:
    A. CodominanceB. Incomplete dominanceC. PleiotropyD. Epistasis
    Show answer

    Answer: Codominance

    Both A and B antigens are expressed.

  3. Short answer State Mendel's law of independent assortment.
    Show answer

    Genes for different traits are inherited independently of each other during gamete formation.
