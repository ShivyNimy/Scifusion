The Gaseous State and the SI Units

Gases are highly compressible and diffuse rapidly. They exert pressure and have measurable properties related to temperature, volume and amount.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Air is a mixture of gases; it can be squeezed into a small cylinder because of large inter-particle spaces.
  • Gas pressure is due to collisions of particles with container walls.
  • The SI unit of temperature is the kelvin; 0 °C = 273.15 K.
  • The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa).
  • Diffusion of gases is fast because their particles move quickly and randomly.

Worked examples

Example 1 LPG in a cylinder

LPG is filled as a liquid under high pressure. On release it vaporises, expanding hugely, which is why a small cylinder supplies a lot of gas.

Example 2 Smell spreading through a room

The smelly particles move randomly and collide with air molecules, spreading through the room by diffusion.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ 0 °C equals:
    A. 273.15 KB. 100 KC. -100 KD. 373 K
    Show answer

    Answer: 273.15 K

    Kelvin temperature = Celsius + 273.15.

  2. MCQ Gases are highly compressible because:
    A. There is a lot of space between particlesB. Particles are heavyC. Particles are solidD. Particles are charged
    Show answer

    Answer: There is a lot of space between particles

    Large inter-particle spaces allow compression.

  3. Short answer Convert 27 °C to kelvin.
    Show answer

    27 + 273.15 = 300.15 K.
