States of Matter and Interconversion

Matter exists as solid, liquid or gas depending on the arrangement and energy of particles. Heating and cooling convert one state into another.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Particles of matter have spaces between them and are in constant motion; their kinetic energy increases with temperature.
  • Melting (solid -> liquid), boiling/vaporisation (liquid -> gas), condensation (gas -> liquid) and freezing (liquid -> solid) are interconversions.
  • Sublimation is a direct solid-to-gas change (camphor, naphthalene, dry ice).
  • Latent heat of fusion is the heat needed to change 1 kg of solid to liquid at its melting point.
  • Evaporation is a surface phenomenon; it increases with temperature, surface area, wind speed and decreases with humidity.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why we feel cool after sweating

Sweat absorbs latent heat from the skin as it evaporates, so the body cools. On a humid day evaporation is slow, so we feel sticky and hotter.

Example 2 Water at 0 °C versus ice at 0 °C

Ice at 0 °C absorbs latent heat to melt, so it cools a drink more effectively than the same mass of water at 0 °C.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which state has the highest kinetic energy of particles?
    A. GasB. LiquidC. SolidD. All are equal
    Show answer

    Answer: Gas

    Gas particles move fastest and have the most kinetic energy.

  2. MCQ The heat required to change 1 kg of ice at 0 °C into water at 0 °C is called:
    A. Latent heat of fusionB. Specific heatC. Latent heat of vaporisationD. Thermal energy
    Show answer

    Answer: Latent heat of fusion

    Latent heat of fusion melts the ice without changing temperature.

  3. Short answer Why does evaporation cause cooling?
    Show answer

    The fastest particles escape as vapour, taking away energy and lowering the average kinetic energy (temperature) of what remains.
