Momentum, Impulse and Conservation

Momentum is mass times velocity. In the absence of external forces, total momentum of a system is conserved.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • p = mv, with units kg·m/s.
  • Impulse = force × time = change in momentum.
  • Momentum is conserved in collisions and explosions.
  • A longer contact time reduces the force for the same momentum change — the principle behind airbags.
  • Recoil of a gun follows conservation of momentum.

Worked examples

Example 1 Airbag physics

An airbag increases the time over which a passenger's momentum drops to zero, reducing the force on the body.

Example 2 A gun and bullet

A 2 kg gun fires a 0.02 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Recoil velocity = (0.02 × 400) ÷ 2 = 4 m/s backwards, so total momentum stays zero.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The SI unit of momentum is:
    A. kg·m/sB. NC. JD. m/s
    Show answer

    Answer: kg·m/s

    Momentum is mass times velocity, kg·m/s.

  2. MCQ Momentum is conserved when:
    A. No external force actsB. Gravity is strongC. Friction is largeD. Temperature rises
    Show answer

    Answer: No external force acts

    Conservation holds for isolated systems.

  3. Short answer Why are airbags safer than rigid dashboards?
    Show answer

    They increase the collision time, reducing the force on the passenger.
