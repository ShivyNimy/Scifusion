Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's three laws describe how forces change motion. Inertia, momentum and action-reaction are the key ideas.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • First law: an object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by an external force (law of inertia).
  • Second law: F = ma; force equals mass times acceleration.
  • Third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Momentum p = mv; force is the rate of change of momentum.
  • Inertia depends on mass: heavier objects are harder to start or stop.

Worked examples

Example 1 Seat belts and the first law

When a car stops suddenly, passengers continue moving forward because of inertia. Seat belts provide the restraining force.

Example 2 Recoil of a gun

The gun pushes the bullet forward and the bullet pushes the gun backwards with an equal force; the lighter bullet therefore has the greater acceleration.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Newton's second law is written as:
    A. F = maB. F = m/aC. F = a/mD. F = mv
    Show answer

    Answer: F = ma

    Force equals mass times acceleration.

  2. MCQ A body at rest stays at rest unless:
    A. An external force actsB. It gets hotC. Its mass changesD. Time passes
    Show answer

    Answer: An external force acts

    This is the law of inertia.

  3. Short answer Calculate the force needed to accelerate a 5 kg mass at 3 m/s2.
    Show answer

    F = ma = 5 × 3 = 15 N.
