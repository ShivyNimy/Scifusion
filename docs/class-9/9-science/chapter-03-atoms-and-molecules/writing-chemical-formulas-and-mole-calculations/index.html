Writing Chemical Formulas and Mole Calculations

Chemical formulas show the elements and their ratio. The mole connects mass, number of particles and volume of a gas.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A molecule of a compound is shown by its formula, such as H2O.
  • Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses of the atoms in a formula.
  • Number of moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • Molar volume of a gas at STP is 22.4 L.
  • Percentage composition = (mass of element ÷ molar mass) × 100.

Worked examples

Example 1 Moles in 5.6 g of iron

Atomic mass of Fe is 56, so moles = 5.6 ÷ 56 = 0.1 mol, which is 6.022 × 1022 atoms.

Example 2 Mass of one molecule of water

Molar mass 18 g contains 6.022 × 1023 molecules, so one molecule has mass ≈ 3 × 10-23 g.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Number of moles in 36 g of water (molar mass 18) is:
    A. 2B. 1C. 18D. 36
    Show answer

    Answer: 2

    Moles = 36 ÷ 18 = 2.

  2. MCQ Volume of 1 mole of an ideal gas at STP is:
    A. 22.4 LB. 11.2 LC. 1 LD. 44.8 L
    Show answer

    Answer: 22.4 L

    One mole occupies 22.4 L at STP.

  3. Short answer Calculate the percentage of oxygen in water.
    Show answer

    Oxygen mass = 16 out of 18, so (16/18) × 100 ≈ 88.9%.
