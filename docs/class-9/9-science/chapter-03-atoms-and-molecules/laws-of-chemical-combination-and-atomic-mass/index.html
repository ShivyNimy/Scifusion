Laws of Chemical Combination and Atomic Mass

Chemical reactions follow the laws of conservation of mass and constant proportions. Atoms are the smallest particles of elements; molecules are groups of atoms bonded together.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Law of conservation of mass: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
  • Law of constant proportions: a compound always contains its elements in a fixed mass ratio.
  • Atomic mass is measured relative to carbon-12.
  • 1 atomic mass unit (u) is one-twelfth the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
  • The mole is the amount of substance containing Avogadro's number (6.022 × 1023) of particles.

Worked examples

Example 1 Conservation of mass

When 1.2 g of carbon burns in oxygen, it forms 4.4 g of carbon dioxide. The oxygen used is 4.4 − 1.2 = 3.2 g, so total mass is conserved.

Example 2 Molar mass of water

2 × 1 + 16 = 18 g/mol, so 18 g of water contains 6.022 × 1023 molecules.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Avogadro's number is:
    A. 6.022 × 1023B. 3.14 × 108C. 9.8 × 102D. 1.6 × 10-19
    Show answer

    Answer: 6.022 × 1023

    One mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 1023 particles.

  2. MCQ The law of conservation of mass was proposed by:
    A. LavoisierB. DaltonC. NewtonD. Einstein
    Show answer

    Answer: Lavoisier

    Antoine Lavoisier established the law of conservation of mass.

  3. Short answer Calculate the molar mass of CO2.
    Show answer

    C = 12, O = 16, so 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 g/mol.
