Mixtures, Solutions and Colloids

Matter can be classified as pure substances (elements and compounds) or mixtures (homogeneous and heterogeneous).

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A homogeneous mixture has a uniform composition throughout, for example salt solution.
  • A heterogeneous mixture has visible different parts, for example sand and iron filings.
  • A colloid has particles dispersed in a medium that do not settle, such as milk or fog.
  • A suspension has particles that settle on standing, such as muddy water.
  • The Tyndall effect: colloids scatter a beam of light, solutions do not.

Worked examples

Example 1 Classifying milk

Milk looks uniform but its fat droplets are dispersed in water and scatter light; it is a colloid. When it sours, protein clumps and settles, forming a suspension.

Example 2 Separating salt and sand

Dissolve the mixture, filter off the sand, then evaporate the filtrate to recover salt. The method combines two techniques based on solubility.

  2. MCQ Which mixture shows the Tyndall effect?

    Answer: Colloid

    Colloidal particles scatter light.

  3. Short answer Distinguish between a solution and a suspension.

    In a solution the solute is dissolved and does not settle; a suspension has larger particles that settle on standing.
