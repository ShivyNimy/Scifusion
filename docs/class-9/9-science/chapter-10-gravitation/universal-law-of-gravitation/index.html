Universal Law of Gravitation

Every object attracts every other object with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • F = G m1m2/r2, where G = 6.674 × 10-11 N·m2/kg2.
  • Gravitational force is always attractive and acts along the line joining the centres.
  • The force is weak unless one mass is enormous, which is why we notice gravity only for planets and stars.
  • Weight is the gravitational force on an object: W = mg.
  • Mass is constant; weight changes with the gravitational field.

Worked examples

Example 1 Weight on the Moon

g on the Moon is about 1.6 m/s2. A 60 kg astronaut has the same mass but a weight of only about 96 N, versus 588 N on Earth.

Example 2 Doubling the distance

If the distance between two masses doubles, the gravitational force becomes one-quarter of its original value, since F ∝ 1/r2.

    Answer: 6.674 × 10-11 N·m2/kg2

    G is the universal gravitational constant.

  2. MCQ Weight of a 10 kg object on Earth (g = 9.8) is:
    A. 98 NB. 10 NC. 9.8 ND. 980 N
    Show answer

    Answer: 98 N

    W = mg = 10 × 9.8 = 98 N.
