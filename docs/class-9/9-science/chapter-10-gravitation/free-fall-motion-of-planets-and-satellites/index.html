Free Fall, Motion of Planets and Satellites

Objects fall with acceleration g. Orbital motion is a balance between gravity and tangential velocity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Free fall acceleration near Earth's surface is g ≈ 9.8 m/s2.
  • Equations of motion apply with a = g for vertical motion.
  • Kepler's laws describe planetary orbits: elliptical paths and equal areas in equal times.
  • A satellite stays in orbit because its tangential speed keeps it falling around Earth.
  • Escape velocity from Earth is about 11.2 km/s.

Worked examples

Example 1 Time to fall

A stone dropped from a 20 m tower takes t = √(2s/g) = √(40/9.8) ≈ 2.02 s to reach the ground.

Example 2 Why astronauts float

The spacecraft and astronauts are in continuous free fall around Earth, so they experience weightlessness relative to the cabin.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ g near Earth's surface is about:
    A. 9.8 m/s2B. 1.6 m/s2C. 3 × 108 m/sD. 6.674 × 10-11
    Show answer

    Answer: 9.8 m/s2

    Earth's surface gravity is about 9.8 m/s2.

  2. MCQ Escape velocity from Earth is about:
    A. 11.2 km/sB. 9.8 km/sC. 3 km/sD. 100 km/s
    Show answer

    Answer: 11.2 km/s

    An object needs about 11.2 km/s to escape Earth's gravity.

  3. Short answer Why does a satellite not fall to Earth?
    Show answer

    Its high tangential velocity makes it fall around Earth rather than into it.
