Nature of Sound and Its Propagation

Sound is a mechanical wave produced by vibrating bodies. It requires a medium and travels at different speeds in different materials.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Sound waves are longitudinal; particles vibrate along the direction of travel.
  • Speed of sound in air at 0 °C is about 331 m/s; it increases with temperature.
  • Sound travels faster in solids than in liquids and gases.
  • Frequency (Hz) determines pitch; amplitude determines loudness.
  • Audible range for humans is about 20 Hz to 20 kHz; ultrasound is above 20 kHz.

Worked examples

Example 1 Counting to find distance

Sound travels about 340 m/s in air. If thunder arrives 4 s after lightning, the storm is about 4 × 340 = 1360 m away.

Example 2 Bats and ultrasound

Bats emit ultrasound and listen for echoes to locate insects — echolocation. Sonar uses the same principle in water.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Sound waves are:
    A. LongitudinalB. TransverseC. ElectromagneticD. Light waves
    Show answer

    Answer: Longitudinal

    Sound waves cause compressions and rarefactions along the direction of travel.

  2. MCQ Ultrasound has frequency:
    A. Above 20 kHzB. Below 20 HzC. Between 20 Hz and 20 kHzD. Exactly 20 Hz
    Show answer

    Answer: Above 20 kHz

    Ultrasound lies above the human audible range.

  3. Short answer Why can't sound travel through a vacuum?
    Show answer

    Sound needs particles to vibrate and carry energy; a vacuum has no particles.
