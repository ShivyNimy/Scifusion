Animal Husbandry and Fisheries

Animal husbandry provides milk, meat, eggs and fibre through breeding, feeding and disease control, while fisheries and beekeeping add income.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Cattle breeds: milch (dairy) and draught (work); cross-breeding improves yield.
  • Poultry farming produces eggs and meat; housing and vaccination improve survival.
  • Fish farming (aquaculture) combines capture and culture fisheries.
  • Bee keeping (apiculture) yields honey and wax and supports pollination.
  • Disease control and proper nutrition reduce losses.

Worked examples

Example 1 Cross-breeding for milk

Crossing an indigenous cow with a high-yield breed produces offspring with better milk yield and local adaptability.

Example 2 Apiculture and crops

Placing beehives near orchards improves pollination and fruit set while producing honey.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Rearing of honeybees is called:
    A. ApicultureB. PiscicultureC. SericultureD. Horticulture
    Show answer

    Answer: Apiculture

    Apiculture is beekeeping.

  2. MCQ Which of these improves milk yield in cattle?
    A. Cross-breedingB. Less waterC. More noiseD. Less feed
    Show answer

    Answer: Cross-breeding

    Selective breeding improves productivity.

  3. Short answer Name two products of apiculture.
    Show answer

    Honey and beeswax (and pollination service).
