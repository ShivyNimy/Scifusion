Crop Improvement and Management

Food production can be raised by better varieties, nutrients, water and pest management. Sustainable practices protect soil and water.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Plant breeding develops high-yield, disease-resistant and short-duration varieties.
  • Macronutrients (N, P, K) and micronutrients (B, Zn, Cu, Mn) are needed in small amounts.
  • Manure adds organic matter; fertilisers supply concentrated nutrients but can pollute.
  • Crop rotation with legumes restores nitrogen.
  • Integrated pest management uses biological and chemical control together.

Worked examples

Example 1 Green revolution

High-yield dwarf wheat and rice varieties combined with irrigation and fertiliser raised production sharply in the 1960s, showing the impact of improved varieties.

Example 2 Mixed cropping

Growing a legume with a cereal uses soil nitrogen more efficiently and spreads risk if one crop fails.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which nutrient helps in forming proteins in plants?
    A. NitrogenB. PotassiumC. CalciumD. Iron
    Show answer

    Answer: Nitrogen

    Nitrogen is essential for protein and chlorophyll synthesis.

  2. MCQ Manure is a:
    A. Organic source of nutrientsB. Chemical fertiliserC. PesticideD. Herbicide
    Show answer

    Answer: Organic source of nutrients

    Manure is decomposed organic matter.

  3. Short answer Why is crop rotation with legumes beneficial?
    Show answer

    Legumes fix atmospheric nitrogen, improving soil fertility and breaking pest cycles.
