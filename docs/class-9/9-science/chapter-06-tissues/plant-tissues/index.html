Plant Tissues

Plants have meristematic tissues for growth and permanent tissues for support, transport and photosynthesis.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Meristematic tissue is found at growing tips (apical), in stems (lateral/cambium) and at the base of leaves.
  • Parenchyma has thin walls and stores food; collenchyma provides flexibility; sclerenchyma is dead and rigid.
  • Xylem carries water and minerals upward; phloem carries food in both directions.
  • Epidermis protects and forms root hairs; cork is a dead protective outer layer.
  • Simple tissues have one cell type; complex tissues (xylem, phloem) have several.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why a tall tree can stand

Sclerenchyma fibres and lignified xylem walls give strength and rigidity, while xylem vessels carry water up the trunk.

Example 2 Wood and bark

Wood is largely secondary xylem; bark is cork and phloem at the outside, protecting the tree and moving food.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which tissue transports food in plants?
    A. PhloemB. XylemC. ParenchymaD. Sclerenchyma
    Show answer

    Answer: Phloem

    Phloem carries sugars from leaves to other parts.

  2. MCQ Meristematic tissue is responsible for:
    A. GrowthB. TransportC. ProtectionD. Storage
    Show answer

    Answer: Growth

    It contains actively dividing cells.

  3. Short answer Differentiate xylem and phloem.
    Show answer

    Xylem carries water and minerals upward and is mostly dead; phloem carries food in both directions and is living.
