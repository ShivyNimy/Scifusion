Prevention and Treatment of Disease

Preventing disease is better than treating it. Vaccination, hygiene, safe water and vector control reduce transmission; medicines target specific pathogens.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Vaccines train the immune system by exposing it to weakened or killed pathogens.
  • Antibiotics work against bacteria, not viruses.
  • Clean water, handwashing and safe food prevent waterborne and foodborne diseases.
  • Mosquito nets and draining stagnant water reduce malaria and dengue.
  • Health infrastructure and awareness are as important as medicines.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why antibiotics do not cure a cold

The common cold is caused by viruses, and antibiotics act on bacterial structures that viruses do not have.

Example 2 Two-dose immunisation

Some vaccines need two doses to build a strong memory of the pathogen, so completing the schedule matters for immunity.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Antibiotics are effective against:
    A. BacteriaB. VirusesC. All pathogensD. Only fungi
    Show answer

    Answer: Bacteria

    Antibiotics target bacterial structures and processes.

  2. MCQ A vaccine works by:
    A. Training the immune systemB. Killing all bacteriaC. Replacing bloodD. Curing allergies
    Show answer

    Answer: Training the immune system

    It creates memory cells that respond quickly to infection.

  3. Short answer Name three measures to prevent communicable diseases.
    Show answer

    Vaccination, safe drinking water and good hygiene (also vector control).
