Cell Structure and Function

The cell is the basic unit of life. Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus; eukaryotic cells have membrane-bound organelles.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Plasma membrane is selectively permeable and controls transport.
  • Nucleus contains DNA, the genetic material, and controls cell activities.
  • Mitochondria are the powerhouse and make ATP through respiration.
  • Ribosomes synthesise proteins; the ER and Golgi complex process and transport materials.
  • Lysosomes digest waste and worn-out organelles; plastids in plants carry pigments.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why muscle cells have many mitochondria

Muscle contraction needs large amounts of ATP, so muscle cells contain many mitochondria to produce it.

Example 2 Plant versus animal cell

Plant cells have a rigid cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole; animal cells lack a wall and chloroplasts and have small scattered vacuoles.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which organelle is called the powerhouse of the cell?
    A. MitochondrionB. NucleusC. RibosomeD. Golgi body
    Show answer

    Answer: Mitochondrion

    Mitochondria release energy as ATP.

  2. MCQ Prokaryotic cells lack:
    A. A membrane-bound nucleusB. A cell membraneC. RibosomesD. Cytoplasm
    Show answer

    Answer: A membrane-bound nucleus

    Their DNA is not enclosed in a nucleus.

  3. Short answer Why is the plasma membrane called selectively permeable?
    Show answer

    It allows only certain substances to pass in or out of the cell.
