Classification and the Five Kingdoms

Biodiversity is enormous, so organisms are classified into groups based on shared features. Whittaker proposed five kingdoms.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Monera (bacteria), Protista (unicellular eukaryotes), Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
  • Classification uses cell type, body organisation, mode of nutrition and reproduction.
  • Binomial nomenclature gives each species a genus and species name in Latin, e.g. Homo sapiens.
  • Hierarchy: kingdom, phylum (division), class, order, family, genus, species.
  • Species is the basic unit of classification.

Worked examples

Example 1 Where does a mushroom belong?

Mushrooms are fungi: eukaryotic, heterotrophic with cell walls of chitin, and they absorb nutrients from dead matter.

Example 2 Naming a mango

Mangifera indica — Mangifera is the genus and indica the species, written in italics with the genus capitalised.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which kingdom includes bacteria?
    A. MoneraB. ProtistaC. FungiD. Plantae
    Show answer

    Answer: Monera

    Bacteria are prokaryotes in kingdom Monera.

  2. MCQ Binomial nomenclature uses:
    A. Genus and speciesB. Kingdom and phylumC. Class and orderD. Family and genus
    Show answer

    Answer: Genus and species

    The scientific name has two parts: genus and species.

  3. Short answer Write the hierarchy of classification from kingdom to species.
    Show answer

    Kingdom -> phylum -> class -> order -> family -> genus -> species.
