Plant and Animal Groups

Plants are grouped into thallophytes, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms and angiosperms. Animals are grouped into invertebrates and vertebrates.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Thallophytes (algae) have no true roots, stems or leaves.
  • Bryophytes are amphibians of the plant kingdom and need water for reproduction.
  • Pteridophytes have vascular tissue and reproduce by spores.
  • Gymnosperms have naked seeds (pine, cycas); angiosperms have flowers and enclosed seeds.
  • Vertebrates: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why ferns need moist places

Ferns (pteridophytes) release sperm that swim through water to reach the egg, so they reproduce only in damp conditions.

Example 2 Mammals versus birds

Mammals have hair, mammary glands and give birth to young (mostly); birds have feathers, beaks and lay eggs.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which group has naked seeds?
    A. GymnospermsB. AngiospermsC. PteridophytesD. Bryophytes
    Show answer

    Answer: Gymnosperms

    Gymnosperm seeds are not enclosed in a fruit.

  2. MCQ Which vertebrate group has moist skin and lays eggs in water?
    A. AmphibiansB. ReptilesC. BirdsD. Mammals
    Show answer

    Answer: Amphibians

    Amphibians such as frogs depend on water for reproduction.

  3. Short answer Name the five vertebrate groups.
    Show answer

    Fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.
