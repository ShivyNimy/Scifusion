Describing Motion

Motion is described by displacement, velocity and acceleration. These quantities allow precise predictions of speed and position.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Distance is the total path length; displacement is the shortest straight-line change in position.
  • Speed = total distance ÷ time; velocity = displacement ÷ time.
  • Velocity has direction, so it is a vector; speed is a scalar.
  • Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time.
  • Uniform motion means constant velocity; non-uniform means changing velocity.

Worked examples

Example 1 A runner on a circular track

One lap around a 400 m track covers 400 m of distance but zero displacement, because the runner returns to the start.

Example 2 Car acceleration

A car goes from 0 to 20 m/s in 5 s: acceleration = (20 − 0) ÷ 5 = 4 m/s2.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Displacement can be:
    A. Zero while distance is not zeroB. Always equal to distanceC. Negative onlyD. A scalar
    Show answer

    Answer: Zero while distance is not zero

    On a closed loop displacement is zero, though distance is not.

  2. MCQ The SI unit of acceleration is:
    A. m/s2B. m/sC. mD. s
    Show answer

    Answer: m/s2

    Acceleration is velocity change per unit time.

  3. Short answer A cyclist covers 20 km in 1 hour. Find the average speed.
    Show answer

    Average speed = total distance ÷ total time = 20 ÷ 1 = 20 km/h.
