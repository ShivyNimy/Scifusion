Equations of Motion and Graphs

For uniformly accelerated motion, three equations link velocity, displacement, acceleration and time.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at2
  • v2 = u2 + 2as
  • The area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement; the slope gives acceleration.
  • In a distance-time graph, the slope gives speed.

Worked examples

Example 1 A stone dropped from rest

With u = 0 and a = 9.8 m/s2, after 2 s the velocity is v = 0 + 9.8 × 2 = 19.6 m/s, and distance s = ½ × 9.8 × 4 = 19.6 m.

Example 2 Braking distance

With u = 20 m/s, v = 0 and a = −4 m/s2: v2 = u2 + 2as gives 0 = 400 − 8s, so s = 50 m.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The area under a velocity-time graph gives:
    A. DisplacementB. AccelerationC. ForceD. Speed
    Show answer

    Answer: Displacement

    Area under v-t graph = displacement.

  2. MCQ If v = u + at and u = 0, a = 2 m/s2, t = 3 s, then v is:
    A. 6 m/sB. 9 m/sC. 3 m/sD. 1.5 m/s
    Show answer

    Answer: 6 m/s

    v = 0 + 2 × 3 = 6 m/s.

  3. Short answer A train accelerates from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 10 s. Find the acceleration.
    Show answer

    a = (30 − 10) ÷ 10 = 2 m/s2.
