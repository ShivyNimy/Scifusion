Electronic Configuration and Valency

Electrons fill shells in a fixed order (2, 8, 8 …). The number of electrons in the outermost shell determines chemical behaviour and valency.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The maximum number of electrons in a shell is 2n2.
  • Shells are filled from the inside out (K, L, M, N).
  • Atoms gain, lose or share electrons to achieve a stable outer shell (octet).
  • Valency is the combining capacity of an element.
  • Ions form when atoms lose or gain electrons.

Worked examples

Example 1 Configuration of magnesium

Magnesium has 12 electrons: 2 in K, 8 in L and 2 in M. Losing 2 electrons gives a stable configuration, so its valency is +2.

Example 2 Sodium chloride

Sodium (2, 8, 1) loses one electron to become Na+; chlorine (2, 8, 7) gains one to become Cl−; the ions attract and form NaCl.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The maximum number of electrons in the L shell is:
    A. 8B. 2C. 18D. 32
    Show answer

    Answer: 8

    The L shell holds up to 8 electrons.

  2. MCQ Valency is:
    A. The combining capacity of an elementB. Number of neutronsC. Atomic massD. Number of shells
    Show answer

    Answer: The combining capacity of an element

    Valency reflects how many electrons an atom can gain, lose or share.

  3. Short answer Write the electronic configuration of chlorine and state its valency.
    Show answer

    2, 8, 7; valency 1 (it gains one electron).
