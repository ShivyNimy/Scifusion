Subatomic Particles and Atomic Models

Atoms are made of protons, neutrons and electrons. Successive models — Thomson, Rutherford and Bohr — refined our picture of atomic structure.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Protons are positive, electrons negative and neutrons neutral.
  • Atomic number = number of protons; mass number = protons + neutrons.
  • Rutherford's gold foil experiment showed that most of the atom is empty space with a tiny dense nucleus.
  • Bohr proposed that electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed energy shells.
  • Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reading the symbol of sodium

Sodium has atomic number 11 and mass number 23, so it has 11 protons, 11 electrons and 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

Example 2 Isotopes of hydrogen

Protium (1 proton, 0 neutrons), deuterium (1 proton, 1 neutron) and tritium (1 proton, 2 neutrons) are isotopes with the same chemistry but different masses.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The mass number of an atom is:
    A. Protons + neutronsB. Protons onlyC. Neutrons onlyD. Electrons only
    Show answer

    Answer: Protons + neutrons

    Mass number counts protons plus neutrons.

  2. MCQ Which experiment led to the discovery of the nucleus?
    A. Rutherford's gold foil experimentB. Thomson's cathode ray experimentC. Millikan's oil dropD. Bohr's model
    Show answer

    Answer: Rutherford's gold foil experiment

    Alpha particles scattering showed a small dense nucleus.

  3. Short answer What are isotopes? Give one example.
    Show answer

    Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons; for example chlorine-35 and chlorine-37.
