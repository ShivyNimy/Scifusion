Conservation of Energy and Sources

Energy can change form but is never created or destroyed. Renewable and non-renewable sources differ in how long they last.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Potential energy converts to kinetic energy as an object falls, and back again as it rises.
  • Friction converts mechanical energy into heat, which is still energy but less useful.
  • Renewable sources: solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass.
  • Non-renewable sources: coal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear fuel.
  • Energy efficiency means getting more useful work from the same energy input.

Worked examples

Example 1 Pendulum energy

At the highest point of a swing the bob has maximum potential energy and zero speed; at the lowest point it has maximum kinetic energy and minimum potential energy.

Example 2 Hydroelectric power

Water stored in a dam has potential energy; falling water spins turbines, converting it into electricity through a generator.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Energy is:
    A. Conserved, only changed in formB. Destroyed during workC. Created by machinesD. Always kinetic
    Show answer

    Answer: Conserved, only changed in form

    Total energy stays constant, though its form changes.

  2. MCQ Which is a renewable source?
    A. WindB. CoalC. PetroleumD. Natural gas
    Show answer

    Answer: Wind

    Wind is replenished naturally.

  3. Short answer Why is a machine never 100% efficient?
    Show answer

    Some energy is always lost as heat due to friction and other effects.
