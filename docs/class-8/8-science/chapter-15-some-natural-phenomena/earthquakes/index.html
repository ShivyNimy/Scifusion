Earthquakes

An earthquake is the sudden shaking of the Earth's crust due to movement along faults. Its energy travels as seismic waves.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Earthquakes occur mainly at plate boundaries where tectonic plates collide, separate or slide.
  • The Richter scale measures magnitude; each step of 1 is about 32 times more energy.
  • Seismographs record earthquakes.
  • Safety: drop, cover and hold on; stay away from windows and weak structures.
  • Early warning systems use seismic and satellite data.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why India has earthquakes

The Indian plate pushes north into the Eurasian plate, building stress in the Himalayan region. When the stress releases suddenly, an earthquake occurs.

Example 2 Building design matters

Flexible foundations and reinforced structures can sway with the ground shaking instead of collapsing.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Earthquakes are measured on the:
    A. Richter scaleB. Celsius scaleC. Beaufort scaleD. pH scale
    Show answer

    Answer: Richter scale

    Magnitude is measured on the Richter scale.

  2. MCQ Earthquakes are most common at:
    A. Plate boundariesB. City centresC. DesertsD. Lakes
    Show answer

    Answer: Plate boundaries

    Most earthquakes occur where tectonic plates meet.

  3. Short answer What should you do during an earthquake?
    Show answer

    Drop, cover under a sturdy table and hold on; stay away from windows and heavy objects.
