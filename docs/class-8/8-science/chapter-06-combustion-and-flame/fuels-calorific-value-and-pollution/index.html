Fuels, Calorific Value and Pollution

Fuels release energy when burnt. Calorific value measures the energy per unit mass, and exhaust gases pollute air and cause acid rain.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Good fuel: high calorific value, low cost, low smoke, easy storage and transport.
  • Ideal fuel conditions: high energy per kg, no harmful residue and moderate ignition temperature.
  • Burning fuels produces carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
  • Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides cause acid rain.
  • Alternative fuels: LPG, CNG, biogas, hydrogen, ethanol and electricity.

Worked examples

Example 1 Calorific value comparison

A fuel with a high calorific value releases more energy per kilogram, so less fuel is needed. Hydrogen has the highest calorific value per unit mass but is difficult to store.

Example 2 CNG in city buses

CNG burns more cleanly than diesel, reducing soot and particulate pollution in crowded cities.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The unit of calorific value is:
    A. kJ/kgB. kg/kJC. °CD. m/s
    Show answer

    Answer: kJ/kg

    Calorific value is energy released per kilogram.

  2. MCQ Acid rain is mainly caused by:
    A. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxidesB. OxygenC. Water vapourD. Nitrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides

    These oxides dissolve in rain water to form acids.

  3. Short answer What is a good fuel?
    Show answer

    A fuel that has high calorific value, low pollution, and is cheap and easy to store and transport.
