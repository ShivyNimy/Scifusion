Fossil Fuels and Their Formation

Coal and petroleum are fossil fuels formed from ancient living matter over millions of years. They are exhaustible resources.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Coal formed from compressed plant remains under heat and pressure.
  • Petroleum formed from marine organisms buried in sediments.
  • Fractional distillation separates petroleum into petrol, diesel, kerosene, lubricating oil and bitumen.
  • Exhaustible resources are limited and will run out; inexhaustible resources such as sunlight are not used up.
  • Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas.

Worked examples

Example 1 Refining petroleum

Crude oil is heated in a fractionating column. Different fractions condense at different temperatures: gas at the top, then petrol, kerosene, diesel and bitumen at the bottom.

Example 2 Coal tar and coke

Heating coal in the absence of air gives coke and coal tar. Coke is used in steel making and coal tar in dyes and medicines.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which resource is exhaustible?
    A. CoalB. SunlightC. WindD. Tidal energy
    Show answer

    Answer: Coal

    Coal is finite and cannot be replenished on human timescales.

  2. MCQ Petroleum is refined by:
    A. Fractional distillationB. FiltrationC. EvaporationD. Condensation
    Show answer

    Answer: Fractional distillation

    Fractions separate by boiling point in a fractionating column.

  3. Short answer Why are fossil fuels called non-renewable?
    Show answer

    They formed over millions of years and are being used much faster than they form.
