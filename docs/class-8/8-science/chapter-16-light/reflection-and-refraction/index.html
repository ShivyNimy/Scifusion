Reflection and Refraction

Light reflects from surfaces and bends when it passes from one medium to another. These two behaviours explain mirrors, lenses and optical illusions.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Laws of reflection: angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, in the same plane.
  • Refraction occurs because light changes speed in a new medium.
  • Light bends towards the normal when entering a denser medium, away when leaving it.
  • Plane mirrors produce virtual images; curved mirrors can produce real or virtual images.
  • A prism splits white light into a spectrum by refraction.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why a straw looks bent in water

Light from the submerged part bends at the water surface before reaching the eye, so the straw appears displaced — refraction.

Example 2 The periscope

Two plane mirrors at 45° reflect light twice, allowing a submarine to see above the water surface.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Bending of light when it changes medium is:
    A. RefractionB. ReflectionC. DispersionD. Absorption
    Show answer

    Answer: Refraction

    Refraction is the change in direction due to a change in speed.

  2. MCQ The image in a plane mirror is:
    A. Virtual and erectB. Real and invertedC. MagnifiedD. Not formed
    Show answer

    Answer: Virtual and erect

    Plane mirrors give upright virtual images.

  3. Short answer State the two laws of reflection.
    Show answer

    Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection; incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
