Deforestation and Its Consequences

Deforestation is the clearing of forests, which destroys habitat and disrupts the water cycle, soil and climate.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Causes: agriculture, housing, industry, roads, mining, fire and logging.
  • Consequences: habitat loss, soil erosion, floods, reduced rainfall, carbon dioxide rise and species extinction.
  • Deforestation increases the risk of desertification and landslides.
  • Reforestation and afforestation restore forests and habitats.
  • Endemic species live only in a particular area and are most vulnerable.

Worked examples

Example 1 A landslide after the trees are cut

Tree roots grip the soil and slow rainwater. Without roots, water saturates the slope and soil slides away — a direct, visible consequence of deforestation.

Example 2 The red panda

The red panda lives only in eastern Himalayan forests and is endangered because bamboo habitat is being cleared. It is an endemic species.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Clearing of forests is called:
    A. DeforestationB. AfforestationC. ConservationD. Reforestation
    Show answer

    Answer: Deforestation

    Deforestation is the removal of forest cover.

  2. MCQ Animals found only in a specific area are called:
    A. EndemicB. ExtinctC. DomesticD. Migratory
    Show answer

    Answer: Endemic

    Endemic species are restricted to a specific region.

  3. Short answer State two consequences of deforestation.
    Show answer

    Habitat loss and increased soil erosion and floods.
