Protected Areas and Wildlife Conservation

India protects biodiversity through national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves, and through laws that ban hunting and illegal trade.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A national park conserves the whole ecosystem; a sanctuary protects particular species.
  • The Wildlife Protection Act (1972) protects wildlife in India.
  • Project Tiger protects tigers and their habitats.
  • Biosphere reserves allow conservation together with sustainable human activity.
  • Species are classified as endangered, vulnerable or extinct based on risk.

Worked examples

Example 1 Kaziranga and the rhino

Kaziranga National Park protects the one-horned rhinoceros in Assam. Patrols, grassland management and tourism fees have helped numbers recover.

Example 2 Why community participation matters

Bird sanctuaries and community reserves work best when local people see income or pride in protecting wildlife, such as in the Chilika lagoon.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which law protects wildlife in India?
    A. Wildlife Protection Act 1972B. Factories ActC. Forest Survey ActD. Environment Act 1986
    Show answer

    Answer: Wildlife Protection Act 1972

    The Wildlife Protection Act provides legal protection to wildlife.

  2. MCQ Project Tiger was launched to:
    A. Protect tigers and their habitatB. Count treesC. Plant cropsD. Tour forests
    Show answer

    Answer: Protect tigers and their habitat

    It conserves tiger populations and their ecosystems.

  3. Short answer Differentiate a national park from a wildlife sanctuary.
    Show answer

    A national park conserves the whole ecosystem; a sanctuary mainly protects specific species and allows some regulated activities.
