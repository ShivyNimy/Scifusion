Uses of Metals and Non-metals

Properties decide uses. Metals make structures, wires and utensils; non-metals are vital in life, fertilisers and industry.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Iron and steel: construction, machines and vehicles.
  • Copper and aluminium: electrical wiring; aluminium is light and corrosion-resistant.
  • Gold and silver: jewellery because they are unreactive and lustrous.
  • Non-metals: oxygen for breathing, nitrogen in fertilisers, carbon in steel, chlorine in water treatment.
  • Alloys combine metals to improve properties, for example stainless steel (iron, chromium, nickel).

Worked examples

Example 1 Aluminium in aircraft

Aluminium is light and strong enough when alloyed, and resists corrosion by forming an oxide layer, so it is ideal where weight matters.

Example 2 Noble metals in jewellery

Gold is unreactive and does not tarnish, so it keeps its shine. Pure gold is soft, so it is alloyed with copper or silver for strength.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which metal is used for electrical wiring because of good conductivity?
    A. CopperB. SulphurC. IodineD. Carbon
    Show answer

    Answer: Copper

    Copper conducts electricity and is ductile enough to draw into wire.

  2. MCQ Stainless steel is an alloy of:
    A. Iron, chromium and nickelB. Copper and zincC. Aluminium and leadD. Gold and silver
    Show answer

    Answer: Iron, chromium and nickel

    Chromium and nickel improve strength and rust resistance.

  3. Short answer Give one use each of oxygen and nitrogen.
    Show answer

    Oxygen: respiration and medical support; nitrogen: fertilisers and food packaging.
