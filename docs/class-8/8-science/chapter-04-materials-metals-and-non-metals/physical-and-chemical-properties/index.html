Physical and Chemical Properties

Metals are typically shiny, hard, malleable, ductile and good conductors. Non-metals are dull, brittle and poor conductors. Their reactions reveal their reactivity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Metals: iron, copper, aluminium, zinc, gold, silver.
  • Non-metals: oxygen, sulphur, carbon, phosphorus, chlorine, nitrogen.
  • Metals react with oxygen to form basic oxides; non-metals form acidic oxides.
  • Metals react with acids to give hydrogen; gold and silver do not react with dilute acids.
  • Metals are sonorous; non-metals are not.

Worked examples

Example 1 Metal oxides and litmus

Burn magnesium in air to get magnesium oxide, then dissolve it in water. The solution turns red litmus blue, showing it is basic. Burning sulphur gives an acidic oxide.

Example 2 Why a metal spoon bends

Metals are malleable, so their layers can slide over each other without breaking. That is why metal sheets can be hammered into shape.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which of these is a non-metal?
    A. SulphurB. CopperC. AluminiumD. Zinc
    Show answer

    Answer: Sulphur

    Sulphur is a non-metal, brittle and a poor conductor.

  2. MCQ Metals react with dilute acids to produce:
    A. HydrogenB. OxygenC. NitrogenD. Chlorine
    Show answer

    Answer: Hydrogen

    Hydrogen gas is displaced from the acid.

  3. Short answer Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene?
    Show answer

    It is so reactive that it catches fire in air; kerosene keeps out air and moisture.
