Sound: Vibration, Medium and Propagation

Sound is produced by vibrating objects and travels as waves through a medium. It cannot travel through a vacuum.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Vibration is the to-and-fro motion of particles of a source.
  • Sound needs a medium: solids, liquids and gases all transmit sound, but a vacuum cannot.
  • Sound travels fastest in solids and slowest in gases.
  • Compressions and rarefactions make up a sound wave.
  • An echo is a reflected sound heard after a delay.

Worked examples

Example 1 The thunder delay

Light from lightning reaches us almost instantly, but sound takes about 3 seconds per kilometre. Counting seconds until thunder gives a rough distance of the storm.

Example 2 A bell in a vacuum jar

As air is pumped out of the jar, the ringing becomes fainter and finally inaudible, showing sound needs a medium.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Sound cannot travel through:
    A. A vacuumB. AirC. WaterD. Steel
    Show answer

    Answer: A vacuum

    A vacuum has no particles to carry the vibration.

  2. MCQ Sound travels fastest in:
    A. SteelB. WaterC. AirD. A vacuum
    Show answer

    Answer: Steel

    Particles in solids are closest, so vibrations pass quickly.

  3. Short answer Why can you hear the sound of a distant train through rails before hearing it in air?
    Show answer

    Sound travels much faster through steel than through air.
