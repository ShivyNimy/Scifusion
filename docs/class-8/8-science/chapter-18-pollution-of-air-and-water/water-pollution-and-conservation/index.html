Water Pollution and Conservation

Water pollution is the contamination of water by domestic sewage, industrial waste, agricultural chemicals and plastic. Clean water is a limited resource.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Sources: sewage, industrial effluents, fertilisers and pesticides, oil spills, plastic waste.
  • Effects: waterborne diseases, death of aquatic life, eutrophication and poisoned groundwater.
  • Treatment removes solids, consumes organic matter with bacteria and disinfects the water.
  • Reduce, reuse and recycle; safe disposal of chemicals.
  • Water harvesting and watershed management protect supplies.

Worked examples

Example 1 Eutrophication

Excess fertiliser runs into a lake and fuels algal growth. When algae die, bacteria decompose them and use up oxygen, suffocating fish.

Example 2 Oil spills

Oil forms a film on the sea surface, blocking oxygen and light and coating birds' feathers, so they cannot float or keep warm.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Eutrophication is caused by:
    A. Excess nutrients from fertilisersB. Oil spills onlyC. Plastic onlyD. Salt only
    Show answer

    Answer: Excess nutrients from fertilisers

    Nutrient enrichment causes excessive algal growth.

  2. MCQ Which is a waterborne disease?
    A. CholeraB. RicketsC. ScurvyD. Night blindness
    Show answer

    Answer: Cholera

    Cholera spreads through contaminated water.

  3. Short answer State two ways to reduce water pollution.
    Show answer

    Treat sewage before release and avoid dumping industrial chemicals and plastic into water bodies.
