Air Pollution

Air pollution is the contamination of air by harmful substances from vehicles, industries and domestic activities. It harms health, plants and buildings.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Pollutants include carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, particulate matter and smoke.
  • Sources: vehicles, factories, burning of fossil fuels and biomass, dust and construction.
  • Effects: respiratory disease, acid rain, smog, greenhouse effect and ozone depletion.
  • Greenhouse gases trap heat; CFCs destroy ozone in the stratosphere.
  • Control: cleaner fuels, catalytic converters, filters, afforestation and public transport.

Worked examples

Example 1 The Taj Mahal and acid rain

Sulphur dioxide from nearby industries reacts with rain to form acid, which attacks the marble of the Taj Mahal. Cleaner fuels and restrictions on polluting factories help protect it.

Example 2 Why smog forms over cities

Pollutants and moisture combine under still, cool conditions to form smog, reducing visibility and irritating lungs.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which gas causes acid rain?
    A. Sulphur dioxideB. OxygenC. NitrogenD. Helium
    Show answer

    Answer: Sulphur dioxide

    Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides dissolve in rain to form acids.

  2. MCQ CFCs harm the:
    A. Ozone layerB. Soil onlyC. Water onlyD. Rocks
    Show answer

    Answer: Ozone layer

    Chlorofluorocarbons break down ozone molecules.

  3. Short answer Name three sources of air pollution.
    Show answer

    Vehicles, factories and burning of fossil fuels (also dust and biomass burning).
