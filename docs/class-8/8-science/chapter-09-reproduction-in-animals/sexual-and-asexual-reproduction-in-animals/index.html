Sexual and Asexual Reproduction in Animals

Animals reproduce sexually by the fusion of gametes, or asexually from a single parent. Sexual reproduction creates variation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Male gametes are sperm; female gametes are eggs (ova).
  • Fertilisation may be external (frogs, fish) or internal (humans, birds).
  • Viviparous animals give birth to young; oviparous animals lay eggs.
  • Asexual methods include binary fission (Amoeba), budding (Hydra) and regeneration (Planaria).
  • In humans, the zygote develops inside the uterus for about nine months.

Worked examples

Example 1 Frog versus hen

Frogs lay eggs in water and fertilise them externally, while hens lay shelled eggs that are fertilised internally and incubated outside the body.

Example 2 Hydra budding

A bud grows on the side of a Hydra, develops tentacles and detaches as a new individual — one parent, no fertilisation.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Fertilisation in frogs is:
    A. ExternalB. InternalC. AbsentD. In the uterus
    Show answer

    Answer: External

    Eggs and sperm meet in water.

  2. MCQ Animals that give birth to young are called:
    A. ViviparousB. OviparousC. AsexualD. Budding
    Show answer

    Answer: Viviparous

    Viviparous animals develop the embryo inside the mother.

  3. Short answer Why is sexual reproduction advantageous to a species?
    Show answer

    It produces genetic variation, helping the population adapt to changing conditions.
