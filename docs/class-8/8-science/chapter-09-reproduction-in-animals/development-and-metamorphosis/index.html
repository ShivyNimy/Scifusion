Development and Metamorphosis

After fertilisation, the zygote develops into an embryo. Some animals change form dramatically during development.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The zygote divides repeatedly to form an embryo, which develops organs in the uterus.
  • The placenta exchanges oxygen, nutrients and wastes between mother and embryo.
  • Metamorphosis transforms a larva into an adult, as in frogs and butterflies.
  • Tadpoles have gills and tails; adult frogs have lungs and legs.
  • Care of young increases survival in many species.

Worked examples

Example 1 Frog metamorphosis

Egg -> tadpole (gills, tail) -> froglet -> adult frog. Each stage is adapted to a different way of life.

Example 2 Placenta in mammals

The placenta lets nutrients and oxygen reach the embryo from the mother's blood and removes wastes.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The change from larva to adult is called:
    A. MetamorphosisB. FertilisationC. BuddingD. Fragmentation
    Show answer

    Answer: Metamorphosis

    Metamorphosis is a dramatic change in body form.

  2. MCQ A tadpole breathes using:
    A. GillsB. LungsC. SpiraclesD. Skin only
    Show answer

    Answer: Gills

    Tadpoles have gills for aquatic life.

  3. Short answer What is the function of the placenta?
    Show answer

    It allows exchange of oxygen, nutrients and wastes between the mother and the developing embryo.
