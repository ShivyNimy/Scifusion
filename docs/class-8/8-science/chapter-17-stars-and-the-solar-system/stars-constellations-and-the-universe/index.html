Stars, Constellations and the Universe

Stars are self-luminous balls of gas. The Sun is our nearest star. Distant stars are grouped into constellations that help navigation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A light year is the distance light travels in one year.
  • The Moon is Earth's natural satellite; it revolves around Earth in about 27 days.
  • Constellations such as Ursa Major (Great Bear) and Orion help locate stars.
  • Phases of the Moon arise from the changing angle of sunlight on its face.
  • The Universe contains billions of galaxies, each with billions of stars.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why the Moon shows phases

As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different fractions of its sunlit half: new moon, crescent, half, gibbous and full moon.

Example 2 Using the Pole Star

The Pole Star appears nearly fixed in the northern sky and lies close to the north direction, so travelers have used it for navigation.

  3. Short answer What is a constellation? Give one example.
