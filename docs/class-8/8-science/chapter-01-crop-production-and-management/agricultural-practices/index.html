Agricultural Practices

Growing a crop involves a sequence of practices: preparing the soil, sowing, adding manure and fertilisers, irrigating, protecting from weeds and pests, harvesting and storing.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Ploughing loosens and aerates the soil so roots can grow and breathe.
  • Sowing should place seeds at the right depth and spacing.
  • Manure is organic and improves soil texture; fertilisers are chemical nutrient concentrates.
  • Irrigation supplies water; excessive irrigation can waterlog the soil and damage crops.
  • Weeds compete with crops for water, nutrients and light and must be removed (weeding).

Worked examples

Example 1 Manure versus fertiliser

Manure adds humus and improves water retention but has low nutrient concentration. Fertilisers deliver nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium quickly but can pollute water if overused.

Example 2 Why crop rotation works

Growing a legume such as gram after wheat restores nitrogen through Rhizobium, breaking pest cycles and improving soil fertility without extra fertiliser.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which practice loosens and aerates the soil?
    A. PloughingB. HarvestingC. WinnowingD. Storage
    Show answer

    Answer: Ploughing

    Ploughing turns and loosens the soil.

  2. MCQ Fertilisers are mainly:
    A. Chemical nutrient saltsB. Decomposed plantsC. PesticidesD. Seeds
    Show answer

    Answer: Chemical nutrient salts

    They are concentrated mineral nutrients, usually N, P and K.

  3. Short answer Why should weeds be removed from a crop field?
    Show answer

    Weeds compete with crops for water, nutrients, light and space, reducing yield.
