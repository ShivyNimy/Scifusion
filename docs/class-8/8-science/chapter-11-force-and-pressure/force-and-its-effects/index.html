Force and Its Effects

A force is a push or pull that can change an object's speed, direction or shape. Forces are described by magnitude and direction.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Contact forces: muscular force, friction, tension and normal force.
  • Non-contact forces: magnetic, electrostatic and gravitational force.
  • A force can change the state of motion, the direction of motion or the shape of an object.
  • Force is measured in newtons (N).
  • Resultant force: balanced forces do not change motion; unbalanced forces do.

Worked examples

Example 1 The hockey hit

A hockey stick applies a force to the ball, changing both its speed and direction. Because the ball is light, the same force gives it a large acceleration.

Example 2 Stretching a rubber band

The band stretches when pulled and returns when released. A force has changed its shape; the amount of stretch shows the size of the force.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ Which is a non-contact force?
    A. Magnetic forceB. FrictionC. Muscular forceD. Tension
    Show answer

    Answer: Magnetic force

    Magnetic force acts without touching.

  2. MCQ Force is measured in:
    A. NewtonsB. JoulesC. WattsD. Pascals
    Show answer

    Answer: Newtons

    The SI unit of force is the newton.

  3. Short answer State two effects a force can have on an object.
    Show answer

    It can change the object's shape and change its speed or direction of motion.
