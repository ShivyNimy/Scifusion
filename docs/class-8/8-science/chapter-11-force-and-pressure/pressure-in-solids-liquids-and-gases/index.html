Pressure in Solids, Liquids and Gases

Pressure is force per unit area. Solids exert pressure on surfaces, liquids exert pressure at a depth, and gases exert pressure in all directions.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Pressure = force ÷ area; the SI unit is the pascal (Pa).
  • A sharp knife cuts better because the same force acts on a smaller area, giving higher pressure.
  • Liquids exert pressure on the walls and bottom of the container, and pressure increases with depth.
  • Gases exert pressure equally in all directions; atmospheric pressure acts on everything.
  • A syringe uses pressure differences to draw in or push out liquid.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why snowshoes work

Snowshoes spread body weight over a large area, reducing pressure, so the wearer does not sink into soft snow.

Example 2 A leaking dam

Water pressure increases with depth, so the base of a dam is built thicker and stronger than the top.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Pressure is:
    A. Force ÷ areaB. Force × areaC. Area ÷ forceD. Force × distance
    Show answer

    Answer: Force ÷ area

    Pressure is force per unit area.

  2. MCQ Liquid pressure ______ with depth.
    A. IncreasesB. DecreasesC. Stays constantD. Becomes zero
    Show answer

    Answer: Increases

    More liquid above means more weight pressing down.

  3. Short answer Why does a sharp knife cut better than a blunt one?
    Show answer

    It has a smaller area of contact, so the same force produces greater pressure.
