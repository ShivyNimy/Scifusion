Applications and Environmental Effects

Electrolytic processes are used industrially to purify metals and coat surfaces, but they also consume energy and can create waste.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Electrorefining purifies copper and other metals used in wiring.
  • Electroplating protects metals from corrosion and improves appearance.
  • The electrolyte can be toxic and must be treated before disposal.
  • Electrolysis of brine produces chlorine, hydrogen and sodium hydroxide, all industrially important.
  • Efficient processes reduce energy use and pollution.

Worked examples

Example 1 Copper refining

Impure copper is the anode and pure copper is the cathode. Copper ions move through the solution and deposit as pure copper on the cathode.

Example 2 Chrome plating on taps

A thin layer of chromium gives taps a shiny, corrosion-resistant surface that is harder than the metal beneath.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Pure copper is obtained by:
    A. ElectrorefiningB. FilteringC. SmeltingD. Condensing
    Show answer

    Answer: Electrorefining

    Ions of copper deposit on the cathode, leaving impurities behind.

  2. MCQ Electroplating is used to:
    A. Prevent corrosion and improve appearanceB. Increase weightC. Magnetise metalsD. Soften metals
    Show answer

    Answer: Prevent corrosion and improve appearance

    A thin metal coating protects and beautifies the object.

  3. Short answer Name two industries that use electrolysis.
    Show answer

    Metal refining and electroplating (also chlorine manufacture).
