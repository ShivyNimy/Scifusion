Conductors, Electrolytes and Electrolysis

Electric current can flow through some liquids, called electrolytes, and produce chemical changes. This is electrolysis.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Distilled water is a poor conductor; adding salt or acid makes it conduct.
  • Electrolytes contain ions that carry current.
  • Electrolysis of water produces hydrogen at the cathode and oxygen at the anode.
  • Electroplating deposits a metal layer on an object using electrolysis.
  • Chemical effects have many uses: refining metals, coating jewellery and making chlorine.

Worked examples

Example 1 Testing liquids

Connect a simple circuit with a bulb through various liquids. Lemon juice, salt water and vinegar make the bulb glow; oil and distilled water do not.

Example 2 Electroplating a spoon

The spoon is the cathode, a silver bar is the anode and silver salt solution is the electrolyte. Silver ions deposit on the spoon, giving it a shiny silver coating.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which liquid conducts electricity best?
    A. Salt waterB. Distilled waterC. Vegetable oilD. Sugar solution
    Show answer

    Answer: Salt water

    Dissolved salts provide ions that carry current.

  2. MCQ Electroplating uses:
    A. ElectrolysisB. FiltrationC. DistillationD. Evaporation
    Show answer

    Answer: Electrolysis

    Metal ions are deposited by electric current.

  3. Short answer Why does distilled water not conduct electricity while tap water does?
    Show answer

    Tap water contains dissolved salts that provide ions; distilled water has almost no ions.
