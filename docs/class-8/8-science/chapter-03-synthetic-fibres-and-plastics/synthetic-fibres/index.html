Synthetic Fibres

Synthetic fibres are made from chemicals, usually from petroleum. Rayon, nylon, polyester and acrylic are common, each with distinct properties and uses.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Rayon is semisynthetic, made from wood pulp treated with chemicals; it is cheap and absorbs water.
  • Nylon is strong, elastic and light; it is used in ropes, parachutes, toothbrush bristles and clothing.
  • Polyester is wrinkle-resistant, strong and quick-drying.
  • Acrylic is warm, soft and often used as a wool substitute in blankets.
  • Synthetic fibres do not absorb water well, so they dry quickly but can trap sweat.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why nylon ropes are strong

Long polymer chains in nylon pack closely and resist pulling, making nylon stronger than many natural fibres for a given thickness.

Example 2 Cotton versus polyester in summer

Cotton absorbs sweat and allows air through, so it feels cool. Polyester traps moisture, so it feels sticky in hot weather.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Rayon is made from:
    A. Wood pulpB. PetroleumC. CoalD. Cotton seed
    Show answer

    Answer: Wood pulp

    Rayon is a semisynthetic fibre derived from cellulose in wood pulp.

  2. MCQ Which synthetic fibre is strongest and most elastic for ropes?
    A. NylonB. AcrylicC. RayonD. Wool
    Show answer

    Answer: Nylon

    Nylon has high tensile strength and elasticity.

  3. Short answer Why are synthetic clothes uncomfortable in summer?
    Show answer

    They do not absorb sweat readily, so moisture stays trapped against the skin.
