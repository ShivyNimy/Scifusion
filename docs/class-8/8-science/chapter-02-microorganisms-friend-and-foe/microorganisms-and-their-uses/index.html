Microorganisms and Their Uses

Microorganisms are living things too small to see with the naked eye: bacteria, fungi, protozoa and algae. Many are useful in food, medicine and agriculture.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Lactobacillus turns milk into curd; yeast makes bread and alcohol; moulds ripen cheese.
  • Antibiotics such as penicillin come from fungi and bacteria and kill or stop bacteria.
  • Vaccines use weakened or killed pathogens to train the immune system.
  • Rhizobium fixes nitrogen in legume root nodules; some bacteria decompose waste.
  • Microorganisms are used to make vitamins, enzymes, biogas and sewage treatment.

Worked examples

Example 1 Curd from milk

A spoon of curd added to warm milk provides Lactobacillus bacteria. They convert milk sugar into lactic acid, which coagulates the protein and turns milk into curd.

Example 2 Penicillin's discovery

Alexander Fleming noticed that a mould on a petri dish stopped bacteria from growing. That mould, Penicillium, became the source of the first antibiotic, penicillin.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which microorganism turns milk into curd?
    A. LactobacillusB. YeastC. AmoebaD. Rhizobium
    Show answer

    Answer: Lactobacillus

    Lactobacillus bacteria produce lactic acid.

  2. MCQ Antibiotics are used to treat:
    A. Bacterial infectionsB. Viral infectionsC. All diseasesD. Injuries
    Show answer

    Answer: Bacterial infections

    Antibiotics act on bacteria, not viruses.

  3. Short answer Name two useful products made with the help of microorganisms.
    Show answer

    Curd (Lactobacillus) and bread (yeast); also antibiotics and biogas.
