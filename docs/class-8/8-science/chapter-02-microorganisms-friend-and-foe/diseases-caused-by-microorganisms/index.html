Diseases Caused by Microorganisms

Some microorganisms are pathogens. They spread through air, water, food, contact and vectors, and cause diseases in humans, animals and plants.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Human diseases: tuberculosis and cholera (bacteria), common cold and COVID-19 (viruses), malaria (protozoan), ringworm (fungus).
  • Plant diseases: citrus canker (bacteria), rust of wheat (fungus), mosaic disease (virus).
  • Malaria spreads through the bite of a female Anopheles mosquito; dengue through Aedes.
  • Prevention: vaccination, clean water, handwashing, covering food, vector control.
  • Food preservation (boiling, refrigeration, salting, drying, canning) slows or stops microbial growth.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why boiling kills germs

High temperature denatures the proteins and enzymes of pathogens, so they cannot survive. Boiling water for a minute makes it much safer to drink.

Example 2 Vaccination works by training

A vaccine exposes the immune system to a harmless part of a pathogen. The body makes antibodies and memory cells, so a real infection is fought off quickly.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Malaria is caused by a:
    A. ProtozoanB. VirusC. FungusD. Bacterium
    Show answer

    Answer: Protozoan

    Plasmodium, a protozoan, causes malaria, spread by Anopheles mosquitoes.

  2. MCQ Which food preservation method removes water?
    A. DryingB. FreezingC. BoilingD. Refrigeration
    Show answer

    Answer: Drying

    Drying removes water that microorganisms need to grow.

  3. Short answer Name two ways to prevent the spread of communicable diseases.
    Show answer

    Vaccination and safe drinking water (also handwashing and vector control).
