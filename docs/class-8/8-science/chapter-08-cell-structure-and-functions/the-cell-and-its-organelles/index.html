The Cell and Its Organelles

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Organisms are unicellular or multicellular, and plant and animal cells differ in important ways.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Cell membrane controls entry and exit of substances; cytoplasm holds organelles; nucleus contains chromosomes.
  • Mitochondria release energy; ribosomes make proteins; vacuoles store water and waste.
  • Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole, while animal cells have neither cell wall nor chloroplasts.
  • Unicellular organisms such as Amoeba carry out all life processes in one cell.
  • Cells specialize by shape and function, for example nerve cells, muscle cells and red blood cells.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why plant cells stay firm

The rigid cell wall supports the plant and resists water pressure, so leaves and stems stay upright. Animal cells lack a wall and rely on a flexible membrane.

Example 2 Nerve cell shape

A nerve cell has long projections to carry electrical signals quickly over distances, showing that cell shape matches function.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The control centre of the cell is the:
    A. NucleusB. MitochondrionC. Cell membraneD. Vacuole
    Show answer

    Answer: Nucleus

    The nucleus contains the genetic material that directs cell activity.

  2. MCQ Which structure is present in plant cells but not in animal cells?
    A. Cell wallB. NucleusC. MitochondriaD. Cell membrane
    Show answer

    Answer: Cell wall

    The cellulose cell wall gives plants shape and rigidity.

  3. Short answer Why is the cell called the structural and functional unit of life?
    Show answer

    It is the smallest unit that can carry out all life processes and makes up all living things.
