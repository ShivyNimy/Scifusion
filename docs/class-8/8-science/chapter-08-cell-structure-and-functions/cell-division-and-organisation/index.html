Cell Division and Organisation

Cells divide to grow and reproduce. In multicellular organisms, cells are organised into tissues, organs, organ systems and the whole organism.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Mitosis produces identical cells for growth and repair.
  • Meiosis produces gametes with half the chromosome number.
  • Tissues are groups of similar cells performing a specific function.
  • Organs are made of tissues (for example, the stomach), and organ systems of organs (for example, the digestive system).
  • In unicellular organisms, cell division is reproduction.

Worked examples

Example 1 From cell to organism

Muscle cells form muscle tissue, muscle tissue plus other tissues forms the heart, the heart works with blood vessels as the circulatory system, and the systems form the organism.

Example 2 Why cells must stay small

A small cell has a large surface area relative to its volume, so exchange of gases and nutrients is fast enough. Very large cells cannot supply their interior efficiently.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Mitosis is used for:
    A. Growth and repairB. Producing gametesC. DigestionD. Respiration
    Show answer

    Answer: Growth and repair

    Mitosis produces identical daughter cells.

  2. MCQ A group of similar cells performing a specific function is a:
    A. TissueB. OrganC. Organ systemD. Organism
    Show answer

    Answer: Tissue

    Tissues are groups of similar cells.

  3. Short answer Arrange in order of organisation: organ, cell, organ system, tissue.
    Show answer

    Cell -> tissue -> organ -> organ system.
