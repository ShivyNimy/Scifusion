Friction in Nature and Technology

Friction shapes the design of tyres, shoes, aircraft and sports equipment, and also causes energy loss in machines.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Treaded tyres increase friction on wet roads and prevent skidding.
  • Streamlining reduces fluid friction for fish, aircraft and high-speed trains.
  • Friction between tectonic plates can cause earthquakes when they suddenly slip.
  • The heat from friction is used in matches and rubbers.
  • Polishing surfaces reduces friction, so more force is needed to grip them.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why racing cars have wide, smooth tyres

Wide tyres have a larger contact area, and the smooth surface maximises grip on dry tracks; in rain, grooves clear water to keep contact. Design balances friction with speed.

Example 2 Streamlining a cyclist

Tucking into an aerodynamic posture cuts air resistance at speed, allowing the cyclist to go faster with the same effort.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Treaded tyres are used to:
    A. Increase friction and gripB. Reduce frictionC. Reduce weightD. Increase speed
    Show answer

    Answer: Increase friction and grip

    Treads push water away and improve grip on wet roads.

  2. MCQ Streamlining reduces:
    A. Fluid frictionB. WeightC. TemperatureD. Volume
    Show answer

    Answer: Fluid friction

    A streamlined shape lets air or water flow smoothly around an object.

  3. Short answer Why is friction called a necessary evil?
    Show answer

    It causes wear and wastes energy, but without it we could not walk, hold objects or stop vehicles.
