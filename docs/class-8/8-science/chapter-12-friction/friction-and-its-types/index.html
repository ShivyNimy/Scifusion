Friction and Its Types

Friction opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact. It can be static, sliding, rolling or fluid friction.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Static friction prevents a stationary object from starting to move.
  • Sliding friction acts when an object slides; rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction.
  • Fluid friction acts on objects moving through liquids or gases.
  • Friction produces heat and causes wear, so oil and lubricants reduce it.
  • Friction is useful: we could not walk or hold objects without it.

Worked examples

Example 1 Ball bearings

Ball bearings replace sliding contact with rolling contact, greatly reducing friction. That is why machines with bearings run smoothly and need less energy.

Example 2 Why shoes wear out

Repeated contact with rough ground rubs away material, and friction converts motion into heat. Soles are thick to last longer.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The friction that acts when a body rolls is:
    A. Rolling frictionB. Static frictionC. Sliding frictionD. Fluid friction
    Show answer

    Answer: Rolling friction

    Rolling friction is much less than sliding friction.

  2. MCQ Friction can be reduced by:
    A. Using lubricantsB. Increasing weightC. Making surfaces roughD. Increasing speed
    Show answer

    Answer: Using lubricants

    Lubricants create a thin layer that separates surfaces.

  3. Short answer Give two examples where friction is useful.
    Show answer

    Walking on the ground and writing with a pen (also braking a bicycle).
