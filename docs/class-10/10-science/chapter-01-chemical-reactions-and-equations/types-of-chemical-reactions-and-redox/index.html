Types of Chemical Reactions and Redox

Reactions are classified by how atoms rearrange. Oxidation involves loss of electrons or gain of oxygen; reduction is the reverse.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Combination: A + B -> AB, such as CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2.
  • Decomposition: AB -> A + B, such as electrolysis of water.
  • Displacement: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from its salt.
  • Double displacement: ions swap partners, often forming a precipitate.
  • In redox, oxidation and reduction happen together.

Worked examples

Example 1 Displacement in a test tube

Put an iron nail in copper sulphate solution; the blue colour fades and a brown copper coating appears because iron is more reactive and displaces copper.

Example 2 Rusting as a redox reaction

Iron is oxidised to iron oxide while oxygen is reduced. Moist air speeds up both halves of the process.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which is a decomposition reaction?
    A. CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2B. CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2C. Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + CuD. NaOH + HCl -> NaCl + H2O
    Show answer

    Answer: CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2

    One compound splits into simpler substances.

  2. MCQ In the reaction Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu, zinc is:
    A. OxidisedB. ReducedC. UnchangedD. A catalyst
    Show answer

    Answer: Oxidised

    Zinc loses electrons and is oxidised while copper ions are reduced.

  3. Short answer Why is the reaction between iron and copper sulphate a displacement reaction?
    Show answer

    Iron is more reactive than copper, so it displaces copper from its salt solution.
