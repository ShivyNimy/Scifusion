Writing and Balancing Chemical Equations

A chemical equation uses symbols and formulas to describe a reaction. Balancing shows that mass is conserved.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Reactants are on the left of the arrow and products on the right.
  • Physical states are shown as (s), (l), (g) and (aq).
  • A balanced equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.
  • Conditions such as heat, light or a catalyst are written above the arrow.
  • Combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement and redox are the main reaction types.

Worked examples

Example 1 Balancing hydrogen combustion

H2 + O2 -> H2O is balanced as 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O: four H and two O atoms on each side.

Example 2 Decomposition of calcium carbonate

CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2 on heating. One compound splits into a solid oxide and a gas.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ In a balanced chemical equation, the number of atoms of each element is:
    A. Equal on both sidesB. Greater on the reactant sideC. Greater on the product sideD. Not counted
    Show answer

    Answer: Equal on both sides

    Balancing conserves mass.

  2. MCQ The symbol (aq) means the substance is:
    A. Dissolved in waterB. A solidC. A gasD. A liquid metal
    Show answer

    Answer: Dissolved in water

    aq stands for aqueous solution.

  3. Short answer Balance: Fe + H2O -> Fe3O4 + H2.
    Show answer

    Three iron atoms and eight hydrogen atoms require 3Fe and 4H2O.
