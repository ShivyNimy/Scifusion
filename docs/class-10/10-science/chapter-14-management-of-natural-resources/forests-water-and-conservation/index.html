Forests, Water and Conservation

Natural resources must be used in ways that allow renewal. Community participation makes conservation effective.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Forests protect watersheds, soil and biodiversity and provide livelihoods.
  • Dams and canals help irrigation but displace people and affect river ecology.
  • Rainwater harvesting recharges groundwater.
  • Community forest management, such as joint forest management, can improve forest cover.
  • Sustainable use balances present needs with future availability.

Worked examples

Example 1 Chipko movement

Local villagers hugged trees to prevent felling, showing that conservation succeeds when people have a stake in the resource.

Example 2 Watershed management

Building check dams and planting trees in a catchment area slows runoff, recharges wells and improves water availability downstream.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which practice recharges groundwater?
    A. Rainwater harvestingB. Flood irrigationC. DeforestationD. Burning waste
    Show answer

    Answer: Rainwater harvesting

    Harvested rainwater seeps into the ground.

  2. MCQ The Chipko movement was related to:
    A. Protection of forestsB. MiningC. FishingD. Urbanisation
    Show answer

    Answer: Protection of forests

    People hugged trees to stop felling.

  3. Short answer State two benefits of forests.
    Show answer

    They prevent soil erosion and recharge groundwater (also support biodiversity).
