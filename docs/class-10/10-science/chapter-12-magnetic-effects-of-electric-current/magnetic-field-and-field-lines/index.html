Magnetic Field and Field Lines

A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field. Field lines show the direction and strength of the field.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A straight wire produces circular field lines around it; the right-hand thumb rule gives the direction.
  • A solenoid produces a field like a bar magnet.
  • Magnetic field lines never cross and are closer where the field is stronger.
  • An electromagnet uses a current in a coil around a soft iron core.
  • Field direction reverses when current direction reverses.

Worked examples

Example 1 Right-hand thumb rule

Point your right thumb along the current; your curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field around the wire.

Example 2 A doorbell electromagnet

Current through a coil magnetises a soft iron core that pulls a hammer; the movement breaks the circuit and the cycle repeats.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Magnetic field lines around a straight wire are:
    A. CircularB. StraightC. EllipticalD. Square
    Show answer

    Answer: Circular

    They form concentric circles around the wire.

  2. MCQ The direction of the magnetic field is given by:
    A. Right-hand thumb ruleB. Ohm's lawC. Boyle's lawD. Newton's law
    Show answer

    Answer: Right-hand thumb rule

    The thumb rule relates current direction to field direction.

  3. Short answer What is a solenoid?
    Show answer

    A coil of many turns of insulated wire that produces a uniform magnetic field like a bar magnet when current flows.
