Hormones and Plant Coordination

Hormones are chemical messengers that travel in the blood and regulate growth, metabolism and reproduction. Plants use hormones to respond to light and gravity.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Thyroxine regulates metabolism; insulin controls blood sugar; adrenaline prepares the body for emergencies.
  • Growth hormone controls growth; oestrogen and testosterone control sexual development.
  • Plants: auxins (growth and phototropism), gibberellins (stem elongation), cytokinins (cell division) and abscisic acid (stress and dormancy).
  • Tropisms are directional growth responses: phototropism, geotropism, hydrotropism and thigmotropism.
  • Nastic movements are non-directional, such as the folding of touch-me-not leaves.

Worked examples

Example 1 Adrenaline rush

During danger, adrenaline raises heart rate, opens airways and releases glucose, preparing muscles for fight or flight.

Example 2 Roots bending down

Roots grow away from light and towards gravity (positive geotropism) and towards water (hydrotropism), helping the plant reach water.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which hormone prepares the body for emergencies?
    A. AdrenalineB. InsulinC. ThyroxineD. Auxin
    Show answer

    Answer: Adrenaline

    Adrenaline triggers the fight-or-flight response.

  2. MCQ Growth of a shoot towards light is called:
    A. Positive phototropismB. Negative geotropismC. HydrotropismD. Nastic movement
    Show answer

    Answer: Positive phototropism

    Shoots bend towards light.

  3. Short answer Why is the endocrine system slower but longer-lasting than the nervous system?
    Show answer

    Hormones travel in the blood and act gradually but sustain effects for longer.
