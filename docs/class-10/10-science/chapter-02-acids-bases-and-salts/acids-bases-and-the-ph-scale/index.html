Acids, Bases and the pH Scale

Acids release H+ ions in solution; bases release OH− ions. Their strength is measured on the pH scale.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Acids turn blue litmus red; bases turn red litmus blue.
  • Strong acids (HCl, H2SO4) ionise completely; weak acids (acetic acid) ionise partially.
  • pH below 7 is acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic.
  • The pH scale is logarithmic: a change of 1 unit means a tenfold change in H+ concentration.
  • Neutralisation: acid + base -> salt + water.

Worked examples

Example 1 Tooth decay and pH

Bacteria in the mouth produce acid from sugar, lowering pH below 5.5 and dissolving enamel. Brushing with a basic toothpaste neutralises the acid.

Example 2 Why dilute acid is added to water, not water to acid

Dilution releases a lot of heat. Adding water to concentrated acid can make it spit and splash; adding acid slowly to water spreads the heat safely.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ A solution of pH 11 is:
    A. BasicB. AcidicC. NeutralD. Strongly acidic
    Show answer

    Answer: Basic

    pH above 7 indicates a base.

  2. MCQ Which acid is present in the stomach?
    A. Hydrochloric acidB. Sulphuric acidC. Nitric acidD. Acetic acid
    Show answer

    Answer: Hydrochloric acid

    Gastric juice contains HCl, which helps digest protein.

  3. Short answer What happens when a solution of an acid is mixed with a solution of a base?
    Show answer

    They neutralise each other to form salt and water.
