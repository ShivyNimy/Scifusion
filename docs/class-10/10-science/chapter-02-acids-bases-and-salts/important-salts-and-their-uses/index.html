Important Salts and Their Uses

Salts are formed by neutralisation and by reactions of acids with metals or carbonates. Many salts have daily uses.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Sodium chloride: cooking, preservation, raw material for chlorine and NaOH.
  • Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda): baking, antacid, fire extinguisher.
  • Washing soda (Na2CO3·10H2O): softening hard water, cleaning.
  • Bleaching powder: disinfecting water and bleaching cloth.
  • Plaster of Paris: making casts and decorative moulds.

Worked examples

Example 1 Baking soda in the kitchen

Baking soda plus an acid such as lemon juice releases carbon dioxide, which makes batter rise.

Example 2 Hard water and washing soda

Calcium and magnesium ions make water hard. Washing soda precipitates them so soap lathers easily.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Baking soda is chemically:
    A. Sodium bicarbonateB. Sodium carbonateC. Sodium chlorideD. Calcium carbonate
    Show answer

    Answer: Sodium bicarbonate

    NaHCO3 is sodium hydrogen carbonate.

  2. MCQ Plaster of Paris is:
    A. Calcium sulphate hemihydrateB. Calcium carbonateC. Sodium chlorideD. Calcium oxide
    Show answer

    Answer: Calcium sulphate hemihydrate

    Heating gypsum gives CaSO4·½H2O, Plaster of Paris.

  3. Short answer Why is baking powder used in cakes?
    Show answer

    It releases carbon dioxide when heated and moistened, making the batter rise and become fluffy.
