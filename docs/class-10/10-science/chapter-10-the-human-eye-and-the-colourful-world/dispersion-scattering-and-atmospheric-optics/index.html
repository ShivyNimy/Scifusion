Dispersion, Scattering and Atmospheric Optics

White light splits into colours when refracted at different angles. Scattering explains the blue sky and red sunsets.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Dispersion occurs because each colour has a different refractive index.
  • A rainbow forms when raindrops refract, disperse and internally reflect sunlight.
  • Rayleigh scattering: shorter wavelengths scatter more, making the sky blue.
  • At sunset, light travels farther and blue is scattered away, leaving red and orange.
  • Atmospheric refraction makes the Sun visible before actual sunrise.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why the sky is blue but sunsets are red

Blue light scatters more in all directions, so the sky looks blue. Near the horizon, light passes through more air, losing blue and leaving red.

Example 2 Advanced sunrise

Atmospheric refraction bends sunlight around the curve of Earth, so we see the Sun about two minutes before it geometrically rises.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The sky appears blue because of:
    A. Scattering of lightB. ReflectionC. DispersionD. Absorption
    Show answer

    Answer: Scattering of light

    Short wavelengths scatter most strongly.

  2. MCQ Which colour deviates most in a prism?
    A. VioletB. RedC. GreenD. Yellow
    Show answer

    Answer: Violet

    Violet has the highest refractive index.

  3. Short answer Why does the Sun appear reddish at sunrise and sunset?
    Show answer

    Light travels a longer path through the atmosphere, so blue light is scattered away and red reaches the eye.
