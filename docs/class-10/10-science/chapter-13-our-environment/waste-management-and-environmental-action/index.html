Waste Management and Environmental Action

Human activities generate waste that must be managed. Reduce, reuse, recycle and treat waste to protect the environment.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Biodegradable waste can be composted; non-biodegradable waste must be recycled or safely disposed.
  • Segregation at source makes recycling efficient.
  • Plastic pollution harms wildlife and enters food chains.
  • Sewage and industrial effluent must be treated before release.
  • Sustainable habits — saving energy and water, planting trees — reduce environmental pressure.

Worked examples

Example 1 A school compost pit

Fruit peels, leaves and paper are layered in a pit and turned periodically; after weeks they become compost for the garden, reducing landfill waste.

Example 2 Plastic in the ocean

Bags and bottles break into microplastics that are eaten by fish and can end up in human food.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which waste should be composted?
    A. Fruit peelsB. Plastic bagsC. Glass bottlesD. Metal cans
    Show answer

    Answer: Fruit peels

    Organic waste decomposes to compost.

  2. MCQ Segregation of waste means:
    A. Separating waste by typeB. Burning wasteC. Dumping wasteD. Burying waste
    Show answer

    Answer: Separating waste by type

    Separating biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste enables recycling.

  3. Short answer Why is plastic pollution a serious problem?
    Show answer

    Plastic is non-biodegradable, persists for centuries and harms wildlife and food chains.
