Ecosystems and Food Chains

An ecosystem includes living organisms and their physical environment interacting together. Energy flows through food chains; nutrients cycle.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Producers (plants) capture solar energy; consumers eat other organisms; decomposers break down waste.
  • Energy decreases at each trophic level: the 10% law.
  • A food web is a network of interconnected food chains.
  • Bioaccumulation is the build-up of non-biodegradable substances along a food chain.
  • Ozone depletion and greenhouse gases affect the whole biosphere.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why large predators are few

Only about 10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next, so top predators have much less energy available and their populations are smaller.

Example 2 DDT in food chains

DDT accumulates in fish and then in birds that eat them, causing thin eggshells — a classic case of bioaccumulation.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Energy passed from one trophic level to the next is about:
    A. 10%B. 50%C. 90%D. 1%
    Show answer

    Answer: 10%

    The ten per cent law describes energy transfer.

  2. MCQ Organisms that break down dead matter are:
    A. DecomposersB. ProducersC. Primary consumersD. Top carnivores
    Show answer

    Answer: Decomposers

    Bacteria and fungi decompose organic matter.

  3. Short answer What is a food chain? Give one example.
    Show answer

    A sequence of organisms in which each eats the previous one, e.g. grass -> grasshopper -> frog -> snake.
