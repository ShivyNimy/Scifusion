Mendel's Laws and Inheritance

Traits are passed from parents to offspring through genes. Mendel's experiments with pea plants established the basic laws of inheritance.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Each trait is controlled by a pair of alleles; one may be dominant, the other recessive.
  • Law of segregation: alleles separate during gamete formation.
  • Law of independent assortment: different traits are inherited independently (for unlinked genes).
  • Phenotype is the visible trait; genotype is the allele combination (TT, Tt, tt).
  • Sex determination in humans: XX female, XY male.

Worked examples

Example 1 Monohybrid cross

Crossing tall (TT) with dwarf (tt) pea plants gives all tall Tt offspring in F1, and a 3 tall : 1 dwarf ratio in F2.

Example 2 Why some traits skip a generation

A recessive allele is hidden in a heterozygous carrier but appears again when two carriers have offspring.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ In a monohybrid cross, the F2 ratio is:
    A. 3 : 1B. 1 : 1C. 9 : 3 : 3 : 1D. 2 : 1
    Show answer

    Answer: 3 : 1

    The phenotypic ratio is 3 dominant : 1 recessive.

  2. MCQ In humans, the male sex chromosomes are:
    A. XYB. XXC. YYD. XO
    Show answer

    Answer: XY

    Males have one X and one Y chromosome.

  3. Short answer State Mendel's law of segregation.
    Show answer

    The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one allele.
