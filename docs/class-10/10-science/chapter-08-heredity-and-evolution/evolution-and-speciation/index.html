Evolution and Speciation

Evolution is change in inherited characteristics of populations over generations. It is driven by variation, selection and isolation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Variation arises from mutation and sexual reproduction.
  • Natural selection favours traits that improve survival and reproduction.
  • Speciation happens when populations become reproductively isolated.
  • Evidence comes from fossils, homologous organs and molecular similarities.
  • Human evolution is traced through a series of hominid fossils.

Worked examples

Example 1 Peppered moths

During the Industrial Revolution, dark moths were better camouflaged on soot-covered trees and survived better, so their frequency rose — natural selection in action.

Example 2 Homologous organs

The forelimbs of humans, whales and bats have similar bone structure but different functions, indicating common ancestry.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Natural selection acts on:
    A. Variation within a populationB. A single individualC. Genes directlyD. Fossils
    Show answer

    Answer: Variation within a population

    Individuals with advantageous traits leave more offspring.

  2. MCQ Homologous organs indicate:
    A. Common ancestryB. Convergent evolutionC. No evolutionD. Random mutation
    Show answer

    Answer: Common ancestry

    Similar structure suggests a shared ancestor.

  3. Short answer Why does sexual reproduction speed up evolution?
    Show answer

    It creates more genetic variation, giving natural selection more material to act on.
