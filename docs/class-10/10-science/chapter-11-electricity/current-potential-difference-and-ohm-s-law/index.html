Current, Potential Difference and Ohm's Law

Electric current is the flow of charge. Potential difference drives current and resistance opposes it.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Current I = Q/t, measured in amperes (A).
  • Potential difference V = W/Q, measured in volts (V).
  • Ohm's law: V = IR for metallic conductors at constant temperature.
  • Resistance R = ρL/A; it increases with length and decreases with cross-sectional area.
  • Series resistors add: R = R1 + R2 + …; parallel: 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …

Worked examples

Example 1 Parallel resistors

Two 6 Ω resistors in parallel give 1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3, so R = 3 Ω. The equivalent resistance is always less than the smallest resistor.

Example 2 A bulb and Ohm's law

A 6 V supply across a 3 Ω bulb gives I = V/R = 2 A. Power P = VI = 12 W.

  1. MCQ The SI unit of resistance is the:

    Answer: Ohm

    Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω).

  2. MCQ Two 4 Ω resistors in series give:
    A. 8 ΩB. 2 ΩC. 4 ΩD. 16 Ω
    Show answer

    Answer: 8 Ω

    Series resistances add.

  3. Short answer State Ohm's law.
    Show answer

    Potential difference across a metallic conductor is directly proportional to current, provided temperature is constant.
