Heating Effect and Electric Power

Current through a resistor produces heat. Electrical energy and power calculations are used to choose wires, fuses and appliances.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Power P = VI = I2R = V2/R.
  • Energy = P × t, measured in joules or kilowatt-hours.
  • The heating effect is used in irons, heaters and fuses.
  • A fuse is rated for a maximum current; exceeding it melts the wire and breaks the circuit.
  • Earthing and correct insulation prevent shock and fire.

Worked examples

Example 1 Cost of electricity

A 100 W bulb used for 10 hours consumes 1 kWh, one unit of electricity.

Example 2 Why wires have low resistance

Copper wires are thick and made of a good conductor, so they waste little energy as heat; heating elements use alloys with high resistance to produce heat.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The commercial unit of electrical energy is the:
    A. Kilowatt-hourB. JouleC. WattD. Volt
    Show answer

    Answer: Kilowatt-hour

    One kWh equals 3.6 × 106 J.

  2. MCQ Power can be calculated as:
    A. VIB. V/IC. I/VD. Q/t
    Show answer

    Answer: VI

    Power is voltage times current.

  3. Short answer Calculate the energy used by a 60 W lamp in 5 hours in kWh.
    Show answer

    Energy = 0.06 kW × 5 h = 0.3 kWh.
