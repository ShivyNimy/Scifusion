Metallurgy, Corrosion and Alloys

Extracting a metal from its ore depends on its reactivity. Corrosion damages metals; alloys improve their properties.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Highly reactive metals are extracted by electrolysis; less reactive ones by reduction with carbon.
  • Roasting is heating a sulphide ore in air; calcination heats a carbonate ore without air.
  • Thermite reaction: Al + Fe2O3 -> Al2O3 + Fe, used to weld railway tracks.
  • Corrosion: rusting of iron needs both oxygen and moisture; galvanisation coats iron with zinc.
  • Alloys are mixtures of metals that are harder and more corrosion-resistant.

Worked examples

Example 1 Galvanising a bucket

Coating iron with zinc protects it because zinc corrodes first, sacrificing itself — cathodic protection.

Example 2 Stainless steel

Adding chromium and nickel to iron produces a protective oxide layer, making the alloy resistant to rust.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The process of coating iron with zinc is called:
    A. GalvanisationB. ElectrolysisC. RoastingD. Calcination
    Show answer

    Answer: Galvanisation

    Zinc coating prevents rusting.

  2. MCQ Which is an alloy?
    A. BrassB. CopperC. ZincD. Aluminium
    Show answer

    Answer: Brass

    Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc.

  3. Short answer Why does iron rust in moist air but not in dry air?
    Show answer

    Rusting needs both oxygen and moisture; dry air lacks water vapour.
