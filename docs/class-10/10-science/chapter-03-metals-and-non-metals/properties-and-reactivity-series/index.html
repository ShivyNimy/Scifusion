Properties and Reactivity Series

Metals lose electrons to form positive ions; non-metals gain or share electrons. Reactivity varies and is ordered in the reactivity series.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Metals are malleable, ductile, sonorous and good conductors.
  • Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.
  • More reactive metals displace less reactive ones from salt solutions.
  • Metals react with oxygen to form basic oxides; some oxides are amphoteric (Al2O3, ZnO).
  • Non-metals form acidic oxides and are usually poor conductors.

Worked examples

Example 1 Zinc in copper sulphate

Zinc is above copper in the series, so it displaces copper, turning the blue solution colourless and depositing red-brown copper.

Example 2 Why gold occurs native

Gold is at the bottom of the reactivity series, so it does not react with oxygen or water and is found uncombined in nature.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which metal is most reactive?
    A. PotassiumB. GoldC. CopperD. Silver
    Show answer

    Answer: Potassium

    Potassium is at the top of the reactivity series.

  2. MCQ Aluminium oxide is:
    A. AmphotericB. Only acidicC. Only basicD. Neutral
    Show answer

    Answer: Amphoteric

    It reacts with both acids and bases.

  3. Short answer Why does iron displace copper from copper sulphate but copper cannot displace iron from iron sulphate?
    Show answer

    Iron is more reactive than copper, so only the more reactive metal can displace the less reactive one.
