Reflection at Spherical Mirrors

Spherical mirrors focus or spread light. Their images depend on the object's position relative to the focus.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Concave mirrors converge light and can form real or virtual images; convex mirrors diverge.
  • Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, with sign convention from the pole.
  • Magnification m = h'/h = −v/u.
  • For a concave mirror, f is negative in the standard sign convention; R = 2f.
  • Uses: concave — headlights, shaving mirrors; convex — rear-view mirrors.

Worked examples

Example 1 Object at infinity

Rays parallel to the axis converge at the focus of a concave mirror, forming a real, inverted and highly diminished image.

Example 2 Which mirror for a dentist

A concave mirror gives a magnified virtual image when the object is inside the focus, so dentists use it to see teeth clearly.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A concave mirror can form:
    A. Real and virtual imagesB. Only real imagesC. Only virtual imagesD. No image
    Show answer

    Answer: Real and virtual images

    Depending on object position, it forms both types.

  2. MCQ The focal length of a spherical mirror is:
    A. Half of the radius of curvatureB. Twice the radiusC. Equal to the radiusD. Quarter of the radius
    Show answer

    Answer: Half of the radius of curvature

    f = R/2.

  3. Short answer State two uses of a convex mirror.
    Show answer

    Rear-view mirrors in vehicles and security mirrors in shops, because they give a wide field of view.
