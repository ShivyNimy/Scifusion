Refraction, Lenses and the Eye

Refraction bends light at boundaries between media. Lenses use refraction to form images, and the eye is a natural optical instrument.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Refractive index n = speed in vacuum ÷ speed in medium.
  • Convex lens: converging, can form real images; concave lens: diverging, always virtual.
  • Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f; power P = 1/f (in metres), measured in dioptres.
  • Myopia is corrected with a concave lens; hypermetropia with a convex lens; presbyopia with bifocals.
  • Dispersion splits white light into colours; a prism and raindrops produce spectra.

Worked examples

Example 1 Power of a lens

A lens with focal length 0.25 m has power P = 1/0.25 = +4 dioptres. A negative power means a diverging lens.

Example 2 Why a pond looks shallower

Light from the bottom bends away from the normal as it leaves water, so the apparent depth is less than the real depth.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The power of a lens is measured in:
    A. DioptresB. NewtonsC. WattsD. Joules
    Show answer

    Answer: Dioptres

    Power is the reciprocal of focal length in metres.

  2. MCQ Myopia is corrected using a:
    A. Concave lensB. Convex lensC. Cylindrical lensD. Plane mirror
    Show answer

    Answer: Concave lens

    A diverging lens moves the image back onto the retina.

  3. Short answer What is the refractive index of a medium whose light speed is half that in vacuum?
    Show answer

    n = c/v = c/(c/2) = 2.
