Human Reproduction and Reproductive Health

Human reproduction involves specialised organs and hormones. Reproductive health includes hygiene, safe behaviour and access to information.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Male reproductive system: testes produce sperm and testosterone; sperm travel through the vas deferens.
  • Female reproductive system: ovaries produce eggs; fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube.
  • The embryo implants in the uterus and develops with the placenta nourishing it.
  • Contraception prevents pregnancy; barrier methods also reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections.
  • Reproductive health includes hygiene, nutrition and avoidance of harmful substances.

Worked examples

Example 1 The journey of the egg

An egg is released from an ovary, travels into the fallopian tube and, if fertilised, implants in the uterus. If not fertilised, it disintegrates and the lining is shed during menstruation.

Example 2 Why prenatal care matters

Good nutrition, avoidance of alcohol and tobacco and regular check-ups reduce risks to both mother and foetus.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Fertilisation in humans occurs in the:
    A. Fallopian tubeB. UterusC. OvaryD. Vagina
    Show answer

    Answer: Fallopian tube

    Sperm meets the egg in the fallopian tube.

  2. MCQ Which organ produces sperm?
    A. TestisB. OvaryC. UterusD. Prostate
    Show answer

    Answer: Testis

    The testes produce sperm and testosterone.

  3. Short answer What is the function of the placenta?
    Show answer

    It exchanges oxygen, nutrients and wastes between mother and foetus.
