Asexual and Sexual Reproduction

Reproduction may be asexual (one parent, identical offspring) or sexual (two parents, genetic variation). Variation helps species survive change.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Asexual methods: binary fission (Amoeba), budding (Hydra, yeast), fragmentation (Spirogyra), regeneration (Planaria), vegetative propagation and spore formation.
  • Sexual reproduction involves gametes: sperm and egg.
  • Fertilisation may be external (frogs, fish) or internal (humans, mammals).
  • In flowering plants, pollination leads to fertilisation and seed formation.
  • Variation from sexual reproduction is the raw material for evolution.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why gardeners use cuttings

A stem cutting produces a plant identical to the parent, preserving desirable traits such as fruit quality or flower colour.

Example 2 Cross-pollination advantage

Pollen from a different plant brings new gene combinations, so offspring may be more disease-resistant.

  2. MCQ Genetic variation is greatest in:
    A. Sexual reproductionB. Binary fissionC. BuddingD. Vegetative propagation

    Answer: Sexual reproduction

    Two parents contribute different gene sets.

  3. Short answer What is the advantage of sexual reproduction?
