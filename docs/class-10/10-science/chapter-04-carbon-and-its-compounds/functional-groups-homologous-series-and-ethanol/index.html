Functional Groups, Homologous Series and Ethanol

Functional groups give carbon compounds their chemical character. Homologous series share a general formula and show graded properties.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Functional groups: alcohol (−OH), aldehyde (−CHO), ketone (−CO−), carboxylic acid (−COOH), halide.
  • A homologous series has members differing by a −CH2− unit.
  • Ethanol is a colourless liquid used in alcoholic drinks, fuels and disinfectants.
  • Ethanol burns with a blue flame to give CO2 and water; it reacts with sodium to release hydrogen.
  • Ethanoic acid (vinegar) is a weak carboxylic acid.

Worked examples

Example 1 Ethanol and sodium

2C2H5OH + 2Na -> 2C2H5ONa + H2. The hydrogen comes from the −OH group, showing ethanol has an alcohol functional group.

Example 2 Ester formation

Ethanol plus ethanoic acid with an acid catalyst gives ethyl ethanoate, an ester with a fruity smell used in perfumes.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The functional group −COOH is characteristic of:
    A. Carboxylic acidsB. AlcoholsC. AldehydesD. Ketones
    Show answer

    Answer: Carboxylic acids

    −COOH is the carboxylic acid group.

  2. MCQ The next member of the homologous series after methane is:
    A. EthaneB. PropaneC. EtheneD. Methanol
    Show answer

    Answer: Ethane

    Adding a −CH2− unit to CH4 gives C2H6, ethane.

  3. Short answer Why is ethanol used as a fuel?
    Show answer

    It burns with a blue flame releasing a large amount of heat and produces relatively less soot.
