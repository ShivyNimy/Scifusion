Nutrients in Food

Food contains nutrients — carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water and roughage — each with a specific job in the body.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Carbohydrates and fats are energy-giving foods.
  • Proteins are body-building foods; they are needed for growth and repair.
  • Vitamins and minerals are protective foods; they keep the body healthy and fight disease.
  • Water carries nutrients and removes wastes; roughage (dietary fibre) helps the gut push food along.
  • A simple test for starch uses iodine (blue-black colour); for protein, copper sulphate and caustic soda give a violet colour; for fat, a translucent oily patch appears on paper.

Worked examples

Example 1 Testing a potato for starch

Put a drop of dilute iodine on a cut potato. It turns blue-black, showing starch is present. Iodine also turns rice water blue-black, but not on a slice of cucumber.

Example 2 Why growing children need dal and eggs

Pulses, milk, eggs, fish and soyabean are rich in protein. Growing children need extra protein because new cells are being built every day; a protein-poor diet slows growth.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which nutrient is the main source of energy?
    A. CarbohydrateB. ProteinC. Vitamin CD. Iron
    Show answer

    Answer: Carbohydrate

    Carbohydrates are the body's main fuel and are found in cereals, potato and sugar.

  2. MCQ Iodine solution is used to test for:
    A. StarchB. ProteinC. FatD. Vitamin
    Show answer

    Answer: Starch

    Iodine turns blue-black in the presence of starch.

  3. Short answer Name the two groups of foods that protect the body from disease.
    Show answer

    Vitamins and minerals are protective nutrients; deficiency causes specific diseases.
