Handpicking, Threshing, Winnowing and Sieving

Mixtures can be separated using differences in size, weight and appearance. Choosing the method depends on the difference between the components.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Handpicking removes small amounts of visible impurities such as stones from pulses.
  • Threshing separates grain from stalks by beating or by machines.
  • Winnowing separates lighter husk from heavier grain with wind or blowing air.
  • Sieving separates components of different particle size, such as bran from flour.

Worked examples

Example 1 Farmer's sequence

After harvesting, wheat is threshed to free the grain, winnowed to blow away husk, then sieved to remove dirt and broken grain. Each step uses a different property — size, weight, size again.

Example 2 Separating a mixture of salt and sand

Neither handpicking nor winnowing works well. Add water: salt dissolves, sand does not. Filter to remove sand, then evaporate the filtrate to recover salt.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Winnowing separates components based on:
    A. Difference in weightB. Difference in colourC. SolubilityD. Magnetism
    Show answer

    Answer: Difference in weight

    Wind carries the lighter husk away while heavier grain falls close by.

  2. MCQ Which method removes small stones from rice?
    A. HandpickingB. EvaporationC. CondensationD. Filtration
    Show answer

    Answer: Handpicking

    Stones are visible and few, so they are removed by hand.

  3. Short answer Why is threshing done before winnowing?
    Show answer

    You cannot blow away husk while it is still attached to the stalk.
