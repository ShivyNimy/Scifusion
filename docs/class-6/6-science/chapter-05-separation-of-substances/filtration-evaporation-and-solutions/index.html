Filtration, Evaporation and Solutions

Solids can be separated from liquids by filtration, sedimentation and decantation; dissolved solids are recovered by evaporation and condensation.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Sedimentation lets heavy insoluble particles settle; decantation pours off the clear liquid.
  • Filtration traps insoluble solids on filter paper, allowing the clear filtrate through.
  • Evaporation boils off the solvent and leaves the dissolved solid behind.
  • A solution has a solute dissolved in a solvent; a saturated solution cannot dissolve more solute at that temperature, and heating usually increases how much can dissolve.

Worked examples

Example 1 Clean water from muddy water

Muddy water is left to stand so mud settles (sedimentation), the clear water is poured off (decantation) and then filtered through filter paper. It looks clear but still needs boiling to be safe to drink, because dissolved germs are not removed by filtering.

Example 2 Recovering salt from seawater

Seawater is evaporated in shallow ponds by sunlight. Water turns to vapour, salt crystals stay behind. This is exactly how salt is produced at the coast.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The clear liquid that passes through filter paper is called:
    A. FiltrateB. ResidueC. SoluteD. Solvent
    Show answer

    Answer: Filtrate

    The liquid that passes through is the filtrate; the solid left on the paper is the residue.

  2. MCQ Which method recovers dissolved salt from salt water?
    A. EvaporationB. FiltrationC. SievingD. Winnowing
    Show answer

    Answer: Evaporation

    Water evaporates and salt is left behind.

  3. Short answer What is a saturated solution?
    Show answer

    Extra solute added to a saturated solution simply settles at the bottom.
