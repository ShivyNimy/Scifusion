Making Magnets and Demagnetisation

Magnets can be made by stroking or by an electric current, and can lose magnetism when heated, dropped or stored carelessly.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Stroke a piece of iron in one direction with one pole of a magnet, repeatedly, to magnetise it.
  • An electromagnet is a coil of wire around an iron core; it is magnetic only while current flows.
  • Heating, hammering or dropping a magnet can demagnetise it.
  • Store magnets in pairs with opposite poles together and a piece of iron across them, to preserve strength.
  • Magnets should be kept away from mobile phones and music players.

Worked examples

Example 1 A simple electromagnet

Wind 30 turns of insulated copper wire around an iron nail and connect the ends to a cell. The nail picks up pins while the circuit is on and drops them when it is switched off.

Example 2 Why compasses are stored carefully

A compass needle is a weak magnet. Keeping it beside strong magnets or dropping it can reverse or weaken the needle, making direction readings unreliable.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ An electromagnet loses its magnetism when:
    A. Current is switched offB. It is cooledC. It is paintedD. It is weighed
    Show answer

    Answer: Current is switched off

    Magnetism depends on the flowing current.

  2. MCQ To demagnetise a magnet you can:
    A. Heat it stronglyB. Paint itC. Wrap it in paperD. Wash it
    Show answer

    Answer: Heat it strongly

    High temperature, hammering or dropping disturb the alignment of domains.

  3. Short answer How should two bar magnets be stored?
    Show answer

    In pairs with opposite poles together and a soft iron piece across the ends.
