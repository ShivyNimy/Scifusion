Waste and Disposal

Waste comes from homes, markets, farms and factories. It can be biodegradable (rottable) or non-biodegradable (not rotted by microbes).

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Biodegradable waste: vegetable peels, paper, leftover food, leaves.
  • Non-biodegradable waste: plastics, glass, metals, polythene bags.
  • Landfills bury waste; when badly managed they attract flies, rats and stray animals and leak harmful liquids.
  • Composting converts biodegradable waste into manure with the help of microbes; vermicomposting uses earthworms.
  • Burning waste releases toxic gases and should be avoided.

Worked examples

Example 1 Making compost at home

Collect fruit and vegetable peels in a covered bin, add dry leaves in layers, keep it moist and turn it every few days. In about two months, dark crumbly compost is ready for plants.

Example 2 Why an open dump is unhealthy

Open dumps breed flies and mosquitoes, smell bad and allow rainwater to carry harmful liquids into the soil and groundwater.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which waste is biodegradable?
    A. Vegetable peelsB. Plastic bagC. Glass bottleD. Aluminium can
    Show answer

    Answer: Vegetable peels

    Peels are broken down by microorganisms.

  2. MCQ Vermicomposting uses:
    A. EarthwormsB. Fungi onlyC. Bacteria onlyD. Plastic
    Show answer

    Answer: Earthworms

    Earthworms consume organic waste and produce nutrient-rich castings.

  3. Short answer What is the difference between biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste? Give one example of each.
    Show answer

    Biodegradable waste is broken down by living organisms (peels); non-biodegradable waste is not (plastic).
