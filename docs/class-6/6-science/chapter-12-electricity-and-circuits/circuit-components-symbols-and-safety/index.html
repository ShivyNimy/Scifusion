Circuit Components, Symbols and Safety

Electric circuits are drawn with standard symbols. Safety rules prevent shocks and fires.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Symbols: a straight line for a wire, a long and short parallel line for a cell, a circle with a cross for a bulb.
  • A switch is shown as a break in the wire with a lever arm.
  • Never touch electric switches with wet hands; water conducts electricity.
  • Do not use damaged wires or overload sockets; a short circuit can cause fire.
  • Electric current in household wires comes from the mains and is much stronger and more dangerous than a cell.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reading a circuit diagram

A rectangle of straight lines with a cell symbol, a bulb symbol and a switch symbol describes a complete circuit. If the switch symbol is open, the bulb is off.

Example 2 Why wet hands are dangerous

Water with dissolved salts conducts electricity, so wet skin lowers resistance. Current can pass through the body, which is why switches are never touched with wet hands.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The symbol of a cell in a circuit diagram is:
    A. A long and a short parallel lineB. A circle with a crossC. A zigzag lineD. A rectangle
    Show answer

    Answer: A long and a short parallel line

    The long line is the positive terminal and the short line the negative terminal.

  2. MCQ Water conducts electricity because it contains:
    A. Dissolved saltsB. AirC. PlasticD. Rubber
    Show answer

    Answer: Dissolved salts

    Dissolved salts provide ions that carry charge.

  3. Short answer Write two electric safety rules for home.
    Show answer

    Never touch switches with wet hands; never overload a socket or use damaged wires.
