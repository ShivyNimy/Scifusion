Bones, Joints and the Skeleton

The human skeleton is a framework of bones that supports the body, protects organs and, together with muscles, allows movement. Bones meet at joints.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The skull protects the brain, the rib cage protects the heart and lungs, and the backbone protects the spinal cord.
  • Immovable joints (skull), ball-and-socket joints (shoulder, hip), hinge joints (elbow, knee), pivot joints (neck) and gliding joints (wrist) allow different movements.
  • A joint is where two or more bones meet; cartilage and a fluid cushion reduce friction.
  • Bones are living tissue: they grow, repair and store calcium.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why the knee bends one way

The knee is a hinge joint, like a door hinge, so it allows bending and straightening in one plane. The shoulder is a ball-and-socket joint, so the arm can swing in all directions.

Example 2 The rib cage as a shield

The ribs curve around the chest and attach to the backbone and breastbone. This cage protects the lungs and heart while still allowing the chest to expand when you breathe.

Practice questions

    A. Hinge jointB. Ball-and-socket jointC. Pivot jointD. Immovable joint
    Show answer

    Answer: Hinge joint

    Elbows and knees work like door hinges.

  2. MCQ Which structure protects the brain?
    A. SkullB. Rib cageC. BackboneD. Pelvis
    Show answer

    Answer: Skull

    The skull encloses the brain.

  3. Short answer Why are joints needed?
    Show answer

    Different joint types permit different ranges of movement, from fixed skull plates to freely moving hips.
