Muscles and Movement in Animals

Muscles pull on bones to make them move; they work in pairs. Animals without bones use other systems: shells, body-fluid pressure, segmented bodies or fins.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Contracting a muscle shortens it and pulls the bone; the opposing muscle relaxes.
  • Earthworms move with body-fluid pressure and bristles (setae) that grip the soil.
  • Snails glide on a muscular foot and mucus; cockroaches use legs and wings attached to a segmented body.
  • Fish swim with fins and a flexible backbone; birds have hollow bones and wings for flight.

Worked examples

Example 1 Bending the arm

The bicep on top of the upper arm contracts and shortens, pulling the forearm up. To straighten the arm, the triceps contracts and the bicep relaxes. Muscles can only pull, so they work in pairs.

Example 2 How an earthworm burrows

The worm lengthens by filling its front segments with fluid, anchors with bristles and then pulls its rear forward. No bones, but the same principle: force against the surroundings.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Muscles can only:
    A. PullB. PushC. Neither pull nor pushD. Both equally
    Show answer

    Answer: Pull

    A contracting muscle shortens and pulls; pairs of muscles produce opposite movements.

  2. MCQ Earthworms move with the help of:
    A. Body fluid and bristlesB. Bones and jointsC. Shells and feetD. Wings
    Show answer

    Answer: Body fluid and bristles

    Fluid pressure plus setae let the worm grip and extend its body.

  3. Short answer Why do birds have hollow bones?
    Show answer

    Lightness reduces the energy needed to lift the body against gravity.
