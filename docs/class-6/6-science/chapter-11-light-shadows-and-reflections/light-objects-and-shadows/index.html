Light, Objects and Shadows

Light travels in straight lines. When an opaque object blocks light, a shadow forms behind it. Materials are transparent, translucent or opaque.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Light enables us to see things; we see an object when light from it reaches our eyes.
  • Transparent: clear glass; translucent: frosted glass or butter paper; opaque: wood, cardboard.
  • A shadow forms on a screen when an opaque object blocks light; it has the same shape as the object.
  • In a pinhole camera, light travels in straight lines and forms an upside-down image.
  • Shadows are larger when the object is closer to the light source.

Worked examples

Example 1 Shadow size investigation

Hold a ball between a torch and a wall. Move the ball towards the torch and the shadow on the wall becomes bigger; move it towards the wall and the shadow shrinks. Size depends on the geometry of light travelling in straight lines.

Example 2 The pinhole camera

Place a small hole in one side of a box and tracing paper on the other. A candle outside forms an inverted image on the paper — proof that light travels in straight lines and crosses at the hole.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A shadow is formed by:
    A. An opaque object blocking lightB. A transparent objectC. A mirror reflecting lightD. A light source alone
    Show answer

    Answer: An opaque object blocking light

    Only a light-blocking object produces a shadow.

  2. MCQ In a pinhole camera the image is:
    A. Upside downB. UprightC. Always blueD. Not formed
    Show answer

    Answer: Upside down

    Rays cross at the hole, so the image is inverted.

  3. Short answer Classify materials as transparent, translucent or opaque: clear glass, butter paper, wood.
    Show answer

    Clear glass is transparent, butter paper is translucent and wood is opaque.
