Mirrors and Reflection

Plane mirrors reflect light and form images that are virtual, erect and laterally inverted. Curved mirrors are used as magnifiers and as rear-view mirrors.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Reflection occurs when light bounces off a surface; a plane mirror gives a virtual image of the same size.
  • In a plane mirror, the image is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
  • Lateral inversion swaps left and right, which is why an ambulance has mirror-image writing.
  • A convex mirror shows a wider view and makes images smaller; a concave mirror can magnify.
  • A periscope uses two plane mirrors at 45° to see over obstacles.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why writing looks reversed in a mirror

Raise your right hand in front of a mirror: the image raises its left hand. The mirror does not swap up and down, it swaps left and right — lateral inversion.

Example 2 The periscope

Light from the object strikes the top mirror, is reflected down to a second mirror and reaches the eye. Submarines and periscopes use this to see above water while staying hidden.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The image formed by a plane mirror is:
    A. Virtual and erectB. Real and invertedC. Real and erectD. Always magnified
    Show answer

    Answer: Virtual and erect

    The image cannot be caught on a screen and is upright.

  2. MCQ A convex mirror is used as a rear-view mirror because it:
    A. Gives a wider field of viewB. Magnifies the trafficC. Inverts the imageD. Blocks sunlight
    Show answer

    Answer: Gives a wider field of view

    A wider view shows more of the road behind.

  3. Short answer Why does an ambulance carry 'AMBULANCE' written in reverse?
    Show answer

    So that drivers reading it in a mirror see it correctly, because a plane mirror laterally inverts the image.
