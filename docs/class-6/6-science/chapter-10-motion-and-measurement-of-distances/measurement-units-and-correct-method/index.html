Measurement, Units and Correct Method

Measurement means comparing a quantity with a standard unit. Standard units are needed so that measurements mean the same thing everywhere.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • The SI unit of length is the metre (m); 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1000 m.
  • A horizontal distance is measured with a metre scale, a curved line with a string, and long distances with a surveyor's tape or a trundle wheel.
  • The eye must be directly above the scale marks to avoid parallax error.
  • The scale should touch the object and zero should coincide with one end; if the zero is broken, start from another mark and subtract.
  • Decimal units make multiplication and conversion simple: 1.5 m = 150 cm.

Worked examples

Example 1 Measuring a curve correctly

To measure the length of a curved leaf edge, place a thread along the edge, mark the ends on the thread, then straighten it against a scale. Straight rulers cannot follow curves accurately.

Example 2 Broken zero

If a ruler is broken at the zero mark, place the object starting at the 2.0 cm mark. If the other end reads 12.5 cm, the length is 12.5 − 2.0 = 10.5 cm.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The SI unit of length is the:
    A. MetreB. CentimetreC. KilometreD. Foot
    Show answer

    Answer: Metre

    The metre is the SI base unit of length.

  2. MCQ 1.5 m equals:
    A. 150 cmB. 15 cmC. 1500 cmD. 0.15 cm
    Show answer

    Answer: 150 cm

    1 m = 100 cm, so 1.5 m = 150 cm.

  3. Short answer How would you measure the length of a curved line?
    Show answer

    Place a thread along the curve, mark the ends, straighten the thread and measure it with a scale.
