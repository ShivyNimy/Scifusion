Animal Fibres: Wool and Silk

Wool comes from the fleece of sheep and other animals; silk comes from the cocoon of the silkworm. Both are animal fibres with different properties and processing.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Sheep are sheared in summer; the fleece is cleaned (scouring), sorted and spun into wool yarn.
  • The outer hair of sheep is coarse; the under-hair (undercoat) is soft and is used for fine wool.
  • Silk is produced by the larva of the silkworm moth, which spins a cocoon of silk fibre.
  • Sericulture is the rearing of silkworms; silk is strong, lustrous and absorbs moisture.

Worked examples

Example 1 Why wool keeps us warm

Wool fibres trap air between them; air is a poor conductor of heat, so body heat cannot escape easily. That is why woollen clothes are used in winter and loosely woven cotton in summer.

Example 2 Silk: a protein thread

A silkworm can spin a continuous filament hundreds of metres long. The filaments from several cocoons are twisted together to make a silk thread strong enough for weaving.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which animal gives us wool?
    A. SheepB. SilkwormC. CowD. Hen
    Show answer

    Answer: Sheep

    Sheep fleece is the most common source of wool.

  2. MCQ Sericulture is the rearing of:
    A. SilkwormsB. SheepC. BeesD. Fish
    Show answer

    Answer: Silkworms

    Sericulture is silk farming.

  3. Short answer Why is wool a good insulator?
    Show answer

    Air trapped between wool fibres is a poor conductor, so it reduces heat transfer from the body.
