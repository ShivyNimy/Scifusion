Plant Fibres: Cotton and Jute

Clothes are made from fibres. Natural fibres come from plants (cotton, jute, linen) or animals (wool, silk); synthetic fibres are made by humans.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Cotton comes from the bolls of the cotton plant and grows best in black soil and warm climates.
  • Jute comes from the stem of the jute plant and is harvested when the plant is flowering; it is called the golden fibre.
  • Fibres are spun into yarn and yarn is woven or knitted into fabric.
  • Ginning separates cotton fibres from seeds; retting softens jute stems in water so fibres can be peeled.

Worked examples

Example 1 From field to shirt

Cotton is picked, ginned, combed and spun into yarn, then woven into cloth and stitched into a shirt. Each step removes impurities and lines the fibres up so they can twist into a strong yarn.

Example 2 Why jute is used for sacks

Jute fibres are long, coarse and strong but not very fine, so they make excellent ropes, gunny bags and carpets rather than soft clothing.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Jute fibre is obtained from the:
    A. StemB. SeedC. LeafD. Root
    Show answer

    Answer: Stem

    Jute is a bast fibre taken from the stem of the plant.

  2. MCQ The process of separating cotton fibres from seeds is called:
    A. GinningB. SpinningC. WeavingD. Retting
    Show answer

    Answer: Ginning

    Ginning separates seeds from cotton fibre.

  3. Short answer Write the steps from fibre to fabric.
    Show answer

    Fibres are cleaned, spun into yarn on a spinning wheel or machine, then interlaced (woven) or looped (knitted) into fabric.
