The Water Cycle

Water moves continuously between the oceans, atmosphere and land in the water cycle, driven by the Sun's heat.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Evaporation changes water into vapour; condensation changes vapour back into droplets; precipitation is rain, hail or snow.
  • Transpiration releases water vapour from plant leaves and adds to atmospheric moisture.
  • Groundwater seeps into the soil and collects above an impermeable layer; wells and boreholes reach it.
  • The demand for water depends on rainfall patterns and how much is used for farming, industry and homes.
  • Water is a universal solvent, which is why most substances dissolve in it.

Worked examples

Example 1 The water cycle in a rainy week

Sun heats a lake, water evaporates, rises, cools and condenses into clouds, and finally falls as rain. The rain flows back to the lake through streams. The same water keeps circulating.

Example 2 Why plants matter in the cycle

A single mature tree can release hundreds of litres of water vapour a day through transpiration. Cutting forests reduces this flow and can make an area drier.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The process of changing water vapour into liquid is:
    A. CondensationB. EvaporationC. FiltrationD. Precipitation
    Show answer

    Answer: Condensation

    Cooling vapour forms droplets.

  2. MCQ Water that collects underground is called:
    A. GroundwaterB. RainwaterC. Surface waterD. Sea water
    Show answer

    Answer: Groundwater

    Water that seeps into the soil and fills the spaces above impermeable rock is groundwater.

  3. Short answer List the steps of the water cycle.
    Show answer

    Evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection (runoff and groundwater).
