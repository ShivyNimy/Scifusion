Water Conservation and Rainwater Harvesting

Fresh water is limited, so it must be used carefully. Rainwater harvesting, drip irrigation and leak repair all save water.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Rainwater harvesting collects roof rainwater and stores it or sends it into the ground to recharge groundwater.
  • Drip irrigation delivers water directly to plant roots and wastes much less than flood irrigation.
  • Fixing leaking taps, closing taps while brushing and reusing washing water for plants save litres every day.
  • Dams and lakes store water and recharge groundwater but can displace people and affect rivers.
  • A drought is a long period of unusually low rainfall; a flood is an overflow of water onto normally dry land.

Worked examples

Example 1 Harvesting a rooftop

A 100 m2 roof with 50 mm of rain can collect about 5,000 litres (100 × 0.05 m × 1000). Piped into a tank or a recharge pit, that water is saved instead of running down the drain.

Example 2 Drip versus flood irrigation

Flood irrigation can lose more than half the water to evaporation and runoff. Drip irrigation delivers water drop by drop at the root and can save 30–60% of water.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Drip irrigation is useful because it:
    A. Delivers water directly to roots and saves waterB. Floods the fieldC. Uses sea waterD. Needs no plants
    Show answer

    Answer: Delivers water directly to roots and saves water

    It minimises evaporation and runoff.

  2. MCQ Rainwater harvesting mainly helps to:
    A. Recharge groundwater and store waterB. Increase rainfallC. Filter sea waterD. Produce electricity
    Show answer

    Answer: Recharge groundwater and store water

    Captured rain is stored or allowed to percolate into the ground.

  3. Short answer Give three ways to save water at home.
    Show answer

    Fix leaks, turn off taps while brushing, reuse water for plants (also take shorter showers).
