Life, Air and the Balance of Gases

Living things depend on air. Plants add oxygen and remove carbon dioxide, keeping the balance of gases steady.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Respiration uses oxygen and releases carbon dioxide; photosynthesis does the reverse.
  • Plants release oxygen during the day; at night only respiration occurs, but oxygen use is small compared with the ocean and forest reserves.
  • Air pollution from burning fuels adds harmful gases and particles, causing asthma and acid rain.
  • Green plants act as the lungs of the planet and should be protected.

Worked examples

Example 1 Oxygen in a sealed jar

A mouse in a sealed jar uses up oxygen and dies; adding a green plant with light restores oxygen by photosynthesis and the mouse survives longer. This classic experiment shows the gas exchange between plants and animals.

Example 2 Why cities need trees

A tree filters dust, absorbs carbon dioxide and releases oxygen. A street lined with trees can be several degrees cooler and noticeably cleaner than a bare one.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ During photosynthesis plants take in:
    A. Carbon dioxideB. OxygenC. NitrogenD. Argon
    Show answer

    Answer: Carbon dioxide

    Carbon dioxide is the carbon source for making food.

  2. MCQ Air pollution can cause:
    A. Respiratory problemsB. Better eyesightC. Stronger bonesD. More oxygen
    Show answer

    Answer: Respiratory problems

    Particles and gases irritate the lungs and worsen asthma.

  3. Short answer How do plants help maintain the balance of gases in air?
    Show answer

    They take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen during photosynthesis, balancing respiration.
