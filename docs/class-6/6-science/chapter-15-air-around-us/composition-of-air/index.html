Composition of Air

Air is a mixture of gases. Nitrogen makes up about 78%, oxygen about 21%, and the rest is argon, carbon dioxide, water vapour and dust.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Air is a mixture, so its components can be separated by physical means and keep their own properties.
  • Oxygen supports burning; nitrogen does not; carbon dioxide puts out fire and is used in fire extinguishers.
  • Water vapour condenses on cold surfaces, showing that air contains moisture.
  • Dust particles make a beam of sunlight visible and settle on surfaces.
  • Air is needed by all living things: plants and animals use oxygen for respiration.

Worked examples

Example 1 The candle experiment

Place a burning candle inside an inverted glass over water. As oxygen is used up, the flame goes out and water rises into the glass, roughly one-fifth of the way — evidence that oxygen is about a fifth of air.

Example 2 Why the sky looks blue

Tiny gas molecules scatter short blue wavelengths more than long red ones. That scattered light reaches our eyes from all directions, making the sky appear blue.

Practice questions

  1. MCQ The gas present in the largest amount in air is:
    A. NitrogenB. OxygenC. Carbon dioxideD. Argon
    Show answer

    Answer: Nitrogen

    Nitrogen makes up about 78% of the atmosphere.

  2. MCQ Which gas supports burning?
    A. OxygenB. NitrogenC. Carbon dioxideD. Water vapour
    Show answer

    Answer: Oxygen

    Combustion needs oxygen.

  3. Short answer Why does a burning candle go out when covered by a glass?
    Show answer

    The oxygen inside is used up, so combustion cannot continue.
