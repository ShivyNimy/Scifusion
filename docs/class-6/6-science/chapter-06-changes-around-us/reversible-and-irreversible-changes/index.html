Reversible and Irreversible Changes

Some changes can be reversed by reversing the conditions, and some cannot. Reversibility is one of the simplest ways to describe a change.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Melting ice, folding paper, stretching a rubber band and dissolving salt are reversible.
  • Burning paper, cooking rice, rusting iron, curdling milk and baking clay pots are irreversible.
  • A change may be fast (burning) or slow (rusting, growth of a tree).
  • Irreversible changes usually create a new substance with new properties.

Worked examples

Example 1 Reversing a change

Water freezes to ice in a freezer; ice melts back to water at room temperature. Heating and cooling reverse each other because no new substance is formed.

Example 2 An irreversible kitchen change

A raw egg cannot be turned back from a fried egg. The proteins have unfolded and bonded differently, so no amount of cooling restores the raw egg.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which of these changes is irreversible?
    A. Burning of paperB. Melting of waxC. Folding of paperD. Dissolving sugar in water
    Show answer

    Answer: Burning of paper

    Burning forms ash, smoke and gases — new substances that cannot be turned back into paper.

  2. MCQ Melting of ice is a ______ change.
    A. ReversibleB. IrreversibleC. ChemicalD. Permanent
    Show answer

    Answer: Reversible

    Ice can be refrozen, so the change is reversible.

  3. Short answer Give two examples each of reversible and irreversible changes.
    Show answer

    Reversible: melting of wax, dissolving salt. Irreversible: rusting of iron, cooking food.
