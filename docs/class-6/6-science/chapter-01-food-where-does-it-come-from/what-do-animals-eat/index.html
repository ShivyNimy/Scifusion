What Do Animals Eat?

Animals are grouped by what they eat: herbivores eat plants, carnivores eat other animals, and omnivores eat both. Some animals also eat dead matter and are called scavengers.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Herbivores: cow, goat, deer, elephant, rabbit.
  • Carnivores: lion, tiger, fox, wolf; some eat insects (insectivores) such as the frog and lizard.
  • Omnivores: human, bear, crow, dog, cockroach.
  • Scavengers such as vultures and crows eat dead animals and help keep the environment clean.
  • Parasites such as ticks and lice live on other animals and feed on them.

Worked examples

Example 1 Tiger versus cow

A tiger has sharp, pointed canine teeth and a short gut — adaptations for tearing and digesting meat quickly. A cow has flat grinding teeth and a long gut for breaking down plant fibre. Teeth and body design match diet.

Example 2 The bee and the flower

Honeybees visit flowers for nectar and pollen, and in doing so carry pollen from flower to flower. The bee gets food and the plant gets pollinated: a partnership, not theft.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ A cow is a ______.
    A. HerbivoreB. CarnivoreC. OmnivoreD. Scavenger
    Show answer

    Answer: Herbivore

    Cows eat grass and other plant material, so they are herbivores.

  2. MCQ Vultures that eat dead animals are best described as:
    A. ScavengersB. ParasitesC. HerbivoresD. Producers
    Show answer

    Answer: Scavengers

    Animals that feed on dead and decaying matter are scavengers.

  3. Short answer Why does a tiger have sharp, pointed teeth while a cow has flat teeth?
    Show answer

    Teeth are adapted to diet: tigers tear meat with pointed canines, cows grind plant material with flat molars.
