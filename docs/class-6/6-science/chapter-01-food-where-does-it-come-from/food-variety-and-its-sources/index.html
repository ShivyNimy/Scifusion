Food Variety and Its Sources

Every living thing needs food. Humans eat a huge variety of food because we are omnivores, and almost all of it comes either from plants or from animals.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Plants give us cereals, pulses, fruits, vegetables, spices, oil and sugar.
  • Animals give us milk, eggs, meat, honey and fish.
  • Different plant parts are edible: roots (carrot), stems (sugarcane), leaves (spinach), flowers (broccoli), fruits (tomato) and seeds (rice, wheat).
  • A food ingredient is what a dish is made from; many dishes combine plant and animal ingredients.

Worked examples

Example 1 A simple meal, traced back

Rice, dal and milk: rice comes from the seeds of the rice plant, dal from split pulses (seeds of legumes), and milk from a cow or buffalo. A vegetarian meal still has two plant sources and one animal source.

Example 2 Sprouts — a living food

Soak gram seeds overnight, drain the water and leave them wrapped in cloth for a day. Small white shoots appear: the seeds have germinated into sprouts, which are easier to digest and richer in some vitamins than dry seeds.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which of these is an animal product?
    A. HoneyB. RiceC. SpinachD. Mustard oil
    Show answer

    Answer: Honey

    Honey is made by honeybees, so it is an animal product. Rice, spinach and mustard oil are plant products.

  2. MCQ Which plant part do we eat when we eat a carrot?
    A. RootB. StemC. LeafD. Flower
    Show answer

    Answer: Root

    A carrot is a modified tap root that stores food.

  3. Short answer Name three food items that come from plants and three that come from animals.
    Show answer

    Cereals, pulses, fruits and vegetables are plant foods; milk, egg, meat, fish and honey are animal foods.
