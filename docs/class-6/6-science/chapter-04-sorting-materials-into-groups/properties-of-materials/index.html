Properties of Materials

We group materials by observable properties such as appearance, hardness, solubility, transparency and conductivity. Grouping makes it easy to study and use materials.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Materials may be shiny (metals) or dull (wood, rubber, paper).
  • Hard materials scratch softer ones; iron is harder than chalk, diamond is the hardest natural material.
  • Some solids dissolve in water (sugar, salt) and are soluble; others (sand, chalk) are insoluble.
  • Transparent materials let light pass fully (glass), translucent let some light pass (butter paper), opaque let no light pass (wood).
  • Metals are good conductors of heat and electricity; wood, plastic, rubber and glass are poor conductors.

Worked examples

Example 1 Sorting a kitchen drawer

A spoon is shiny, hard, insoluble and opaque. A plastic lid is dull, flexible, insoluble and opaque. A glass tumbler is transparent. Sorting by these five properties tells us which item suits which job — metal for the pan, plastic for the handle.

Example 2 Sugar in cold water versus hot water

The same spoon of sugar dissolves faster in hot water because heat speeds up the movement of water molecules. Solubility is a property of the material, but dissolving speed changes with temperature.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Which material is transparent?
    A. GlassB. WoodC. CardboardD. Iron
    Show answer

    Answer: Glass

    Glass allows most light to pass through.

  2. MCQ Materials that do not allow electricity to pass are called:
    A. InsulatorsB. ConductorsC. SolutesD. Soluble
    Show answer

    Answer: Insulators

    Insulators such as rubber and plastic resist the flow of current.

  3. Short answer Name three properties used to sort materials.
    Show answer

    Appearance, hardness and solubility (also transparency and conductivity).
