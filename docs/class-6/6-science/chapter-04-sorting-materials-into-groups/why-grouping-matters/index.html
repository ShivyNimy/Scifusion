Why Grouping Matters

Sorting is not just neatness: grouping reveals patterns, helps us choose the right material for a purpose and is the first step of scientific classification.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Objects made of the same material share properties even when their shape differs.
  • Grouping lets us predict behaviour: all metals conduct, all plastics insulate.
  • Mixing materials can change properties — glass is transparent but brittle, so safety glass is layered.
  • Early scientists grouped living things and substances; modern chemistry uses the same logic with elements and compounds.

Worked examples

Example 1 Cooking utensils

The body of a pan is metal because metals conduct heat well. The handle is plastic or wood because those materials do not conduct heat, so they protect our hands. Two material groups, one object.

Example 2 Recycled sorting

A recycling centre separates paper, plastic, glass and metal by hand and by machine. Each group is processed differently, so correct sorting directly saves energy and raw material.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Why is the handle of a cooking pan made of plastic?
    A. Plastic is a poor conductor of heatB. Plastic is shinyC. Plastic is heavyD. Plastic dissolves in water
    Show answer

    Answer: Plastic is a poor conductor of heat

    Insulating handles stay cool enough to hold.

  2. MCQ Objects are grouped mainly to:
    A. Study and use them convenientlyB. Make them heavierC. Change their colourD. Increase their price
    Show answer

    Answer: Study and use them conveniently

    Grouping reveals shared properties so materials can be used scientifically.

  3. Short answer Give one example each of a soluble and an insoluble substance.
    Show answer

    Sugar dissolves in water, sand does not and settles at the bottom.
