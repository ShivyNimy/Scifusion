Roots, Stems and Leaves

Flowering plants have roots that anchor and absorb, stems that support and transport, and leaves that make food.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • Tap roots have one main root with branches (mustard, carrot); fibrous roots are a bunch of thin roots (grass, wheat).
  • Roots absorb water and minerals; some store food (carrot, radish) or help climbing (money plant).
  • The stem bears leaves, flowers and fruits and carries water upward through xylem and food through phloem.
  • Leaves have a blade, petiole, midrib and veins; stomata on the surface allow gas exchange.
  • Venation is reticulate (net-like, dicots) or parallel (monocots, grasses).

Worked examples

Example 1 Reading a leaf

A mango leaf has a network of veins (reticulate) and a tap root system; a grass leaf has parallel veins and fibrous roots. Leaf venation and root type usually match.

Example 2 Why roots are pulled from the soil with difficulty

Branched roots hold the plant firmly and increase surface area for absorbing water. This is also why plants bind soil and reduce erosion.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ Carrot is an example of a:
    A. Modified tap rootB. Fibrous rootC. StemD. Leaf
    Show answer

    Answer: Modified tap root

    Carrot stores food in its tap root.

  2. MCQ Water is transported through the plant by:
    A. XylemB. PhloemC. StomataD. Lamina
    Show answer

    Answer: Xylem

    Xylem carries water and minerals upward from the root.

  3. Short answer State two functions of leaves.
    Show answer

    Leaves also lose water by transpiration, which helps pull water up the plant.
