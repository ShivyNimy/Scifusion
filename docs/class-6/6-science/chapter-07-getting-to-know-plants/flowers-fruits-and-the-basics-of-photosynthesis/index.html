Flowers, Fruits and the Basics of Photosynthesis

Flowers are reproductive structures. After pollination and fertilisation they become fruits, and seeds develop inside. Leaves make food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide.

2 worked examples · 3 practice questions

Key points

  • A flower has sepals (protect the bud), petals (attract pollinators), stamens (male, produce pollen) and pistil (female, contains ovary and ovules).
  • Pollination is the transfer of pollen to the stigma; fertilisation is the fusion of gametes.
  • The ovary develops into the fruit and ovules into seeds.
  • Photosynthesis needs chlorophyll, sunlight, carbon dioxide and water, and produces glucose and oxygen.
  • Plants release oxygen during the day and respire throughout the day and night.

Worked examples

Example 1 From flower to fruit

After a bee visits a pumpkin flower, pollen grains land on the stigma and grow tubes to the ovules. The ovary swells into a pumpkin containing seeds — each seed from one fertilised ovule.

Example 2 Why a plant in a dark room goes pale

Without light, chlorophyll is not made and photosynthesis stops, so leaves look yellow and the plant slowly starves. Light is the energy source for food production.

Practice questions

Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. MCQ The female part of a flower is the:
    A. PistilB. StamenC. PetalD. Sepal
    Show answer

    Answer: Pistil

    The pistil contains the stigma, style and ovary.

  2. MCQ Which gas is released during photosynthesis?
    A. OxygenB. Carbon dioxideC. NitrogenD. Hydrogen
    Show answer

    Answer: Oxygen

    Oxygen is a by-product of splitting water during photosynthesis.

  3. Short answer Which part of the flower becomes the fruit?
    Show answer

    The ovary becomes the fruit; ovules become seeds.
